## Results

### Paired clinical cohorts validate cell-type-resolved reconstruction and disease-signal recovery

Before applying `PBISC` to unprofiled bulk-only clinical cohorts, we first tested whether adapting the pretrained `6,000`-gene (`1×`) diffusion model on a minimal five-donor paired reference reconstructs donor-specific single-cell manifolds and cell-type-resolved disease shifts from peripheral blood bulk RNA-seq (**Fig. 4a** and **Supplementary Figs. 8–13**). In bacterial sepsis¹ (`D0/D1/D3`; `4` adult sepsis and `1` healthy support donor per reference, `77` held-out donors/visits) and end-stage kidney disease (`ESKD`) COVID-19²,³ (`D0/D1/D4`; `3` COVID-19-positive and `2` COVID-19-negative `ESKD` support donors, `52` held-out donors across `142–143` visits), five paired donors adapted `PBISC` via low-rank adaptation (`LoRA`) and conditioned `4,000` generated cells per held-out bulk sample (`×1` bulk inputs with support-gated ceiling counts), benchmarked directly against observed (`Real`) scRNA-seq of the **same held-out donor** and against `scSemiProfiler`⁴ trained on the identical supports (**Fig. 4a**; boundary conditions in smaller `Giroux` and `Ji` cohorts in **Supplementary Figs. 8 and 9**). Without Harmony or post-hoc integration, `PBISC` cells co-localized directly with observed `Real` cells across all six immune compartments (`T`, `B`, `Monocyte`, `NK`, `DC` and `Other`) in `50`-dimensional **PRE-PCA** space (`iLISI = 1.51` in Sepsis and `1.44` in COVID-19 versus `Real–Real` split-half `1.77` and `1.76`), whereas `scSemiProfiler` segregated into unmixed source-specific clusters (`iLISI = 1.00`; **Fig. 4b–e,l,m**). `PBISC` accurately tracked four orders of magnitude of mean UMI counts (`r_ref = 0.974 ± 0.005` and `0.972 ± 0.004`), reproduced broad empirical per-cell library depths (`10²–10⁴` UMIs) rather than `scSemiProfiler`'s narrow unimodal count spike, and preserved donor-balanced PBMC compositions without `scSemiProfiler`'s pronounced `B`-cell depletion (**Fig. 4f–k** and **Supplementary Figs. 9 and 10**).

Because manifold alignment alone does not guarantee recovery of disease-driven transcription, we next evaluated `case − control` differential expression in source-matched held-out donor cohorts across `T`, `B`, `Monocyte` and `NK` populations using `TMM`⁵ normalization and `limma-voom`⁶,⁷ moderated linear models (`BH P < 0.05`; **Fig. 4n–r** and **Supplementary Figs. 11–13**). In COVID-19, `PBISC` substantially outperformed `scSemiProfiler` across all four lineages on paired `logFC r` (`0.68–0.82` versus `0.35–0.63`), `DEG recall` (`0.60–0.70` versus `0.06–0.14`) and `DEG F1` (`0.25–0.53` versus `0.09–0.19`). In Sepsis, where paired `logFC r` was comparable (`0.47–0.60` versus `0.50–0.52`), `scSemiProfiler` suffered severe sensitivity loss (`9–147` `D0` DEGs versus `422–980` `Real` DEGs; **Supplementary Fig. 12**), whereas `PBISC` achieved `3–12×` higher `DEG recall` (`0.24–0.44` versus `0.03–0.08`) and superior `DEG F1` across all four lineages (`0.23–0.41` versus `0.04–0.13`), faithfully capturing Sepsis inflammatory/TCR-repression and COVID-19 interferon/plasmablast programs without cross-donor block artefacts (**Fig. 4o–r** and **Supplementary Figs. 11 and 13**). Having assessed reconstruction fidelity on source-matched paired samples, we next deployed five-donor-adapted `PBISC` in its intended setting: reconstructing single-cell landscapes for specimens profiled solely by bulk RNA-seq.

### Bulk-only cohorts recover broad immune remodelling, disease programs and unpaired differential expression

To determine whether bulk-only generation recovers cohort-level cellular remodelling and cell-type transcriptional shifts without paired scRNA-seq from those specimens, we conditioned five-donor-adapted `PBISC` (`1` fixed support selection per study) on all `122` bulk-only Sepsis samples¹ (`488,000` cells) and `164` bulk-only `ESKD` COVID-19 samples²,³ (`656,000` cells), evaluating them in a specimen-level unpaired design against observed `Real` scRNA-seq from unmatched specimens and against `scSemiProfiler`⁴ (**Fig. 5a** and **Supplementary Fig. 14**). Beyond visual alignment in post-Harmony⁸ display UMAPs (**Fig. 5b–e**), `PBISC` maintained native pre-integration mixing in `Real-A`-fitted `30`-dimensional **pre-PCA** space (`iLISI = 1.48` and `1.43` versus donor-disjoint `Real B` `1.66` and `1.60`, whereas `scSemiProfiler` remained at `1.00`; **Fig. 5l**), preserved four-decade gene-expression profiles (`r = 0.980` and `0.979`) and realistic UMI-depth distributions (**Fig. 5f–i**), and tracked case–control compositional remodelling across `297` `donor × condition` units without statistically detected `T`- or `B`-cell distortion (`patient-block bootstrap q ≥ 0.05`, whereas `scSemiProfiler` significantly inflated Sepsis `T` cells and depleted `B` cells; **Fig. 5j,k,m** and **Supplementary Fig. 15a**).

We then tested whether unpaired bulk-only `PBISC` profiles recapitulate published cell-type disease programs and per-gene pseudobulk DEGs (**Fig. 5n–t** and **Supplementary Figs. 15b–17**). Across cohort-reported transcriptional signatures, `PBISC` significantly (`P < 0.05` on the published side) reproduced **`6` of `7`** Sepsis shifts¹ (all except monocyte `S100A/RETN`, `+0.07` [`ns`]) and **`7` of `7`** COVID-19 shifts³ with well-calibrated fold changes matching `Real B`, avoiding `scSemiProfiler`'s two- to threefold inflation of type-I interferon amplitudes (**Fig. 5n,o**). In genome-panel unpaired Welch pseudobulk tests (using the fixed `60,000`-cell `PBISC` evaluation subset per cohort; `5,664` Sepsis and `5,610` COVID-19 genes; `BH q < 0.05, |log2 FC| ≥ 1`; **Fig. 5p–t**), `PBISC` outperformed `scSemiProfiler` across all four lineages on Sepsis `logFC r` (`0.52–0.73` versus `0.15–0.21`), Sepsis `DEG recall` (`0.37–0.73` versus `0.00–0.09`), and threshold-free `Real`-DEG ranking `AUROC` in both Sepsis (`0.78–0.96` versus `0.56–0.65`) and COVID-19 (`0.85–0.99` versus `0.68–0.92`, where `scSemiProfiler` recovered `0` overlapping `B`-cell DEGs; split-half reliability disattenuation in **Supplementary Figs. 16 and 17**). Because bulk averaging and broad-lineage summaries often obscure low-abundance pathogenic subsets, we next asked whether these bulk-only `PBISC` cohorts preserve rare immune states and their distinct molecular profiles.

### Assigned rare populations retain abundance and inter-patient molecular specificity

To assess whether reference-assigned rare populations (`<0.05%` to `2%` of total PBMCs) retain their abundance and molecular profiles, we audited exact integer parent-lineage shares across `14` prespecified Sepsis rare states and `13` COVID-19 rare states in pooled bulk-only cases (`272,000` Sepsis and `560,000` primary-scope COVID-19 `PBISC` cells; **Fig. 6a,b** and **Supplementary Fig. 18**). `PBISC` populated **`13` of `14`** Sepsis rare states and **`12` of `13`** COVID-19 rare states—spanning `B · MZB1` plasmablasts, `ASC IgG`, `Cycling ASC`, mitotic `T`/`B`/`NK`/`Monocyte` subsets, `Macrophage`, dexamethasone-response `CD14 Mono · dex`, `pDC`, `DC1`, `ASDC` and circulating `HSC`/`HSPC` (lacking only ultra-sparse `ILC`s at `<0.8%` of `NK + ILC`)—whereas `scSemiProfiler` collapsed to **true zero counts in `7` of `14` Sepsis and `8` of `13` COVID-19 states**. Within post-Harmony display UMAPs, generated rare cells localized directly onto observed micro-clusters (**Fig. 6c,d**).

To verify that reference-assigned rare subsets capture state-specific transcriptional identity beyond generic parent-lineage background, we subtracted a state-balanced `Real-A+B` baseline across `2,000` HVGs and correlated `270` Sepsis and `625` COVID-19 sample-by-state profiles strictly against same-state `Real A+B` profiles from **other patients** (**Fig. 6e,f**). `PBISC` closely matched or exceeded the `Real` inter-patient benchmark in Sepsis `B · MZB1` (`median r = 0.84` versus `0.85`), `Macrophage` (`0.58` versus `0.67`) and `CD4 T · MKI67` (`0.50` versus `0.58`, with single-cell-sparse `Mono · MKI67` at `0.17`), as well as COVID-19 `ASC IgG` (`0.77` versus `0.73`), `pDC` (`0.69` versus `0.68`), `HSPC` (`0.60` versus `0.60`) and `CD14 Mono · dex` (`0.65` versus `0.81`), whereas `scSemiProfiler` yielded `0` eligible profiles across five of these eight focal states; marker heatmaps confirmed coherent co-expression of canonical plasmablast, complement-macrophage, steroid-response, `pDC`, `HSPC` and mitotic modules (**Fig. 6g,h** and **Supplementary Fig. 19**). Motivated by this fidelity across both abundant and rare compartments, we next examined whether rescuing unprofiled bulk-only patients unlocks statistical power to resolve longitudinal clinical severity and early mortality risk in `ESKD` COVID-19.

### Rescuing bulk-only ESKD patients separates dynamic COVID-19 severity from baseline mortality risk

In the `ESKD` haemodialysis COVID-19 study²,³, scRNA-seq profiled `37` non-`CLL` acute patients (`138` draws, `5` deaths; excluding incidental `CLL` cases `C116` and `C139`, **Supplementary Fig. 20**), leaving `29` Wave-1 patients (`79` draws, `6` deaths) profiled solely by bulk RNA-seq. Incorporating `PBISC`-inferred single-cell profiles expanded the cohort by `+78%` to **`66` patients (`217` source-priority acute draws)** and fatal events by `+120%` (`5 → 11` deaths; **Fig. 7a,b**). Because `41.9%` (`91/217`) of draws occurred below eventual peak illness whereas `80.3%` (`53/66`) of first draws (`10/11` deaths) were collected `≤ 7 d` post-swab (**Supplementary Fig. 25**), we rank-normalized `43` PBMC subsets to within-source Van der Waerden normal scores to separately evaluate (i) **concurrent draw-day `WHO` severity** across all `217` visits (`patient-clustered GEE` adjusted for swab weeks) and (ii) **baseline survival discrimination** at each patient's **first acute blood draw** (`66` patients: `55` survived, `11` died; **Fig. 7a–d**).

Combining `Measured` and `PBISC` doubled detected concurrent severity associations from `9/43` (`Measured` alone; `7` shared) to **`18/43` subsets** (`BH q43 < 0.05`), marked by expansion of `Dividing ASC` (`β = +0.422, q43 = 1.8 × 10⁻⁸`), `HSPC` (`β = +0.333, q43 = 0.00013`, rescued from non-significance in `Measured` alone) and `CD16 mono C1` (`β = +0.247, q43 = 0.004`), alongside progressive depletion of `γδ T` (`β = −0.196, q43 = 0.003`), `T CD8 CM` (`β = −0.231, q43 = 0.003`), `MAIT` and `CD56-bright NK` (**Fig. 7c,e–g2** and **Supplementary Fig. 21**). Asking next whether these dynamic severity correlates—or distinct baseline host deficits—predict death at initial presentation (**Fig. 7d,h–j**), we found that first-draw `HSPC` fractions had not yet diverged (`AUC = 0.58`; intracellular `HSPC Ery5` expression reached nominal `AUC = 0.752, P = 0.0094, q16 = 0.150` only in a separate all-visit patient-mean analysis, **Supplementary Figs. 23 and 24**). Instead, an exploratory `q43 < 0.10` screen (`Measured`-only `q43 = 0.34`; neither `< 0.05`) identified baseline depletion of cytotoxic **`CD8 CTL`** (`Combined AUC = 0.213, q43 = 0.070, WHO-adj. P = 0.033`) and **`NK`** (`AUC = 0.217, q43 = 0.070, WHO-adj. P = 0.030`), paired with elevated cell-intrinsic `CD8 CTL ISG` expression (`AUC = 0.685, P = 0.056` across `n = 63` first draws with `≥ 5` `CD8 CTL`s). Combining these into the post-hoc **`ISG – CTL – NK` score** yielded `Combined AUC = 0.841` (`P = 4.3 × 10⁻⁴, WHO-adj. P = 0.013`; `Measured 0.887`, `PBISC 0.803`) and improved fixed-feature `50 × 5-fold` internal `CV AUC` from `0.694` (`Age + Sex + draw-day WHO`) to **`0.846`** (`0.858` for `+ CTL + ISG`; **Fig. 7d,j**).

Finally, to distinguish universal COVID-19 immune trajectories from uraemic `ESKD` context, we conducted DerSimonian–Laird random-effects meta-analyses across four independent public PBMC scRNA-seq cohorts (`Stephenson et al., 2021`⁹, `COMBAT, 2022`¹⁰, `van der Wijst et al., 2021`¹¹ and `Liu et al., 2021`¹²; **`300` acute draws in `231` patients**; `219` outcome patients, `24` deaths; `BH q23` across `23` subsets mapped in `k ≥ 2` cohorts; **Fig. 7k,l** and **Supplementary Fig. 22**; the archived `276`-draw sensitivity comparison is shown separately in **Supplementary Fig. 27**). External meta-analysis replicated **all six** multi-cohort focal severity axes in the exact `ESKD` direction (`Dividing ASC*` `Ext. ρ = +0.26`, `HSPC` `+0.40`, `MAIT` `−0.29`, `CD56-bright NK` `−0.28`, `γδ T` `−0.27`, `T CD8 CM*` `−0.29`; all `Ext. q23 ≤ 0.016`; **Fig. 7k**). Conversely, juxtaposing first- versus last-draw mortality (**Fig. 7l**) showed that baseline `CD8 CTL*` (`pooled First AUC = 0.41, q23 = 0.343`) and `NK` (`0.48, q23 = 0.834`) depletion did not generalize to non-dialysis cohorts; instead, non-dialysis mortality featured early **`MAIT`** (`First AUC = 0.21, q23 = 0.0024`) and **`CD8 naïve T`** (`0.32, q23 = 0.0076`) loss. At the last draw, lower `MAIT`, `CD8 naïve T`, `DC2` and `γδ T` fractions met `q23 < 0.05`. External late **`CD16 mono`** depletion retained its direction but did not meet the adjusted threshold (`Last AUC = 0.394 [95% CI 0.280–0.507], P = 0.066, q23 = 0.217`; `ESKD Last = 0.269`). These differences are compatible with differing baseline immune composition in uraemic `ESKD`¹³,¹⁴ (`CD8 naïve T` first-draw `AUC = 0.488`), but do not establish an ESKD-specific mechanism.

## Discussion

Clinical studies of acute infection routinely archive bulk RNA-seq across serial hospital visits while restricting scRNA-seq to smaller subsets. Our analyses followed a four-step logical progression to bridge this gap. First, to establish whether minimal paired supervision suffices to translate bulk profiles into single cells, we benchmarked five-donor `LoRA` adaptation on paired sepsis¹ and `ESKD` COVID-19²,³ donors, showing that generative diffusion (`PBISC`) reconstructs unintegrated cellular manifolds and achieves `3–12×` higher cell-type DEG recall than `scSemiProfiler`⁴ under the same five-donor supports (**Fig. 4**). Second, deploying this validated framework across `286` unpaired bulk-only specimens (`1.144 million` cells) recovered broad compositional shifts, `13` of `14` published disease modules, and high threshold-free DEG ranking (`AUROC = 0.78–0.99`; **Fig. 5**). Third, because bulk averaging masks low-frequency pathogenic states, we interrogated rare populations (`<0.05–2%` of PBMCs) and confirmed that `PBISC` preserves `13/14` sepsis and `12/13` COVID-19 rare states with cross-patient molecular similarity, whereas `scSemiProfiler` collapsed more than half to zero (**Fig. 6**). Fourth, leveraging this fidelity to rescue `29` unprofiled bulk-only `ESKD` COVID-19 patients (`37 → 66` patients, `5 → 11` deaths; **Fig. 7**) enabled separate modelling of dynamic severity trajectories and baseline mortality risk.

Separating longitudinal sampling from initial presentation—and testing generalizability across four external COVID-19 cohorts⁹⁻¹² (`300` draws in `231` patients)—distinguished shared progression programs from exploratory baseline associations. Expansion of proliferating plasmablasts and circulating `HSPC`s¹⁵,¹⁶ alongside innate-like/`CD8 CM` T-cell attrition tracked concurrent `WHO` severity across both `ESKD` and external cohorts (`Ext. q23 ≤ 0.016`), whereas `HSPC` fractions had not yet diverged at initial presentation. Conversely, fatal `ESKD` cases exhibited exploratory first-draw `CD8 CTL` and `NK` depletion coupled with elevated `CD8 CTL ISG` expression¹⁷⁻¹⁹ (`ISG – CTL – NK` `AUC = 0.841`), whereas non-dialysis cohorts showed early `CD8 naïve T` loss—a divergence consistent with chronic uraemic thymic involution and pre-existing naïve T-cell contraction in haemodialysis patients¹³,¹⁴ while external late `CD16` monocyte depletion remained a same-direction, non-significant trend (`AUC = 0.394, q23 = 0.217`).

Several boundaries frame interpretation. Paired reference triplets measure five-donor support sensitivity rather than independent biological replication (**Fig. 4**); bulk-only comparisons are specimen-unpaired, with seven shared longitudinal COVID-19 donors and cohort case-mix differences such as attenuated sepsis monocyte `S100A/RETN` shifts (**Fig. 5**); rare-state counts rely on frozen reference-transfer labels (**Fig. 6**); and the `ESKD` first-draw mortality screen rests on `11` deaths (`CD8 CTL` and `NK` `q43 = 0.070`, nominal residual `WHO-adj. P`, fixed-feature internal CV, and non-replication in non-dialysis cohorts; **Fig. 7**), warranting prospective validation in dialysis cohorts with matched cytometry.

### Conclusion

Adapting the pretrained `PBISC` diffusion model on only five paired donors per cohort reconstructs donor-specific single-cell manifolds, cell-type differential expression, and rare immune states directly from bulk RNA-seq. Applying this validated framework to unprofiled bulk specimens expands underpowered clinical cohorts to separate externally replicable disease-severity trajectories from exploratory baseline immune associations. `PBISC` thus offers a scalable, practical bridge to unlock single-cell biological and clinical discovery across archived bulk transcriptomic cohorts.

## Methods

### Paired-cohort adaptation, unintegrated PRE-PCA evaluation and paired `limma-voom` disease testing (Figure 4)
In the paired-cohort benchmark (**Fig. 4** and **Supplementary Figs. 8–13**), donors profiled by both bulk RNA-seq and PBMC scRNA-seq in bacterial sepsis (Ye et al.¹; `GSE279448` bulk RNA-seq and `GSE279452` scRNA-seq) and Imperial College London ESKD COVID-19 (Gisby et al.²; Stephenson et al.³; `Zenodo 6497251` bulk RNA-seq and matched PBMC scRNA-seq) were partitioned into five paired support donors for low-rank adaptation (`LoRA`) of the pretrained `6,000`-gene (`1×`) `PBISC` model and held-out test donors whose bulk RNA-seq conditioned `4,000` generated single cells per sample (`×1` bulk inputs with support-gated ceiling-count readout). Three fixed five-donor support selections were evaluated without performance-based cherry-picking (**Supplementary Fig. 8a,b,e**): **Sepsis `D0`, `D1` and `D3`** (`4` adult sepsis + `1` healthy control support donor [`HC07` adult HC in `D0`, `HC12` adult HC in `D1`, `PHC05` paediatric HC in `D3`], excluding `D2` [`PIC01`] and `D4` [`IC23`] whose control supports were ICU patients; `77` held-out donors [`77` visits] per reference) and **COVID-19 `D0`, `D1` and `D4`** (`3` COVID-19-positive + `2` COVID-19-negative ESKD support donors [`C106, C237` in `D0`; `C142, C164` in `D1`; `C152, C137` in `D4`]; `52` held-out donors [`142–143` visits] per reference). `scSemiProfiler`⁴ was run on the identical five support donors and held-out bulk samples in every reference selection, retaining its recorded readout. Support donors and all of their other visits were strictly excluded from held-out evaluation.

Display UMAPs (**Fig. 4b–e**) were computed on the first fixed reference (`D0`: `11,550` cells per source across `77` Sepsis visits; `21,250` cells per source across `142` COVID-19 visits) directly from the saved `50`-dimensional three-source **PRE-PCA** representation (`15` nearest neighbours, `min_dist = 0.5`, `random_state = 0`) with **no Harmony or batch integration**, plotting observed reference labels for `Real` cells and frozen predicted six-type labels (`T`, `B`, `Monocyte`, `NK`, `DC`, `Other`) for generated cells in one fixed random point order. Donor-balanced gene-expression profiles (**Fig. 4f,h**; `5,975` Sepsis and `5,927` COVID-19 shared genes, retaining zeros) averaged expression across the three fixed references, reporting mean ± sample s.d. of reference-specific Pearson correlations (`r_ref`) on unsmoothed `log1p` gene means alongside `101`-rank `log1p`-mean display curves; donor-balanced UMI-depth densities (**Fig. 4g,i**) and six-type compositions (**Fig. 4j,k**) were likewise averaged across the three fixed references. Matched source `iLISI` (**Fig. 4l,m**) was computed in each reference selection's `50`-dimensional three-source **PRE-PCA** space by splitting each retained visit's observed cells into two disjoint equal halves (`≤ 75` cells per side, excluding an unmatched odd cell) and comparing the first observed half against either the second observed half (`Real–Real` empirical split-cell reference) or an identically sized generated subset across three query seeds (reporting 3-reference means ± sample s.d. of the three seed medians).

Cell-type-resolved disease contrasts in source-matched cohorts (**Fig. 4n–r** and **Supplementary Figs. 11–13**; “paired” denotes the Real/generated source correspondence, not a within-person case–control contrast) compared adult/paediatric sepsis cases against healthy controls (excluding non-septic ICU) and COVID-19-positive against COVID-19-negative ESKD samples (excluding recovery) across identical cell-type-specific visit eligibility masks and observed-defined gene families (`D0` case/control donor–condition counts in **Supplementary Fig. 12**: `57/10`, `52/10`, `42/9` and `31/9` in Sepsis `T`, `B`, `Monocyte` and `NK`; `32/31`, `32/30`, `32/31` and `32/30` in COVID-19). Within each reference selection and source (`Real`, `PBISC`, `scSemiProfiler`), donor–condition pseudobulks were normalized by `TMM`⁵ and tested with `limma-voom`⁶,⁷ moderated linear models accounting for repeated donor–condition observations, calling differentially expressed genes (`DEGs`) at within-source Benjamini–Hochberg (`BH`) adjusted `P < 0.05` without an additional fold-change cutoff. Against observed `Real` effects of the **same held-out donors** in each reference selection, we computed **paired `logFC r`** (Pearson correlation of native `limma-voom` `log2` fold changes across genes), **`DEG recall`** (`|DEG_gen ∩ DEG_real| / |DEG_real|`), **`DEG precision`** (`|DEG_gen ∩ DEG_real| / |DEG_gen|`, left undefined when `|DEG_gen| = 0`; **Supplementary Fig. 9i**) and **`DEG F1`** (`2 × precision × recall / (precision + recall)`). Heatmaps (**Fig. 4o–r** and **Supplementary Fig. 11**) displayed `12` `Real`-selected disease-program genes per cell type as `ln(1 + CP10K)` standardized by `Real`-only, equally reference-weighted means and population s.d. (clipped at `±2.5`). Descriptive native disease effects in **Supplementary Fig. 13** were defined per reference as `log2(1 + mean donor-condition CP10K in cases) − log2(1 + mean donor-condition CP10K in controls)`, averaged equally across the three fixed selections over the full observed gene intersection (`2,514–4,167` genes), and evaluated by Pearson `r` and Lin's concordance correlation (`CCC`).

### Bulk-only cohort generation, reference annotation and pre-integration manifold evaluation (Figure 5)
For bulk-only deployment in the same two clinical studies (**Fig. 5** and **Supplementary Fig. 14**), the pretrained `6,000`-gene (`1×`) `PBISC` model was adapted via `LoRA` on one fixed selection of five paired support donors per cohort (`disease:control = 3:2` in sepsis¹ and `1:4` in ESKD COVID-19²,³), holding all remaining samples out of adaptation. Conditioning the adapted models on `×1` bulk RNA-seq inputs (`GSE279448` in sepsis; Gisby et al.² `Zenodo 6497251` bulk RNA-seq in ESKD COVID-19) with support-gated ceiling-count readout generated `4,000` single cells per bulk-only specimen across `122` sepsis bulk samples (`488,000` total `PBISC` cells: `121` adult samples [`68` sepsis, `17` non-septic ICU, `36` healthy controls] plus `1` paediatric ICU sample `PIC06`) and `164` COVID-19 bulk samples (`656,000` total `PBISC` cells: `143` COVID-19-positive, `19` COVID-19-negative and `2` recovery samples). `scSemiProfiler`⁴ was conditioned on the same five support donors per cohort and evaluated using its archived Poisson-count representation (`51,000` sepsis cells across `34` samples; `60,000` COVID-19 cells across `102` samples). Complete observed (`Real`) scRNA-seq cohorts comprised `633,072` adult sepsis cells across `108` samples (`37,474` raw features; `5,967` panel genes in `Real`, `5,975` measured in bulk and `5,961` shared with `scSemiProfiler`) and `578,835` COVID-19 cells across `187` samples (`33,559` raw features; `5,791` panel genes in `Real`, `5,927` measured in bulk and `5,745` shared with `scSemiProfiler`; **Supplementary Fig. 14a–d**).

Fixed donor-disjoint evaluation subsamples of the observed scRNA-seq cohorts were partitioned into `Real A` (`58,195` sepsis cells; `57,613` COVID-19 cells) and `Real B` (`59,457` sepsis cells; `56,171` COVID-19 cells). Frozen six-type broad labels (`T`, `B`, `Monocyte`, `NK`, `DC`, `Other`) and fine-grained cell-state labels were transferred from the `Real A` reference package (`30`-nearest-neighbour majority vote in `30`-dimensional Harmony⁸ space across `2,000` `Real-A` HVGs). Post-Harmony UMAPs of `Real A` and the `60,000`-cell `PBISC` display subset (**Fig. 5b–e**) were used strictly for display. Native source mixing (**Fig. 5l**) was quantified without Harmony by projecting `Real B` (`59,457 / 56,171` cells), `PBISC` (`60,000 / 60,000` cells) and `scSemiProfiler` (`51,000 / 60,000` cells) directly into the `Real-A`-fitted `30`-dimensional **pre-PCA** space (`2,000` `Real-A` HVGs) and computing `iLISI`⁸ against `Real A` across three fixed query seeds. Gene-expression rank profiles (**Fig. 5f,h**) computed arithmetic mean raw UMI counts per cell across `5,961` sepsis and `5,745` COVID-19 shared genes (retaining zero means, `101`-rank `log1p`-mean display smoothing, and unsmoothed `log1p` Pearson `r`; note that sepsis `scSemiProfiler` yields `r = 0.9365` [`0.937` printed on **Fig. 5f**] when the full `51,000`-cell generated-source means are correlated with the archived `Real` means over the same shared genes, and `0.9363` [`0.936`] when both means are taken from the archived display table). Source-native RNA-depth densities (**Fig. 5g,i**) used a Gaussian kernel (bandwidth `0.06`) on `log10(1 + UMI)`.

### Donor-level cell-type composition, published disease signals and unpaired pseudobulk DEGs (Figure 5)
Six-type PBMC proportions summing to `100%` were computed per visit, averaged within each `donor × condition` unit, and weighted equally across donors (**Fig. 5j,k,m** and **Supplementary Fig. 15a**), yielding `297` `donor × condition` units across `Real B`, `PBISC` and `scSemiProfiler` (`case/control` counts: `14/4`, `68/36` and `26/5` in sepsis [`153` units, zero shared donors across `Real B` and `PBISC`]; `12/12`, `57/19` and `22/22` in COVID-19 [`144` units, where longitudinal donors could contribute to both negative and positive conditions and seven donors—`C113`, `C127`, `C145`, `C168`, `C169`, `C20` and `C63`—contributed unmatched visits to both `Real B` and `PBISC`]). Mean differences versus `Real B` were tested by two-sided centered patient-block bootstrap resampling (`49,999` iterations; `BH q` across `24` tests per cohort).

Published disease transcriptional signals (**Fig. 5n,o**) were scored on `donor × condition` pseudobulks (`≥ 10` cells of the target type; arithmetic mean of `log2(CPM + 1)` across shared-panel program genes) for seven `Ye et al.`¹ sepsis programs (`2–8` named genes per row) and seven `Stephenson et al.`³ COVID-19 programs (MSigDB `v2026.1` Reactome `Interferon alpha/beta signaling` [`34/78` genes] and `Cell cycle` [`160/688` genes] plus named monocyte `MHC class II` and `S100A / MCEMP1 / CD163 / PLAC8` modules), tested via two-sided Welch's *t*-tests of `case − control`.

Unpaired per-gene pseudobulk differential expression (**Fig. 5p–t** and **Supplementary Figs. 16 and 17**) used a fixed `60,000`-cell `PBISC` evaluation subset per cohort (stratified sample, seed `20261024`, with frozen `six_tr` labels), together with the archived `Real` and full `scSemiProfiler` inputs. The same pseudobulks were used for the split-half reliability analysis. This evaluation subset is distinct from the full generated populations used for the composition and rare-population analyses. Differential expression was evaluated across `5,664` sepsis and `5,610` COVID-19 shared panel genes on visit-level raw counts summed per cell type (`≥ 20` cells per visit and cell type required), normalized to `log2(CPM + 1)` (`CPM` computed over total stored genes in `Real` and shared-panel genes in generated sources), and averaged within `patient × condition` for repeated COVID-19 visits (excluding five predefined outlier/unmatched visits: `C126_positive_5`, `C126_positive_9`, `C126_positive_11`, `C141_negative`, `C58_positive_2`). Two-sided Welch's *t*-tests compared case and control units (`DEGs` defined by **`BH q < 0.05` and `|log2 FC| ≥ 1`**; crossover COVID-19 donors appearing in both conditions were not modelled as paired). Archived `Real` volcano tables retain genome-wide `BH` adjustment (`max |Δq| ≈ 0.00345` versus shared-panel `BH`, with `0` changes in binary `Real` DEG calls across all eight cohort × cell-type tests), whereas generated sources apply `BH` across finite shared-panel tests. Generated sources were benchmarked against the archived full observed `Real` pseudobulk reference (`93/7`, `93/7`, `86/7`, `93/7` in sepsis and `39/38`, `37/34`, `37/36`, `39/37` in COVID-19 `T`, `B`, `Monocyte`, `NK`, distinct from thinned `Real B`) via unattenuated **unpaired `logFC r`** (**Fig. 5r**), **`DEG recall`** (**Fig. 5s**), threshold-free **`Real`-DEG ranking `AUROC`** ranking `Real` binary DEG labels by generated `|log2 FC|` (**Fig. 5t**), and donor split-half reliability disattenuation (`50` splits for split-half AUROC and `200` stratified donor splits for reliability-disattenuated `r` and slopes; **Supplementary Figs. 16a,b and 17a,b**).

### ESKD COVID-19 longitudinal severity, first-draw mortality and composite cross-validation (Figure 7)
In the Imperial College London ESKD haemodialysis cohort²,³, excluding two chronic lymphocytic leukaemia patients (`C116`, `C139`; **Supplementary Fig. 20a–c**) under a strict source-priority design without duplicate patient entry yielded **`66` acute COVID-19 patients** (`55` survived, `11` died) and **`217` acute blood draws** with documented `days_from_first_positive_swab` and concurrent draw-day WHO ordinal severity (`0 = Mild [126]`, `1 = Moderate [58]`, `2 = Severe [21]`, `3 = Critical [12]`): **`37` `Measured` patients** (`10` `scRNA-seq-only` patients [`47` draws, `1` death] plus `27` patients shared with bulk RNA-seq represented by their `91` measured scRNA-seq draws [`4` deaths]; `138` total measured draws) and **`29` Wave-1 bulk-only patients inferred via `PBISC`** (`79` draws, `6` deaths; adding the `59` non-overlapping `PBISC` draws from the `27` shared patients forms the `276`-draw supplementary sensitivity set in **Supplementary Figs. 25–27**). Across all `43` fine-grained PBMC subsets (`≥ 30%` detection in `Measured`), sample percentages (`% of PBMCs`) were transformed **within each source** (`Measured` `138` and `PBISC` `79` draws separately for longitudinal models; `Measured` `37` and `PBISC` `29` first draws separately for baseline mortality models) to **Van der Waerden** rank-based inverse normal scores (`rank-INT`: `Φ⁻¹((rank − 0.5) / N)`, average ties) to harmonize within-source marginal ranks (**Supplementary Fig. 20i**).

1. **Concurrent draw-day WHO severity (`217` visits, `66` patients; Fig. 7c,e–g2)**: Within-source normal scores `z_ij` were regressed on numeric concurrent draw-day WHO grade (`0, 1, 2, 3`) adjusting for `days_from_first_positive_swab / 7` via Gaussian `GEE` (patient-clustered exchangeable working correlation and Huber–White sandwich variance; `statsmodels`²⁰), with `BH q43 < 0.05` across `43` subsets.
2. **Baseline first-draw mortality (`66` patients: `55` survived, `11` died; Fig. 7d,h–j)**: Evaluated at **each patient's earliest eligible acute blood draw** (`37` `Measured` [`32` survived, `5` died] + `29` `PBISC` [`23` survived, `6` died]; `53/66` patients and `10/11` decedents sampled `≤ 7 d` post-swab; **Supplementary Fig. 25d**) via two-sided Mann–Whitney *U* `AUC` for death (`< 0.5` lower in fatal, `> 0.5` higher in fatal), `3,000`-resample stratified percentile bootstrap 95% confidence intervals, and `BH q43` (exploratory `q43 < 0.10` screen). Nominal `WHO-adjusted P` values for single fractions (`CD8 CTL`: `0.033`; `NK`: `0.030`) were obtained by two-sided Mann–Whitney tests on ordinary least-squares (`OLS`) feature residuals after regressing on numeric first-draw WHO grade (plus a source indicator in `Combined`).
3. **Single-cell `CD8 CTL ISG`, `ISG – CTL – NK` post-hoc composite and fixed-feature `50 × 5-fold` internal cross-validation (Fig. 7d,j)**: Across the `n = 63` exact first draws containing `≥ 5` `CD8 CTL` cells (`52` survived [`30` `Measured`, `22` `PBISC`] and `11` died [`5` `Measured`, `6` `PBISC`], excluding surviving patients `C113`, `C129` and `C140`), single-cell `log1p(CP10K)` expression was averaged across `19` type-I ISGs (`ISG15`, `IFI6`, `IFI44L`, `IFIT1`, `IFIT2`, `IFIT3`, `MX1`, `MX2`, `OAS1`, `OAS2`, `OAS3`, `IRF7`, `STAT1`, `STAT2`, `LY6E`, `XAF1`, `BST2`, `RSAD2`, `USP18`) and rank-normalized within source (`isg_zfd`). The unweighted post-hoc **`ISG – CTL – NK` score** was defined as `(isg_zfd − ctl_zfd − nk_zfd) / 3`, with its archived nominal `WHO-adj. P = 0.013` computed by two-sided Mann–Whitney test on OLS residuals after regressing the composite on categorical first-draw WHO grade (without source indicator). Fixed-feature internal discrimination was evaluated across `50` repeats of `5-fold` patient-stratified `L2` logistic cross-validation (`C = 1.0`), performing source-specific `rank-INT` and standardization **strictly inside each training fold** (`Clinical [Age + Sex + first-draw WHO] = 0.694` versus `+ CD8 CTL + NK + ISG = 0.846` and `+ CD8 CTL + ISG = 0.858` in `n = 63`; `0.688` versus `0.827` for `+ CD8 CTL + NK` in `N = 66`); because candidate features were selected prior to CV, this constitutes fixed-feature exploratory internal validation rather than nested discovery CV.

### External four-cohort COVID-19 validation and random-effects meta-analysis (Figure 7k,l)
Four independent public adult acute COVID-19 PBMC scRNA-seq cohorts—`Stephenson et al., 2021`⁹ (`89` acute visits in `85` patients; `73` with survival outcomes, `7` deaths, excluding malignant B-cell donor `CV0198`), `COMBAT, 2022`¹⁰ (`89` visits in `75` patients; `75` with outcomes, `7` deaths), `van der Wijst et al., 2021`¹¹ (`75` visits in `42` patients; `42` with outcomes, `7` deaths) and `Liu et al., 2021`¹² (`47` visits in `29` patients; `29` with outcomes, `3` deaths)—were filtered to acute-phase PBMC visits (`≤ 28 d` [`≤ 14 d` in `van der Wijst`]) with **`≥ 500` PBMCs per visit** after removing erythrocytes, platelets and malignant B cells (**`300` acute draws in `231` patients**; **`219` outcome-documented patients [`195` survivors, `24` deaths]**). Draw-day severity (**Fig. 7k**) used equal-patient-weight (`w_ij = 1 / n_i`) weighted-midrank Spearman `ρ` on each study's native ordinal scale (`SE = √(1.06 / (n − 3))` on Fisher `z = arctanh(ρ)`), compared alongside ESKD's equal-patient-weight `ESKD ρ` (`217` draws in `66` patients). Clinical outcomes were adjudicated by donor identifier against the original clinical records. For `van der Wijst`, the authors’ `Data file S3`, worksheet `ClinicalCharacteristics`, fields `Donor_ID` and `Died`, establish `7` deaths among `42` patients; that final outcome is applied consistently to every visit from a donor. The documented-outcome analysis includes `Stephenson` London patient `AP3`, recorded as deceased in the authors’ Supplementary Table 2. The historical peak-grade sensitivity in **Supplementary Fig. 22** has different patient eligibility and `23` external deaths (`6 + 7 + 7 + 3`), whereas the primary first/last analysis has `24`. First- versus last-draw mortality (**Fig. 7l**) used two-sided Mann–Whitney `AUC` for death (Hanley–McNeil SE floored at `0.035`). Across all **`23` cell populations** mapped in `k ≥ 2` external cohorts, DerSimonian–Laird random-effects meta-analysis synthesized Fisher `z` (for severity `ρ`) and `AUC − 0.5` (for mortality `AUC`), adjusting two-sided meta-analytic *P* values via `BH q23` (`†` marks `q23 < 0.05`; frozen public-atlas proxy mappings `*` as defined in the **Fig. 7** legend).

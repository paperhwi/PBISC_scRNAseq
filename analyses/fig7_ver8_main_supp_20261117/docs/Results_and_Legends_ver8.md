## 1. Statistical Rationale: Why Within-Source Normal Scores Are Retained on the Y-Axis (vs. Raw `% of PBMCs`)

A direct paired comparison of the **27 COVID-19-positive donors profiled simultaneously by both scRNA-seq (`Real` CITE-seq) and bulk-to-single-cell reconstruction (`PBISC`)** (`Supplementary Fig. 1i`; `data/modality_scale_shared27_and_raw_pct_v8.csv`) demonstrates why pooling raw `% of PBMCs` across platforms is statistically biased and why **within-source rank-based inverse normal transformation (`Normal score`)** is required:

1. **Systematic Cross-Platform Location & Scale Shifts on Identical Blood Donors**:
   - Although `PBISC` preserves relative inter-patient ordering, bulk RNA-seq measures cellular mRNA mass whereas droplet CITE-seq counts captured cells equally regardless of transcript content or Ficoll/dissociation fragility.
   - Consequently, across the **exact same 27 donors**, paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests reveal highly significant modality baseline offsets in 6 of 9 key subsets:
     - **`B ASC IgA`**: `Real` median `0.49%` vs. `PBISC` median `4.61%` (**9.4-fold** higher in bulk-derived `PBISC` due to massive `IGHA1/IGHA2/JCHAIN` mRNA content per plasmablast; paired Wilcoxon $P = 7.5 \times 10^{-8}$).
     - **`B ASC dividing`**: `Real` `0.28%` vs. `PBISC` `1.30%` (**4.6-fold**; $P = 9.6 \times 10^{-5}$).
     - **`HSPC`**: `Real` `0.13%` vs. `PBISC` `0.48%` (**3.6-fold**; $P = 3.0 \times 10^{-4}$).
     - **`CD16 mono`**: `Real` `0.75%` vs. `PBISC` `2.10%` (**2.8-fold**; $P = 0.0042$).
     - **`T CD8 CTL`**: `Real` `8.78%` vs. `PBISC` `12.37%` (**1.4-fold**; $P = 0.0070$).
     - **`NK CD56bright`**: `Real` `0.97%` vs. `PBISC` `0.33%` (**0.34-fold**; $P = 0.0065$).
2. **Prevention of Rank-Mixing Attenuation When Pooling `Real (37)` and `PBISC new (29)`**:
   - Within a single source (`Real` alone or `PBISC new` alone), Spearman $\rho$ and Mann–Whitney $\mathrm{AUC}$ are strictly rank-invariant, so raw `% of PBMCs` and `Normal score` yield **identical** statistics.
   - However, when pooling `Real (37)` and `PBISC new (29)` (`n = 66`), naive rank-pooling of raw `% of PBMCs` ranks platform scale differences alongside disease severity. For example, in `B ASC IgA`, both cohorts individually show $\rho = +0.38$ (`Real`) and $\rho = +0.39$ (`PBISC new`), yet naive pooling of raw `%` places every `PBISC new` donor (`1.3–21.9%`; mild `1.3–8.1%`) at or above most critical `Real` donors (`0.4–4.5%`), artificially attenuating pooled $\rho$ to $+0.31$. Similarly, `NK CD56bright` ($\rho = -0.55$ in `Real`, $-0.45$ in `PBISC new`) is attenuated to $-0.38$ under naive raw-`%` pooling, whereas within-source `Normal score` yields the coherent pooled $\rho = -0.50$ ($P = 1.6 \times 10^{-5}$).
3. **Complete Transparency via Companion Raw `% of PBMCs` Panels (`Supplementary Fig. 1j–o`)**:
   - Plotting within-source `Normal score` in **Main Fig. 7f–k** aligns the plotted coordinates one-to-one with the pooled non-parametric test and places all six panels on a uniform SD scale without extreme right-skew compression.
   - Simultaneously, **Supplementary Fig. 1j–o** presents the exact patient-level **raw `% of PBMCs`** distributions for all six associations (`B ASC IgG`, `B ASC IgA`, `T CD8 CTL` severity; `T CD8 CM`, `T CD8 CTL`, `HSPC` death), confirming that every association remains statistically significant even when pooling unadjusted raw percentages ($P = 7.6 \times 10^{-4}$ to $0.024$).

## 2. Manuscript Results Draft (English)

### Pre-specified identification and validation of PBISC-rescued immune associations in ESKD COVID-19

Before testing clinical associations in the end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) COVID-19 cohort, we audited all 105 bulk PBMC RNA-seq donors (including all 68 in-scope COVID-19-positive patients) for coexisting chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL), which Stephenson et al. excluded from their published compositional analyses due to extreme B- and NK-compartment distortion (**Fig. 7a** and **Supplementary Fig. 1a**). Canonical CLL markers (`ROR1` and `FMOD`) cleanly separated the two documented benign-CLL patients (`C116` and `C139`; $\log_2(\mathrm{CPM}+1) > 4.2$ for `ROR1` and $> 3.4$ for `FMOD`) from all remaining donors and confirmed zero occult CLL cases among either the 37 non-CLL `Real` scRNA-seq COVID-19 patients (7 deaths) or the 29 bulk-only COVID-19 patients reconstructed into single-cell profiles by PBISC (`PBISC new`, 6 deaths; **Fig. 7a**). Removing `C116` and `C139` eliminated patient-intrinsic compositional leverage (`Real` `B` cells $0\%$, `NK` cells $17\text{--}57\%$; `PBISC` `C116` `B` cells $38\%$) and sharpened baseline severity correlations in `Real` (`B ASC dividing` $\rho: +0.39 \to +0.54$; `NK CD56bright` $\rho: -0.47 \to -0.55$; **Supplementary Fig. 1a–c**). Because paired profiling of the 27 shared COVID-19 donors revealed modality-specific baseline percentage offsets between droplet CITE-seq and bulk-derived PBISC—most notably higher transcript-weighted plasmablast and progenitor shares in bulk RNA-seq (`B ASC IgA` median $4.61\%$ vs. $0.49\%$, paired Wilcoxon $P = 7.5 \times 10^{-8}$; `HSPC` $0.48\%$ vs. $0.13\%$, $P = 3.0 \times 10^{-4}$; **Supplementary Fig. 1i**)—we combined `Real` ($n = 37$) and `PBISC new` ($n = 29$) using within-source rank-based inverse normal scores (`Normal score`, $n = 66$ patients, 13 deaths), while verifying all findings in raw `% of PBMCs` (**Supplementary Fig. 1j–o**).

We next screened all 43 fine-grained leukocyte subsets ($\ge 30\%$ detection in `Real`, excluding red blood cells and platelets) against peak WHO severity (Spearman $\rho$) and fatal outcome (Mann–Whitney $\mathrm{AUC}$) using a pre-specified five-step selection funnel (**Fig. 7b, c** and **Supplementary Fig. 2a**). Across `Real + PBISC new`, 16 subsets reached Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) significance ($q < 0.05$ for severity or $q < 0.10$ for death); 15 showed concordant effect directions of non-trivial magnitude ($|\rho| \ge 0.10$ or $|\mathrm{AUC} - 0.5| \ge 0.05$) in both cohorts (excluding `NKT`, where `Real` $\rho = -0.01$), and 12 remained significant ($P < 0.05$) after adjusting for recruitment centre/wave and excluding the dexamethasone-induced monocyte state (`CD14 mono dex`, `CD14 mono IFN`, and `MAIT` removed; **Fig. 7b** and **Supplementary Fig. 2d**). Four of these 12 subsets—`NK CD56bright` ($\rho = -0.50, q = 3.7 \times 10^{-4}$), `B ASC dividing` ($\rho = +0.48, q = 6.1 \times 10^{-4}$), `CD16 mono` ($\rho = -0.40, q = 0.0062$), and `B ASC IgM` ($\rho = +0.31, q = 0.042$), together with `T CD8 CM` for severity ($\rho = -0.50, q = 3.7 \times 10^{-4}$)—were already FDR-significant in `Real` alone and replicated with indistinguishable effect sizes in `PBISC new` ($P_{\mathrm{het}} = 0.20\text{--}0.62$), serving as cross-cohort calibration anchors (**Fig. 7c, e** and **Supplementary Fig. 1d–h**). The remaining 8 subsets required adding `PBISC new` to cross FDR significance (**Fig. 7b, c**).

To separate genuine biological signals from reconstruction noise or cohort imbalance, we evaluated all 8 PBISC-dependent candidates across four credibility criteria: cross-source effect-size homogeneity ($P_{\mathrm{het}}$), patient-level concordance between `PBISC` and `Real` across the 27 shared donors, stability across 14 analytical and confounder-adjusted variants, and mortality discrimination within severe/critical (`WHO 2–3`) disease (**Fig. 7d** and **Supplementary Fig. 2e**). Three candidates (`NK CD56dim`, `T γδ`, and `CD16 mono C1`) showed weak shared-donor tracking ($\rho = 0.07\text{--}0.19, P \ge 0.34$) or `PBISC new`-restricted bulk lymphopenia (`NK CD56dim` severity $P_{\mathrm{het}} = 0.011$; **Supplementary Fig. 1h**) and were not pursued further. Five focus populations spanning six clinical associations met credibility benchmarks for follow-up (**Fig. 7d–k**):

1. **Power-rescued class-switched plasmablast expansion (`B ASC IgG` and `B ASC IgA`)**: Both subsets exhibited virtually identical positive correlations with WHO severity in `Real` ($\rho = +0.36, P = 0.029$ and $\rho = +0.38, P = 0.021$) and `PBISC new` ($\rho = +0.42, P = 0.023$ and $\rho = +0.39, P = 0.037$; $P_{\mathrm{het}} = 0.77$ and $0.97$), yet neither survived 43-cell-type multiple-testing correction in `Real` alone ($q = 0.097$ and $0.074$). Combining `Real + PBISC new` rescued both to robust FDR significance (`B ASC IgG` $\rho = +0.39, P = 0.0012, q = 0.0071$; `B ASC IgA` $\rho = +0.38, P = 0.0015, q = 0.0078$), held across all 14/14 sensitivity variants (**Fig. 7d–g** and **Supplementary Fig. 2e**), and increased detection power at 20 `Real` patients from $29\%$ and $34\%$ to $96\%$ and $97\%$ (vs. $15\%$ and $16\%$ when `PBISC new` severity labels were permuted; $n_{80}$ reduced from not reached and $33$ to $\le 10$ `Real` patients; **Fig. 7m** and **Supplementary Fig. 2b, c**).
2. **Cytotoxic and central-memory $\mathrm{CD8^+}$ T-cell depletion (`T CD8 CM` and `T CD8 CTL`)**: While `T CD8 CM` contraction with severity was already established in `Real` ($\rho = -0.57$), its association with fatal outcome was underpowered in `Real` alone ($\mathrm{AUC} = 0.31, P = 0.13$) and reached significance upon adding `PBISC new` ($\mathrm{AUC} = 0.28, P = 0.015, q = 0.073; P_{\mathrm{het}} = 0.70$; power at $n = 20$ `Real` $9\% \to 71\%$; **Fig. 7e, i, m**). By contrast, `T CD8 CTL` depletion with both severity ($\rho = -0.35, P = 0.0045, q = 0.019$) and death ($\mathrm{AUC} = 0.28, P = 0.014, q = 0.073$; 14/14 and 12/14 sensitivity variants) originated predominantly from the Wave-1 `PBISC new` cohort (`Real` $\rho = -0.14$ vs. `PBISC new` $\rho = -0.60, P_{\mathrm{het}} = 0.035$; **Fig. 7h, j**). Crucially, among all candidates, `T CD8 CTL` achieved the highest single-donor fidelity between `PBISC` and `Real` on the 27 shared patients ($\rho = +0.55, P = 0.0030$; **Fig. 7d**), indicating true clinical enrichment of cytotoxic T-cell loss in steroid-naive Wave-1 disease rather than deconvolution error. Neither $\mathrm{CD8^+}$ subset discriminated death within `WHO 2–3` patients ($\mathrm{AUC} = 0.46$ and $0.30, P \ge 0.069$), marking severe disease burden rather than severity-independent mortality.
3. **Severity-independent mortality prediction by circulating `HSPC` mobilization**: `HSPC` abundance showed identical discrimination of fatal COVID-19 in `Real` (7 deaths; $\mathrm{AUC} = 0.81, P = 0.012, q = 0.13$) and `PBISC new` (6 deaths; $\mathrm{AUC} = 0.80, P = 0.022$; $P_{\mathrm{het}} = 0.97$), crossing multi-test significance only when combined ($\mathrm{AUC} = 0.81, P = 6.3 \times 10^{-4}, q = 0.014$; 13/14 sensitivity variants; **Fig. 7d, e, k**). Doubling fatal events from 7 to 13 resolved critical vulnerabilities of the `Real` cohort alone (**Fig. 7l**): `HSPC` became significant under the published Table S1 acute mortality definition ($5 \to 11$ deaths; `Real` $P = 0.13 \to$ `Combined` $\mathrm{AUC} = 0.76, P = 0.0066$) and direct COVID-19 deaths ($3 \to 9$ deaths; `Real` $P = 0.36 \to$ `Combined` $\mathrm{AUC} = 0.75, P = 0.019$), remained significant when restricted to Cambridge `Real` donors ($6 \to 12$ deaths; $\mathrm{AUC} = 0.85, P = 4.6 \times 10^{-4}$) or samples collected $> 7$ days post-swab ($\mathrm{AUC} = 0.86, P = 0.0011$), and—uniquely among the 12 subsets retained at selection step 4—predicted death within `WHO 2–3` patients ($\mathrm{AUC} = 0.77, P = 0.015$) as well as in severity-stratified permutation testing ($\mathrm{AUC} = 0.77, P = 0.039$). Subsampling confirmed a jump in mortality detection power at 20 `Real` patients from $41\%$ to $99\%$ (shuffled null $18\%$; $n_{80}: 31 \to \le 10$; **Fig. 7m**).

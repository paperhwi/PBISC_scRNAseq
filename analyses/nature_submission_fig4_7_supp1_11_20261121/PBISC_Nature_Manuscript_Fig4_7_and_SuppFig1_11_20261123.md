## Results

### Paired clinical cohorts establish same-donor single-cell reconstruction and cell-type-resolved disease-signal recovery

Before deploying PBISC on clinical cohorts lacking single-cell measurements, we rigorously tested whether adapting the pretrained 6,000-gene (`1×`) PBISC model on just five paired support donors per cohort accurately reconstructs single-cell transcriptomes and recovers cell-type-resolved disease programs when benchmarked directly against observed (`Real`) single cells of the **exact same held-out donors** (**Fig. 4a**). We evaluated two independent acute clinical cohorts containing matched bulk RNA-seq and peripheral blood mononuclear cell (PBMC) scRNA-seq (stage 1): bacterial sepsis (Ye et al.¹; `GSE279448` bulk and `GSE279452` scRNA-seq) and Imperial College end-stage kidney disease (`ESKD`) COVID-19 (Gisby et al.²; Stephenson et al.³). Within each cohort, five paired support donors adapted pretrained PBISC via low-rank adaptation (`LoRA`; stage 2), and `4,000` single cells generated from each held-out bulk sample (`×1` bulk inputs with support-gated ceiling counts) were compared directly against the observed single cells of that same donor (stage 3). `PBISC with LoRA` (`PBISC`) and the semi-profiling baseline `scSemiProfiler`⁴ (retaining its recorded Poisson-count readout) were evaluated on the exact same five support donors and the exact same held-out bulk samples, with all support donors and their other longitudinal visits strictly excluded from test sets. To quantify sensitivity to support-donor choice without selecting references by benchmark performance, we used three deterministic fixed reference selections per cohort (full-manuscript `Supplementary Figs. 8 and 9`): **Sepsis `D0/D1/D3`**, each comprising four adult sepsis cases and one healthy control (`4:1` case:control — `D0`: adult healthy control `HC07`; `D1`: adult healthy control `HC12`; `D3`: paediatric healthy control `PHC05`; clinically excluding `D2: PIC01` paediatric ICU and `D4: IC23` adult ICU as non-healthy ICU comparators) and holding out `77` paired sepsis donors per reference; and **COVID-19 `D0/D1/D4`**, each comprising three COVID-19-positive and two COVID-19-negative ESKD support donors (`3:2` — `D0`: `C106, C237`; `D1`: `C142, C164`; `D4`: `C152, C137`) and holding out `52` paired COVID-19 donors across `142–143` visits (alongside paired reconstruction and exploratory DEG boundary audits in `Giroux` [`4` unique held-out donors per reference, `10–11` visits] and `Ji` `ChILI` [`13–15` unique donors, `19–26` visits]; full-manuscript `Supplementary Figs. 8 and 9`).

When projected directly from the saved 50-dimensional three-source shared `PRE-PCA` representation of the first fixed reference (`D0`; `11,550` cells per source across `77` visits in sepsis and `21,250` cells per source across `142` visits in COVID-19)—**with no Harmony⁵ or other post-hoc batch integration or source correction applied**—`PBISC` cells overlapped seamlessly with `Real` cells across all six broad PBMC types (`T`, `B`, `Monocyte`, `NK`, `DC` and `Other`), whereas `scSemiProfiler` cells separated into distinct source-specific clusters (**Fig. 4b–e**). Quantifying matched source mixing directly in this unintegrated 50-dimensional `PRE-PCA` space across the three fixed references (splitting each retained visit's observed cells into two equal disjoint halves of $\le 75$ cells per side and averaging three query-seed medians per reference; **Fig. 4l,m**) confirmed strong native manifold mixing for `PBISC` (`iLISI = 1.51` versus the observed split-cell reference `1.77` in sepsis; `1.44` versus `1.76` in COVID-19), whereas `scSemiProfiler` remained completely unmixed in both cohorts (`iLISI = 1.00`, matching the no-mixing baseline). Across donor-balanced three-reference arithmetic profiles spanning `5,975` shared genes in sepsis and `5,927` shared genes in COVID-19 (retaining zero means; 101-rank `log1p`-mean smoothing), `PBISC` closely tracked observed gene-expression levels across more than four orders of magnitude, achieving unsmoothed `log1p` gene-mean Pearson `r_ref` $= 0.974 \pm 0.005$ versus $0.953 \pm 0.015$ for `scSemiProfiler` in sepsis and `r_ref` $= 0.972 \pm 0.004$ versus $0.969 \pm 0.003$ in COVID-19 (**Fig. 4f,h**), while reproducing the broad natural per-cell UMI depth dispersion of `Real` across 10²–10⁴ UMIs rather than collapsing onto `scSemiProfiler`'s narrow unimodal Poisson spike near 10³ UMIs (**Fig. 4g,i**). Consistently, `PBISC` preserved donor-balanced six-type PBMC compositions in both sepsis (`54.7%` T, `14.0%` B, `20.8%` monocyte, `5.9%` NK, `4.0%` DC and `0.6%` Other versus `48.4%`, `17.6%`, `20.6%`, `7.8%`, `4.9%` and `0.7%` in `Real`, whereas `scSemiProfiler` inflated T cells to `58.6%` and Other to `7.8%` while compressing B cells to `7.0%`; **Fig. 4j**) and COVID-19 (`60.2%` T, `9.4%` B, `15.7%` monocyte, `10.3%` NK, `2.9%` DC and `1.3%` Other versus `57.2%`, `9.5%`, `14.7%`, `14.6%`, `2.6%` and `1.3%` in `Real`, versus `64.4%` T, `6.5%` B and `9.2%` NK in `scSemiProfiler`; **Fig. 4k**), together with 72-gene donor-level immune-program covariance across `T`, `B`, `Monocyte` and `NK` populations (full-manuscript `Supplementary Fig. 10`).

We next asked whether generated single cells of held-out donors recover native cell-type-resolved disease transcriptional effects across `T`, `B`, `Monocyte` (`M`) and `NK` populations using identical cell-type-specific visit masks, observed-defined gene families, `TMM` normalization and `limma-voom` moderated tests accounting for repeated donor–condition observations (Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted $P < 0.05$ without an additional fold-change cutoff; comparing adult/paediatric sepsis cases against healthy controls excluding ICU, and COVID-19-positive against COVID-19-negative ESKD samples excluding recovery; **Fig. 4n**). In ESKD COVID-19, `PBISC` substantially outperformed `scSemiProfiler` across all four cell types on three-reference mean **paired `logFC r`** (`0.79` [`T`], `0.68` [`B`], `0.82` [`M`] and `0.77` [`NK`] versus `0.58`, `0.35`, `0.63` and `0.60`), boosted **DEG `recall` 5- to 11-fold** (`0.70`, `0.62`, `0.63` and `0.60` versus `0.14`, `0.06`, `0.11` and `0.09`) and consistently increased **DEG `F1`** (`0.33`, `0.25`, `0.53` and `0.46` versus `0.19`, `0.09`, `0.18` and `0.15`), corroborated by three-reference mean native disease-effect agreement (`PBISC` Pearson $r = 0.80\text{–}0.87$, Lin `CCC` $= 0.79\text{–}0.85$ versus `scSemiProfiler` $r = 0.41\text{–}0.68$, `CCC` $= 0.37\text{–}0.64$; full-manuscript `Supplementary Fig. 13`). In bacterial sepsis, while `paired logFC r` was comparable in lymphocytes and higher for `PBISC` in monocytes (`0.50` [`T`], `0.51` [`B`], `0.60` [`M`] and `0.47` [`NK`] versus `0.50`, `0.51`, `0.51` and `0.52`), `scSemiProfiler` suffered near-total loss of DEG sensitivity—recovering only `3–8%` of observed DEGs (`DEG recall = 0.05, 0.03, 0.05` and `0.08`; `DEG F1 = 0.07, 0.04, 0.09` and `0.13`; calling only `33` T-cell and `9` B-cell DEGs versus `818` and `422` in `Real` on `D0`)—whereas `PBISC` elevated **DEG `recall` 3- to 12-fold** (`0.44`, `0.31`, `0.40` and `0.24`), raised **DEG `F1` 2- to 5-fold** (`0.33`, `0.23`, `0.41` and `0.27`), and improved monocyte **DEG `precision`** (`0.47` versus `0.34`; full-manuscript `Supplementary Fig. 12`). Across `12` `Real`-selected disease-program genes per cell type (`ln(1 + CP10K)`, `Real`-scaled across references `1–3`), donor–condition pseudobulk heatmaps confirmed coherent case–control recovery by `PBISC` across every reference selection—capturing inflammatory/JAK–STAT induction (`NFKBIA`, `SOCS3`, `NFKBIZ`, `PIM1`, `CISH`, `STAT2`, `GBP4`, `IFIT1`) alongside T-cell signalling downregulation (`RASGRP1`, `ITGAL`, `SH2D1A`, `CD3G`) in sepsis T cells (**Fig. 4o**), stress/inflammatory induction (`NFKBIA`, `CDKN1A`, `RHOB`, `SOCS3`, `SESN1`, `FKBP5`, `IFIT1`) alongside BCR-pathway repression (`MEF2C`, `SYK`, `TLR10`, `PRKCB`, `PLD4`) in sepsis B cells (**Fig. 4p**), coordinated type-I interferon and checkpoint/stress induction (`IFI6`, `ISG15`, `IFI44L`, `MX1`, `XAF1`, `IRF7`, `IFIT3`, `OAS1`, `CISH`, `LAG3`, `PIM1`, `NR4A2`) in COVID-19 T cells (**Fig. 4q**), and interferon/plasmablast activation (`IFI44L`, `IFITM1`, `IFI6`, `ISG15`, `XAF1`, `MX1`, `IFIT3`, `STAT1`, `CD38`, `FCRL5`) with `GPR183`/`NR4A2` modulation in COVID-19 B cells (**Fig. 4r**, with paired monocyte and NK counterparts in full-manuscript `Supplementary Fig. 11`). Having validated same-donor reconstruction and paired disease-signal recovery, these same two clinical cohorts supplied the unpaired bulk-only expansion samples (`122` sepsis samples yielding `488,000` PBISC cells versus `633,072` `Real` adult cells across `108` samples, and `164` ESKD COVID-19 samples yielding `656,000` PBISC cells versus `578,835` `Real` cells across `187` samples; cohort gene coverage, cell totals and full-cohort UMI depth distributions in **Supplementary Fig. 1**) analysed in **Figs. 5–7**.

### Preservation of lineage-resolved disease differentially expressed genes across sepsis and COVID-19

We next tested whether PBISC-generated single-cell profiles from bulk-only patients faithfully recover both whole-PBMC and cell-type-resolved case-versus-control differentially expressed genes (DEGs; Benjamini–Hochberg `BH` $q < 0.05$, $|\log_2\text{FC}| > 1$) across 5,664 expressed panel genes in sepsis and 5,610 genes in COVID-19 (**Fig. 5** and **Supplementary Figs. 4 and 5**). Aggregated across whole PBMCs, PBISC pseudobulk fold changes matched independent cohort bulk RNA-seq benchmarks at the same fidelity as observed scRNA-seq: against `GSE279448` in bacterial sepsis (`4,157` shared genes), PBISC achieved reliability-corrected slope = 0.58 ($r = 0.70$) versus `Real` slope = 0.59 ($r = 0.70$) and `scSemiProfiler` slope = 0.16 ($r = 0.15$; **Fig. 5a** and **Supplementary Fig. 4a**); against `Zenodo 6497251` in ESKD COVID-19 (`4,067` shared genes), PBISC achieved slope = 0.79 ($r = 0.64$) versus `Real` slope = 0.59 ($r = 0.66$) and `scSemiProfiler` slope = 0.76 ($r = 0.37$; **Fig. 5b** and **Supplementary Fig. 5a**).

Within individual major lineages (`T`, `B`, `NK` and `Monocyte`), PBISC ranked true `Real` DEGs near the empirical `Real` split-half reliability ceiling across both acute inflammatory syndromes (**Fig. 5c–f**). In bacterial sepsis, PBISC achieved `Real`-DEG ranking `AUROC`s of 0.96 (`T`), 0.86 (`B`), 0.83 (`NK`) and 0.78 (`Monocyte`)—closely tracking the empirical `Real` split-half ceiling (0.94, 0.89, 0.86 and 0.91)—with 83–99% directional sign agreement on `Real` DEGs, double-disattenuated correlations $r = 0.81, 0.75, 0.66$ and $0.66$, and reliability-corrected effect-size slopes of 1.10, 1.11, 1.07 and 0.71 (**Fig. 5c,e** and **Supplementary Fig. 4b**). In ESKD COVID-19, PBISC achieved `AUROC`s of 0.99 (`T`), 0.87 (`B`), 0.85 (`NK`) and 0.88 (`Monocyte`) against split-half ceilings of 0.99, 0.89, 0.97 and 0.94 (sign agreement 93–100%; disattenuated $r = 0.77, 0.83, 0.58$ and $0.77$; corrected slopes = 0.81, 1.78, 0.76 and 0.79; **Fig. 5d,f** and **Supplementary Fig. 5b**). By contrast, `scSemiProfiler` suffered severe loss of both ranking and calibration in sepsis (`AUROC` = 0.56–0.65, slopes = 0.15–0.31, calling zero `NK` DEGs) and collapsed B-cell regulation in COVID-19 (`AUROC` = 0.68, disattenuated $r = 0.09$, slope = 0.09, 4 DEGs).

Inspecting lineage-specific volcano plots confirmed faithful preservation of canonical disease biology across all four lineages (**Fig. 5g,h** and **Supplementary Figs. 4c–f and 5c–f**). In sepsis monocytes (**Fig. 5g**), PBISC (`463` DEGs across 60 sepsis / 34 control pseudobulks) mirrored `Real` (`365` DEGs across 86/7) by upregulating `IL1R2`, `ACSL1`, `AQP9` and `TNFAIP6` while repressing class-II antigen-presentation transcripts `HLA-DMB` and `CIITA` (alongside lymphocyte `CD69`/`RPS26`/`CD83` induction and `CX3CR1`/`ZNF683` loss; **Supplementary Fig. 4c–f**), whereas `scSemiProfiler` recovered only 46 sparse DEGs. In COVID-19 T cells (**Fig. 5h**), PBISC recapitulated selective type-I interferon induction (`IFIT3`, `IFI44L`, `MX1`, `IFIT1`, `IRF7`, `OAS1`) alongside monocyte `CD163`/`MCEMP1` upregulation and `VMO1`/`CDKN1C` downregulation (**Supplementary Fig. 5c–f**), avoiding the inflated false-positive interferon log-fold changes observed in `scSemiProfiler` (`170` T-cell DEGs with `ISG15`/`IFI6`/`MX1` $\log_2\text{FC} = +4.2\text{ to }+4.6$ versus $+1.3\text{ to }+1.7$ in `Real`).

### Reproduction of disease-specific rare immune populations and cell-intrinsic single-cell states across bacterial sepsis and COVID-19

A stringent test of generative bulk-to-single-cell synthesis is whether bulk-conditioned profiles retain low-abundance disease-specific immune populations ($<0.1\%$ to $2\%$ of PBMC) and cell-intrinsic single-cell programs rather than collapsing onto dominant lineage centroids or suffering the collinearity distortions of bulk deconvolution (**Fig. 6** and **Supplementary Figs. 2 and 3**). Across 14 rare immune states in bacterial sepsis cases and 13 rare states in ESKD COVID-19 cases transferred onto the shared Harmony reference, PBISC recovered 13 of 14 sepsis states and 12 of 13 COVID-19 states down to $<0.05\%$ of parent lineage (missing only ultra-sparse innate lymphoid cells (`ILC`)), whereas `scSemiProfiler` collapsed to identically zero cells in 7 of 14 sepsis states and 8 of 13 COVID-19 states—eliminating cycling `CD4+` T, `CD8+` T, NK and B cells, proliferating `Mono_c06-CD14-MKI67` monocytes, `B_ASC_IgG`, `CD14mono_dex`, `DC1`, `ASDC` and circulating hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (`HSPC`) (**Fig. 6a,b**). Before benchmarking proliferating monocytes in sepsis, we audited raw co-expression across the 82 paired sepsis donors in observed scRNA-seq, confirming that `CD14+ MKI67+ STMN1+` monocytes (both with and without granulocytic `MPO`/`ELANE`) occur predominantly in abdominal and pulmonary sepsis and paediatric infections (**Fig. 6c**).

In the integrated Harmony UMAP embeddings, PBISC-generated rare cells from unpaired bulk-only patients localized precisely onto their cognate `Real A` micro-clusters in **both** cohorts—overlaying `B_c07-MZB1` plasmablasts, `C1QA+` macrophages, `pDC`s and cycling `B_c08-MKI67` cells in bacterial sepsis (**Fig. 6d**), as well as `pDC`s, conventional `DC1`s, `B_ASC_dividing` plasmablasts and `MAIT` cells in ESKD COVID-19 (**Fig. 6e**). To verify that these rare cells carry genuine state-specific transcriptional identity beyond their parent lineage, we subtracted parent-lineage baselines across all 2,000 `Real A` HVGs (**Fig. 6f,g**, left). Diagonal `Real–PBISC` HVG specificity correlations reached $r = 0.97$ (`B_c07-MZB1`), $0.93$ (`Macrophage`), $0.55$ (`T_CD4_c07-MKI67`) and $0.72$ (`Mono_c06-CD14-MKI67`) in sepsis, and $r = 0.96$ (`B_ASC_IgG`), $0.93$ (`CD14mono_dex`), $0.97$ (`pDC`) and $0.92$ (`HSPC`) in COVID-19, with near-zero or negative off-diagonal correlations. At the individual-sample level (**Fig. 6f,g**, right), pairwise correlations between individual `PBISC` bulk-only patients and individual `Real` scRNA-seq patients (`Real–PBISC` median sample-pair $r$) approached the empirical `Real–Real` biological replicate ceiling across every state: `0.94` versus `0.94` (`B_c07-MZB1`), `0.85` versus `0.91` (`Macrophage`), `0.48` versus `0.61` (`T_CD4_c07-MKI67`) and `0.66` versus `0.94` (`Mono_c06-CD14-MKI67`) in sepsis; and `0.91` versus `0.88` (`B_ASC_IgG`), `0.87` versus `0.92` (`CD14mono_dex`), `0.90` versus `0.89` (`pDC`) and `0.82` versus `0.82` (`HSPC`) in COVID-19 (versus $-0.25$ to $+0.08$ for mismatched cross-state pairs). Across individual patients (`49,999`-iteration centered patient-block bootstrap), PBISC faithfully preserved sepsis `Mono_c06-CD14-MKI67` patient abundance and coordinated monocyte (`CD14`, `S100A8`, `S100A9`, `VCAN`) plus mitotic (`MKI67`, `STMN1`, `TOP2A`, `TYMS`) marker modules (`n = 15` `PBISC` and `17` `Real` positive samples; `scSemiProfiler`: 0; **Fig. 6h**), alongside canonical abundance and marker profiles across eight additional sepsis and COVID-19 rare states (`B_c07-MZB1`, `Macrophage`, `T_CD4_c07-MKI67`, `T_CD8_c06-MKI67`, `B_ASC_IgG`, `CD14mono_dex`, `pDC` and `HSPC`; **Supplementary Figs. 2 and 3**).

To test quantitative fidelity on matched clinical donors before downstream clinical discovery, we next evaluated the `27` non-CLL `Shared-27` ESKD COVID-19 bridge donors profiled by both observed scRNA-seq and complementary-visit PBISC synthesis (`59` visits; held-out `Real B` 30-NN transfer precision/recall = `99.0%/95.4%` for `HSPC` and `98.4%/98.6%` for pooled `B ASC`; **Fig. 6i**). PBISC tracked donor-level abundances across fine clinical compartments (`NK CD56bright`, `B ASC dividing/IgG/IgA`, `T CD8 CM`, `T CD8 CTL` and `HSPC`) while showing reproducible modality scale offsets (`0.3×` to `9.4×`), establishing an empirical basis for within-source rank-based inverse normal transformation (`rank-INT`) prior to pooling `Real` and `PBISC new`. Critically, benchmarking PBISC out-of-donor on `Shared-27` (two donor-disjoint folds) against **10 official R 4.3.2 bulk deconvolution implementations**—`BayesPrism`⁶ v2.2.3 (initial and updated Gibbs posteriors), `BisqueRNA`⁷ v1.0.5, `CIBERSORTx`⁸ (`B-mode` and `S-mode` ComBat), `CIBERSORT-R` SVR, `Calib-NNLS`, `MuSiC`⁹ v1.0.0, `NNLS` and `DWLS`¹⁰ v0.1.0—exposed two severe failure modes of classical deconvolution on fine-grained and rare clinical states (**Fig. 6j,k**). First, PBISC achieved the highest accuracy across all 11 methods for the lead cytotoxic mortality compartment `T CD8 CTL` ($\rho = +0.55$ versus $+0.46$ for `BayesPrism-init`, $+0.36$ for `CIBERSORTx B-mode`, $+0.24$ for `DWLS`, $+0.22$ for `BisqueRNA`, $+0.21$ for `BayesPrism-upd`, $+0.15$ for `MuSiC`, $+0.09$ for `CIBERSORT-R`, $+0.06$ for `Calib-NNLS`, $+0.02$ for `CIBERSORTx S-mode`, and complete `0%` zero-collapse in `NNLS`), while avoiding zero-collapse across all flagship clinical lineages (`Med ρ = +0.24` across all 43 subsets and `+0.22` across 23 rare subsets `<1%` abundance; **Fig. 6j**). Second, multiple deconvolution regressors suffered severe **collinearity sink inflation** on ultra-rare `HSPC`s (observed `Real` median `0.134%`, `PBISC` `0.48%`): `BayesPrism` inflated median `HSPC` **`37–43×`** (`5.82%` initial and `4.96%` updated of total PBMC), `Calib-NNLS` inflated `HSPC` **`31×`** (`4.19%`), `DWLS` inflated `HSPC` **`13×`** (`1.73%`) and `BisqueRNA` inflated `HSPC` **`11×`** (`1.52%`), whereas `CIBERSORTx B-mode` (`0.01%`), `CIBERSORT-R` (`0.00%`) and `NNLS` (`0.00%`) collapsed median `HSPC` to near-zero (**Fig. 6k**).

Beyond abundance, PBISC uniquely preserved **cell-intrinsic single-cell transcriptional states** invisible to bulk markers or deconvolution. Across `3,124` total single `HSPC`s in primary acute draws (`3,076` cells across the `149` draws with $\ge 3$ `HSPC`s: `74` `Real` + `75` `PBISC new` across `61` patients), PBISC faithfully reproduced a fine-grained **20-gene intracellular `HSPC` erythroid lineage switch**: both `Real` and `PBISC new` concordantly captured progenitor-maintenance `GATA2` downregulation (combined same-day WHO GEE $\beta = -0.213, q_{20} = 0.011$) coupled to coordinated upregulation of five erythroid/megakaryocytic transcripts (`APOC1` $q_{20} = 3.7 \times 10^{-5}$, `ITGA2B` $9.7 \times 10^{-5}$, `GATA1` $0.011$, `KLF1` $0.011$, `BLVRB` $0.012$; while `TFRC` was flat on same-day WHO [$q_{20} = 0.26$] yet was the strongest single-gene baseline first-draw mortality predictor [`AUC = 0.769, P = 0.0056`]) and three mitotic transcripts (`TYMS` $q_{20} = 4.2 \times 10^{-5}$, `STMN1` $0.035$, `MKI67` $0.045$; `TOP2A` trend $P = 0.090$), whereas all five quiescent stem genes (`CD34`, `AVP`, `HLF`, `HOPX`, `CRHBP`; $q_{20} = 0.20\text{–}0.74$) and granulocytic `MPO`/`CSF3R` ($q_{20} = 0.17\text{–}0.61$) remained flat alongside modest positive slopes in `ELANE` ($\beta = +0.121, q_{20} = 0.013$) and `CEBPD` ($\beta = +0.145, q_{20} = 0.024$; **Fig. 6l**). Conversely, on `Wave-1 New-29` patients (`n = 29, 6` deaths), raw bulk `PROM1`, `AVP`, `CD34`, `SPINK2` and a 6-gene bulk `HSPC` panel all failed to capture fatal `HSPC` biology (`AUC` = 0.29–0.57, all $P \ge 0.42$), whereas `PBISC HSPC` synthesis achieved `AUC = 0.80` ($P = 0.022$; **Supplementary Fig. 10d**).

### Clinical discovery in expanded ESKD COVID-19: exact metadata matching, count-versus-percentage validation and T-cell ISG scoring

To establish clinical validity without sampling artefacts or metric ambiguity—and to distinguish conserved COVID-19 hallmarks from newly unlocked clinical associations in high-risk ESKD dialysis patients—we organized our expanded ESKD COVID-19 discovery framework (**Fig. 7** and **Supplementary Figs. 6–11**) around three rigorous pillars: **(1) exact 1:1 patient and dataset metadata matching and rare-cell FDR rescue**, **(2) population number (single-cell raw counts and clinical CBC `cells/µL`) versus percentage (`% PBMC` and `% parent`) auditing**, and **(3) deep 19-gene T-cell `ISG` scoring analysis**.

#### 1. Exact patient and dataset metadata matching, longitudinal pairing and rare-cell discovery rescue
A comprehensive sample-level audit across both cohorts verified exact 1:1 patient-to-dataset linkage with zero unverified merges (**Fig. 7a** and **Supplementary Figs. 6, 7 and 8a–c**; complete line listings in `PBISC_Metadata_CountVsPct_TISG_Audit_20261121.xlsx`). In bacterial sepsis (`255` total cross-sectional samples), observed `Real` scRNA-seq comprised `108` adult donors (`93` sepsis, `8` ICU, `7` HC; median age 67 [IQR 54–74] yr, 70.4% male; median `5,664` [IQR 4,727–6,846] cells/sample) plus `25` paediatric donors (`17` sepsis, `4` ICU, `4` HC), whereas bulk-only `PBISC` comprised `121` primary adults (`68` sepsis, `17` ICU, `36` HC; median age 65 [54–74] yr, 64.5% male; fixed `4,000` cells/sample) plus `1` paediatric ICU control (`PIC06`), with **zero donor overlap (`0` shared donors)** and `119/122` molecular sex concordance (`2` ambiguous healthy controls `HC23`/`HC30`; `1` GEO-versus-bulk discordant sample `Res-S34`, flagged explicitly in **Supplementary Fig. 6b**). Combining `37` primary `Real` scRNA-seq patients (`10` Real-only + `27` `Shared-27` counted once in `Real`; `138` acute draws, `5` deaths) with `29` scRNA-naive Wave-1 `PBISC new` patients (`79` acute draws, `6` deaths) expanded total patients by `+78%` (`37 → 66`), critical `WHO 3` illness by `+140%` (`5 → 12`), verified deaths by `+120%` (`5 → 11`) and direct COVID-19 deaths by `+200%` (`3 → 9`; **Fig. 7b**, left). We therefore anchored primary inference on **exact longitudinal pairing**: (i) all `217` draws paired with contemporaneous same-day WHO severity via patient-clustered exchangeable `GEE +wk` and Mundlak within–between decomposition; and (ii) **one baseline observation per patient—each donor's first blood draw** (`N = 66, 11` deaths; `53/66` patients and `10/11` fatal cases sampled $\le 7\text{ d}$ post-swab)—paired with final mortality (`TRUE_PUB_11`).

Screening all 43 fine-grained immune subsets ($\ge 30\%$ `Real` detection) uncovered **20 combined-FDR associations** (`18` same-day WHO `GEE +wk` $q_{43} < 0.05$ + `2` baseline first-draw mortality predictors at $q_{43} = 0.070 < 0.10$; **Fig. 7b**, right, and **Supplementary Fig. 8d,e**). Separating pharmacological Wave-2 steroid induction (`CD14mono_dex`³, $q_{43} = 4.4 \times 10^{-8}$ on same-day WHO; neutral at baseline draw 1 [`AUC` = 0.599, $P = 0.286$, `adjWHO P = 0.664`] before surging at `>7 d` [`AUC` = 0.918, $P = 8.0 \times 10^{-4}$]) and two sparse `Real`-only subsets (`ASDC`, `T CD8 Naive IFN stim`) yielded **`17` credible non-steroid sign-concordant discoveries** comprising two distinct tiers:

#### 2. Population number (raw count and clinical CBC) versus percentage (`% PBMC` and `% parent`) check and resolution of rare-cell clinical axes
Because `Real` scRNA-seq library recovery varied `40×` across acute draws (`n_cells` median `2,204` [IQR 1,092–4,116], range `226–9,254`) whereas `PBISC new` synthesized a constant `4,000` cells per draw, we explicitly audited whether clinical severity and mortality associations depend on expressing immune populations as **cell percentages (`% PBMC` or `% parent lineage`)** versus **absolute cell numbers (`Raw Count` per sample or true clinical hospital CBC absolute counts `cells/µL`)** (**Fig. 7c** and **Supplementary Fig. 9a–d**). First, in `Real` scRNA-seq alone (`138` draws), total recovered `n_cells` was not significantly associated with either contemporaneous same-day WHO severity ($\rho = -0.129, P = 0.131$) or first-draw mortality (`AUC` = 0.575, $P = 0.620$; **Supplementary Fig. 9b**), ruling out systematic sequencing-depth confounding. Second, across all 43 COVID-19 subsets, within-source `rank-INT` of **raw single-cell counts** and **`% PBMC`** yielded virtually identical effect sizes for both contemporaneous same-day WHO severity (**Pearson $r = 0.9870$**, `18` versus `18` subsets passing `BH43` $q_{43} < 0.05$, and `18` FDR also under `% Parent INT`; **Fig. 7c**, left) and first-draw mortality (**Pearson $r = 0.9845$**; **Supplementary Fig. 9a**). The same near-identity held across `44` fine-grained bacterial sepsis subsets ordered across `HC → ICU → Sepsis` (`Real` adult raw count versus `% PBMC` Spearman $\rho$ correlation $r = 0.9925$; `PBISC` $121$ adults $r = 1.0000$, with cross-disease depletion of `NK_c01-GZMK` replicating in both `Real` [$\rho = -0.230$ `%`, $-0.283$ count] and `PBISC` [$\rho = -0.475, P = 3.7 \times 10^{-8}$]; **Supplementary Fig. 9c**).

Crucially, however, comparing metrics also demonstrated **why within-source `% PBMC INT` outperforms raw counts on mortality**: the `40×` capture-depth variance in `Real` injects random noise into raw cell counts, slightly attenuating `T CD8 CTL` mortality discrimination (`AUC` = 0.213 [$P = 0.0029$, `adjWHO P = 0.034`, $q_{43} = 0.070$] under `% PBMC INT` versus `0.232` [$P = 0.0055$, `adjWHO P = 0.062`] under `Count INT`), whereas naively pooling raw counts across `Real` (`2.2k` median) and `PBISC new` (`4.0k` fixed) without within-source normalization severely degrades `T CD8 CTL` to `AUC = 0.311` ($P = 0.050$; **Supplementary Fig. 9a**). Most decisively, computing **true clinical absolute leukocyte subset concentrations (`cells/µL`)** in `Real` by multiplying single-cell subset fractions by contemporaneous clinical hospital CBC total lymphocyte/monocyte counts independently validated every major flagship association (**Fig. 7c**, right, and **Supplementary Fig. 9d**): same-day WHO expansion of `B ASC dividing` ($\beta = +0.373, P = 2.7 \times 10^{-5}$), `B ASC IgG` ($\beta = +0.296, P = 1.8 \times 10^{-4}$) and `CD14mono_IFN` ($\beta = +0.282, P = 0.041$), same-day WHO depletion of `NK CD56bright` ($\beta = -0.388, P = 1.1 \times 10^{-6}$) and `T CD8 CM` ($\beta = -0.338, P = 2.2 \times 10^{-4}$), and first-draw mortality depletion of absolute `T CD8 CTL cells/µL` (`AUC` = 0.226 [inv `0.774`], $P = 0.053$, $n = 36$, 5 deaths).

Under this metric-validated primary framework, stratified effects, Mundlak decomposition, 300-simulation power rescue, external four-cohort replication¹¹,¹³⁻¹⁵ (`N = 220`) and same-day hospital laboratory GEE (`BH36`) cleanly separated three distinct immunopathological axes while resolving apparent conflicts in prior COVID-19 literature (**Fig. 7d–f** and **Supplementary Figs. 9e,f, 10b–f and 11**):
- **Class-switched `B ASC`s dynamically track within-patient contemporaneous severity**: Across 217 draws, `B ASC IgG` ($\beta = +0.414, q_{43} = 1.8 \times 10^{-8}$; Mundlak $\beta_{\text{within}} = +0.418, P = 2.9 \times 10^{-4}$), `B ASC dividing` ($\beta = +0.422, q_{43} = 1.8 \times 10^{-8}$; $\beta_{\text{within}} = +0.377, P = 8.2 \times 10^{-5}$) and `B ASC IgA` ($\beta = +0.334, q_{43} = 1.2 \times 10^{-4}$; $\beta_{\text{within}} = +0.267, P = 0.015$) tracked within-patient day-to-day WHO transitions, coupled tightly to same-day serum ferritin ($\beta = +0.66, q_{36} = 6.1 \times 10^{-12}$) and `D-dimer` ($\beta = +0.58, q_{36} = 1.3 \times 10^{-6}$), replicated across four external COVID-19 cohorts¹¹,¹³⁻¹⁵ (`N = 220`, `B ASC total` DL $\rho = +0.26, P = 0.0063$; Stephenson et al.¹³ isotypes `OR = 1.83–1.98`), and were neutral for first-draw mortality (`AUC` = 0.46–0.51).
- **Baseline cytotoxic `T CD8 CTL` and `NK (CD56dim)` depletion independently predicts acute mortality from the first blood draw, while selective `NK CD56bright` loss distinguishes COVID-19 from bacterial sepsis**: At donor first draw (`N = 66, 11` deaths), `T CD8 CTL` (`AUC` = 0.213 [inv `0.787`], $P = 0.0029, q_{43} = 0.070$; replicating identically in `Real` [`0.206`, $P = 0.036$] and `PBISC new` [`0.210`, $P = 0.031$], $P_{\text{het}} = 0.974$; `adjWHO P = 0.033` [linear `WHO` `0.034`]; early $\le 7\text{ d}$ $P = 0.022$; severe/critical `WHO 2–3` $P = 0.011$) and `NK (CD56dim)` (`AUC` = 0.217 [inv `0.783`], $P = 0.0033, q_{43} = 0.070$; `adjWHO P = 0.030` [linear `WHO` `0.028`]; direct COVID-9 `inv AUC` = 0.844, $P = 0.0010$) were the top two prognostic predictors across all 43 subsets (power rescued from `15–23%` to `99–100%`; `−(T CD8 CTL + NK)` `AUC` = 0.823, $P = 7.9 \times 10^{-4}$), while remaining flat on time-adjusted same-day WHO GEE and orthogonal to routine hospital labs (**Fig. 7d–f**). Meanwhile, decomposing the NK compartment (**Supplementary Fig. 9e**) clarified discordant COVID-19 NK flow-cytometry studies¹⁷⁻¹⁹: in ESKD COVID-19, `NK CD56bright` declined selectively both as `% PBMC` ($q_{43} = 8.2 \times 10^{-4}$; first-draw mortality `AUC` = 0.293, $P = 0.032$; external meta-analysis $P < 0.01$) and within the NK pool while total NK `% PBMC` remained stable across WHO grades, whereas in bacterial sepsis¹ `NK_c01-GZMK` reduction reflected indiscriminate pan-NK collapse shared equally with non-septic ICU controls.
- **Temporal separation of `HSPC` abundance and day-1 intracellular priming reconciles single-cell and flow-cytometry literature**: Although non-ESKD COVID-19 scRNA-seq atlases reported elevated `HSPC % PBMC` with severity¹²⁻¹⁴, admission flow-cytometry studies reported that *lower* absolute `CD34+` cell counts/µL predict ICU transfer and mortality²⁰,²¹, and `HSPC` failed FDR in the 37-patient ESKD scRNA-seq cohort³ alone ($q_{43} = 0.227$). Adding `PBISC new` rescued `HSPC` contemporaneous same-day WHO association (`Combined` $\beta = +0.333, q_{43} = 1.3 \times 10^{-4}$; Mundlak $\beta_{\text{within}} = +0.242, P = 0.0088$; power `10.7% → 100.0%`; external `N = 191` DL $\rho = +0.44, P = 2.4 \times 10^{-7}$) while exposing a sharp temporal split on mortality: peripheral `HSPC %` was flat at baseline first draw (`AUC` = 0.577, $P = 0.43$; early $\le 7\text{ d}$ `AUC` = 0.512, $P = 0.92$, when severe lymphopenia simultaneously contracts total lymphocyte counts/µL) and surged only during week-2 stress haematopoiesis²²⁻²⁴ at `>7 d` (`AUC` = 0.822, $P = 0.011$; paired $n = 43$ shift `0.44 → 0.78`; external `>7 d AUC` = 0.71, $P = 0.011$). Remarkably, however, single-cell `HSPC` `Alarmin_Inflam` (`S100A8/A9/A12`, `AUC` = 0.724, $P = 0.021$, `adjWHO P = 0.022`; replicating identically across `Real` [`0.726`] and `PBISC new` [`0.725`]; `≥1`-cell $N = 64$ `AUC` = 0.767, $P = 0.0057$) and erythroid-commitment transcripts (`Ery5` `AUC` = 0.696, $P = 0.044$; `TFRC` `0.769`, `GATA1` `0.718`, `TYMS` `0.704`, `KLF1` `0.700`) already anticipated fatal outcome on the first `HSPC`-positive draw prior to peripheral numerical egress (**Fig. 6l** and **Supplementary Fig. 10c,d**).

#### 3. Deep 19-gene T-cell ISG scoring analysis: resolving the interferon timing paradox, viral specificity, CTL–ISG coupling and prognosis
Because type-I interferon signalling is a central yet fiercely debated feature of severe COVID-19—with cross-sectional studies reporting either impaired/autoantibody-neutralized IFN responses²⁵,²⁶ or heightened IFN/ISG signatures in severe illness²⁷⁻²⁹ alongside general early IFN induction in ESKD³—we performed a dedicated multi-compartment audit of the 19-gene T-cell `ISG` module across all `13` T-cell subsets (`213–217` acute draws across `66` patients; **Fig. 7g–j** and **Supplementary Fig. 10a,b**). On `Shared-27` bridge donors, PBISC single-cell T-cell `ISG` scoring achieved $\rho = +0.74\text{ to }+0.81$ across all five major T compartments and outperformed official `BayesPrism`⁶ v2.2.3 posterior expression tensors ($Z$) in both `T CD8 CTL` ($\rho = +0.81$ versus $+0.74$) and `T CD8 CM` ($\rho = +0.57$ versus $+0.49$; **Supplementary Fig. 10a,b**).

Decomposing clinical associations across time windows directly resolved the cross-sectional interferon paradox (**Fig. 7g**, top, and **Supplementary Fig. 10a**). Under conventional patient-mean peak WHO correlation, all major T compartments appeared positively associated with peak severity ($\rho = +0.29\text{ to }+0.33, q_{13} = 0.037$). However, stratifying longitudinal draws revealed that this positive signal is confined strictly to the **early acute viral phase ($\le 7\text{ d}$ post-swab: `GEE +wk` $\beta = +0.22\text{ to }+0.25, P = 0.014\text{–}0.024$)**, whereas adjusting across all 213–217 draws for time since first positive swab (`SD-WHO GEE +wk`) drove severity slopes to exact zero across all 13 T-cell subsets ($\beta = -0.04\text{ to }+0.03, P = 0.59\text{–}0.96, q_{13} = 0.96$), demonstrating that static patient-mean ISG–severity correlations are an artefact of earlier sampling among rapidly deteriorating patients. To confirm biological specificity to viral infection, we scored the exact same 19-gene T-cell ISG module (all `19/19` genes present) in our bacterial sepsis cohort (**Fig. 7g**, bottom, and **Supplementary Fig. 10b**, bottom): in stark contrast to COVID-19 induction (`log2FC = +0.29 to +2.17` in `PBISC` and `+0.47 to +2.33` in `Real` across all 19 ISGs; `+0.54 to +2.33` across the eight canonical ISGs in **Supplementary Fig. 10b**), PBISC bacterial sepsis T-cell ISG **declined monotonically** across `Adult HC` (median `0.479`) → `ICU` (`0.413`) → `Sepsis` (`0.422`; Spearman $\rho = -0.388, P = 1.10 \times 10^{-5}$; Sepsis versus HC $P = 1.43 \times 10^{-6}$), matching observed `Real` sepsis downregulation of `IFI44L`, `IFIT1`, `STAT1` and `OAS2` (whereas `scSemiProfiler` erroneously inverted sign to positive in sepsis).

Linking T-cell ISG state back to cellular abundance uncovered a previously unrecognized **baseline cytotoxic-loss versus hyper-interferon anti-correlation** (**Fig. 7h**). Across all `213` draws with $\ge 5$ `T CD8 CTL` cells ($\rho = -0.261, P = 1.14 \times 10^{-4}$) and most prominently across the **`n = 63` exact canonical donor first blood draws with $\ge 5$ `T CD8 CTL` cells (`11` deaths; zero draw-2 substitution, excluding only 3 surviving patients [`C113`, `C129`, `C140`] whose earliest draw had `<5` CTL cells)**: $\rho = -0.409, P = 8.7 \times 10^{-4}$ (sensitivity across all `N = 66` first ISG-evaluable draws: $\rho = -0.396, P = 0.0010$), patients with depleted `T CD8 CTL` fractions simultaneously exhibited heightened cell-intrinsic `T CD8 CTL ISG` activation—concentrating fatal cases in the **low-CTL / high-ISG** quadrant (`exact first-draw T CD8 CTL ISG AUC = 0.685, P = 0.056`; `Real` uniform d2–d4 first draws `AUC = 0.794, P = 0.036, adjWHO P = 0.046`). Combining baseline cytotoxic loss and interferon activation into a simple tri-axis score (`−(T CD8 CTL + NK) + T CD8 CTL ISG`) achieved unadjusted first-draw mortality separation of **`AUC = 0.841` ($P = 4.3 \times 10^{-4}$)** on exact first draws (`n = 63`; `0.828, P = 6.6 × 10⁻⁴` in the `N = 66` first ISG-evaluable sensitivity, compared with `0.787` for `−CTL` alone and `0.823` for `−(CTL + NK)` across all `66` exact first draws). Longitudinally across all 213–217 acute draws, fatal patients who started with elevated early T-cell ISG underwent a **steep `Time × Fatal` interferon collapse** across all five major T compartments (**Fig. 7i**): `T CD8 CTL ISG` fell at $\beta = -0.55\text{ s.d. week}^{-1}$ ($P = 3.7 \times 10^{-6}$, replicating identically in `Real` [$-0.53, P = 9.1 \times 10^{-5}$] and `PBISC new` [$-0.51, P = 0.016$]; source-adjusted sensitivity $-0.53\text{ week}^{-1}, P = 7.7 \times 10^{-6}$), with parallel collapse in `T CD4 CTL` ($-0.64\text{ week}^{-1}, P = 2.1 \times 10^{-8}$), `All CD8 T` ($-0.69\text{ week}^{-1}, P = 7.1 \times 10^{-8}$), `All CD4 T` ($-0.67\text{ week}^{-1}, P = 9.5 \times 10^{-11}$) and `All T cells` ($-0.67\text{ week}^{-1}, P = 2.5 \times 10^{-10}$). Finally, in leak-free in-fold `rank-INT` `50 × 5-fold` stratified cross-validation strictly on exact canonical first blood draws (`Age + Sex + same-day WHO` baseline `AUC = 0.688` for `N = 66` and `0.694` for `n = 63`; **Fig. 7j**), adding **first-draw `T CD8 CTL ISG`** alone (`n = 63`) improved out-of-fold mortality `AUC` to **`0.763` ($\Delta\text{AUC} = +0.069$)** (`+CTL + ISG`: **`0.858`**, $\Delta\text{AUC} = +0.164$), adding **`T CD8 CTL`** (`N = 66`) reached **`0.826` ($\Delta\text{AUC} = +0.139$)**, adding **`T CD8 CTL + NK`** (`N = 66`) reached **`0.827` ($\Delta\text{AUC} = +0.139$)**, and restricting to all `53` early first draws $\le 7\text{ d}$ (`10` deaths) lifted `+T CD8 CTL` from `0.647` to **`0.777` ($\Delta\text{AUC} = +0.130$)**, whereas adding late-peaking `HSPC %` within `WHO 2–3` yielded only $\Delta\text{AUC} = +0.01$. All primary findings were fully corroborated by the 14-variant patient-mean sensitivity audit (**Supplementary Figs. 9f and 11**).

## Discussion (Figures 4–7 Section)

A central bottleneck in clinical single-cell genomics is the gap between cohort-scale bulk transcriptomics and small, logistically constrained scRNA-seq subcohorts. Across two distinct acute inflammatory syndromes—bacterial sepsis¹ and ESKD COVID-19²,³—our findings show that adapting pretrained PBISC on just five paired support donors per cohort reconstructs held-out same-donor single-cell repertoires directly in unintegrated PCA space (`PRE-PCA iLISI` = 1.44–1.51 versus 1.76–1.77 observed split-cell reference, versus 1.00 for `scSemiProfiler`) and recovers paired cell-type-resolved `limma-voom` disease programs with 3- to 12-fold higher DEG recall than semi-profiling (**Fig. 4**), generalizes across unpaired bulk-only patients (`K = 4,000` cells per sample) to preserve cell-type-resolved disease DEG programs near the empirical split-half ceiling (`AUROC` = 0.78–0.99; **Fig. 5**), and faithfully recovers low-abundance rare immune populations and cell-intrinsic single-cell transcriptional states across both bacterial sepsis and COVID-19 (**Fig. 6**). Directly benchmarking PBISC against semi-profiling and 10 official R 4.3.2 bulk deconvolution implementations clarifies why generative single-cell synthesis is uniquely required for rare-cell biology: `scSemiProfiler`⁴ collapsed library sizes onto a narrow Poisson spike and eliminated 50–62% of rare states (`Mono_c06-CD14-MKI67`, `B_ASC_IgG`, `CD14mono_dex`, cycling T/NK cells and `HSPC`), `NNLS` collapsed `T CD8 CTL`, `B ASC IgG` and `HSPC` to `0%`, and `BayesPrism`⁶ v2.2.3 (`37–43×`), `Calib-NNLS` (`31×`), `DWLS`¹⁰ (`13×`) and `BisqueRNA`⁷ (`11×`) inflated ultra-rare `HSPC` abundance by absorbing collinear myeloid/progenitor bulk transcripts while trailing PBISC on both `T CD8 CTL` abundance ($\rho = +0.55$ versus $+0.15\text{–}0.46$ across `MuSiC`⁹/`BayesPrism`⁶/`BisqueRNA`⁷/`DWLS`¹⁰/`CIBERSORTx`⁸) and `T CD8 CTL ISG` state scoring ($\rho = +0.81$ versus $+0.74$; **Fig. 6j,k** and **Supplementary Fig. 10b**). Moreover, PBISC uniquely reproduced the fine-grained `GATA2↓ → GATA1/KLF1/APOC1/ITGA2B/TYMS↑` erythroid switch across `3,124` single circulating `HSPC`s (**Fig. 6l**) where raw bulk `CD34`/`SPINK2`/`PROM1`/`AVP` transcripts failed completely.

Translating bulk-to-single-cell cohort expansion into rigorous clinical discovery (**Fig. 7**) hinges on three methodological principles audited directly here:
1. **Exact 1:1 patient-to-dataset metadata auditing and longitudinal pairing**: Forensic reconciliation across `255` bacterial sepsis samples (`0` overlap between `Real` and `PBISC`, `119/122` molecular sex concordant) and `107` ESKD COVID-19 donors (`362` 1:1 mapped samples, `0` shared demographic/WHO/death mismatches, `163/164` sex concordant, `−2 CLL`, `TRUE_PUB_11`; **Fig. 7a** and **Supplementary Figs. 6–8**) prevents silent label contamination, while pairing repeated draws to contemporaneous same-day WHO severity and anchoring mortality strictly to each donor's first blood draw removes both within-patient severity dilution (`41.9%` same-day ≠ Peak WHO) and threefold fatal sampling-frequency bias (`4.0` versus `1.3` draws/patient).
2. **Population number versus percentage invariance—and the need for within-source normalization**: Auditing cell percentages (`% PBMC` and `% parent`) against both raw single-cell counts and clinical hospital CBC absolute counts (`cells/µL`) across `43` COVID-19 and `44` sepsis subsets proves that clinical associations are biologically invariant to number versus percentage representation ($r = 0.985\text{–}0.993$ across endpoints and diseases, independently confirmed in `Real` clinical `CBC cells/µL`; **Fig. 7c** and **Supplementary Fig. 9a–d**). Simultaneously, it highlights a practical pitfall: because real droplet scRNA-seq cell recovery fluctuates `40×` (`226–9,254` cells) whereas synthetic cohorts generate fixed cell counts (`4,000`), naively pooling raw cell counts degrades prognostic discrimination (`T CD8 CTL AUC 0.213 → 0.311`), establishing within-source `% PBMC rank-INT` as the mathematically principled primary scale.
3. **Separating conserved hallmarks, reconciled COVID-19 controversies and newly rescued ESKD single-cell discoveries**: Positioning our expanded ESKD findings against prior COVID-19 immunology clarifies what serves as external biological validation versus what generative cohort expansion uniquely unlocks. First, reproducing established COVID-19 hallmarks—including class-switched/dividing `B ASC` expansion¹¹⁻¹⁴ (coupled within patients to same-day ferritin and `D-dimer`), early pan-leukocyte `ISG` induction and Wave-2 glucocorticoid-responsive `CD14mono_dex`³—provides stringent positive benchmarks in scRNA-naive bulk patients. Second, combining `217`-draw longitudinal pairing with cross-metric and cross-disease contrasts resolves three prominent controversies in prior COVID-19 literature: (i) seemingly contradictory reports of higher relative `HSPC % PBMC` in non-ESKD scRNA-seq atlases¹²⁻¹⁴ versus lower admission absolute `CD34+ cells/µL` in clinical flow-cytometry cohorts²⁰,²¹ are reconciled by temporal dissociation—flat `HSPC %` during profound early lymphopenia at `Draw 1` (`AUC = 0.58`) followed by week-2 stress-haematopoiesis egress²²⁻²⁴ at `>7 d` (`AUC = 0.82`); (ii) selective `NK CD56bright` loss within a preserved total NK pool distinguishes COVID-19¹⁷⁻¹⁹ from the indiscriminate pan-NK collapse seen across both non-septic ICU controls and bacterial sepsis¹; and (iii) opposing cross-sectional claims of impaired²⁵,²⁶ versus heightened²⁷⁻²⁹ type-I IFN activity in severe COVID-19 reflect acute $\le 7\text{ d}$ viral activation confounded by sampling timing (`SD-WHO GEE +wk` $\beta \approx 0$, versus monotonic suppression in bacterial sepsis, $\rho = -0.39$). Third, unlocking `+29` scRNA-naive Wave-1 bulk patients rescues **`12` FDR-significant rare and fine-grained associations** (`HSPC`, `NK CD56bright`, `T g/d`, `MAIT`, `NKT`, `CD16mono_C1`, `T CD4 cycling`, `T CD4 CTL`, `CD14mono`, `CD16mono`, and severity-independent first-draw `T CD8 CTL` and `NK` mortality predictors alongside atypical `B_CD11c`¹⁶ and `CD14mono_IFN`) that could not pass multiple-testing correction in the 37-patient ESKD scRNA-seq cohort³ alone, while uncovering two cell-intrinsic single-cell signatures inaccessible to bulk markers: pre-surge `Draw 1` `HSPC Alarmin_Inflam` (`adjWHO P = 0.022`) and `TFRC`/`GATA1`/`KLF1`/`TYMS` priming, and exact `Draw 1` `T CD8 CTL` depletion coupled inversely to `T CD8 CTL ISG` hyperactivation ($n = 63$: $\rho = -0.409, P = 8.7 \times 10^{-4}$; tri-axis `AUC = 0.841`; leak-free `50×5 CV` `+CTL + ISG AUC = 0.858, ΔAUC = +0.164`) prior to catastrophic five-compartment `Time × Fatal` interferon collapse ($\beta = -0.55\text{ to }-0.69\text{ s.d. week}^{-1}$).

Several scope boundaries warrant explicit emphasis. First, statistical power gains in PBISC cohort expansion originate from real measured bulk transcriptomes of additional clinical donors decomposed through cohort-adapted generative priors (`57–82` paired anchor donors); synthesizing `K = 4,000` cells per sample stabilizes Monte Carlo estimation of rare subsets without increasing biological sample size³⁰, and donor-level inference must remain strictly patient-clustered. Second, reproducible between-modality scale offsets across rare subsets (`0.3×`–`9.4×`; **Fig. 6i**) and imperfect individual-visit concordance for ultra-sparse progenitor states mandate within-source `rank-INT` rather than uncalibrated raw pooling. Third, pharmacological perturbation states (`CD14mono_dex`), incidental clonal haematological disorders (`C116`, `C139`) and sample-level sex QC (`Res-S34`, `C137_positive_2`) underscore the necessity of molecular metadata auditing across recruitment waves. Finally, while early intracellular `HSPC` alarmin/erythroid priming and `CTL–ISG` coupling nominate concrete cellular hypotheses in high-risk ESKD patients, establishing direct causal links to renal anaemia, erythropoietin responsiveness or bone-marrow egress—and deploying these signatures as standalone clinical prognostic assays—will require paired marrow/reticulocyte profiling and prospective external validation. Within these principled boundaries, PBISC establishes generative bulk-to-single-cell synthesis as an auditable framework for unlocking rare-cell transcriptomics and longitudinal clinical discovery from archival and prospective bulk cohorts.

## Methods (Figures 4–7 Section)

### Clinical cohorts, 1:1 metadata matching and molecular sex-QC auditing
**Bacterial sepsis cohort (`Ye et al., 2026`).** Paired whole-blood bulk RNA-seq (`GSE279448`) and PBMC scRNA-seq (`GSE279452`) from Ye et al.¹ (`255` total cross-sectional single-draw samples) were audited 1:1 against GEO sample metadata (`geo_characteristics.csv` and published sample tables; **Supplementary Fig. 6a** and `A1_sepsis_255_samples_matched_metadata.csv`). Observed `Real` scRNA-seq comprised `133` total donors: `108` adult donors (`93` sepsis, `8` non-septic ICU controls, `7` healthy controls; median age 67 [IQR 54–74] yr, 76/108 [70.4%] male; median `5,664` [4,727–6,846] cells/sample; `633,072` adult cells across `37,474` features, of which `5,967` mapped uniquely to the 6,000-gene PBISC panel) plus `25` paediatric donors (`17` sepsis, `4` ICU, `4` HC; median age 5 [1–7] yr, 18/25 [72.0%] male; median `5,822` [5,204–6,482] cells/sample). After excluding 3 QC-failed bulk libraries from `207` bulk RNA-seq samples, `82` paired anchor donors were used for five-fold donor-blocked LoRA adaptation and all `122` unpaired bulk-only donors (`121` primary adults: `68` sepsis, `17` ICU, `36` HC; median age 65 [54–74] yr, 78/121 [64.5%] male; plus `1` paediatric ICU control `PIC06`) were synthesized at `K = 4,000` cells/sample (`0` donor overlap with `Real` scRNA-seq). Molecular sex verification comparing GEO sex labels against bulk Y/X-chromosome expression (`sex_flag`) confirmed `119/122` matches (`2` ambiguous healthy controls `HC23`/`HC30`; `1` discordant sample `Res-S34`, documented in **Supplementary Fig. 6b**).

**Imperial College ESKD COVID-19 cohort (`Gisby et al., 2022`; `Stephenson et al., 2025`).** Longitudinal blood bulk RNA-seq (`Zenodo 6497251` / `E-MTAB-11536`, `336` samples, `15` excluded) and PBMC scRNA-seq (`187` published samples, `578,835` cells across `33,559` features; `5,791` mapped to the 6,000-gene PBISC panel) were integrated across Wave 1 and Wave 2²,³. Exact 1:1 sample linkage across all `107` ESKD donors (`68` acute COVID-19-positive, `39` COVID-19-negative single-draw controls) mapped all `362` processed rows (`172` exact 0-day paired `Real bulk + Real scRNA` samples, `164` exact `Real bulk → PBISC` synthesized samples and `26` `Real scRNA-only` visits), verifying **zero demographic, WHO or fatal-outcome mismatches** across all `32` shared donors and **`163/164` (99.4%) molecular sex concordance** (`1` ambiguous non-primary Shared-27 draw `C137_positive_2`, `0` swaps). Five-fold donor-blocked LoRA adaptation on `57` paired donors (`157` visits) generated `656,000` cells across all `164` bulk-only samples. Two pre-specified clinical audits excluded two incidental monoclonal B-CLL patients (`C116`, `C139`; bulk `ROR1 > 2.5`, `FMOD > 1.0`) and resolved three miscoded Wave-2 survivors (`C101`, `C146`, `C147`) against Stephenson et al.³ Table S1 and two-month convalescent visits, yielding `11` verified acute deaths (`TRUE_PUB_11`, including `9` direct deaths `DIRECT_9`). Primary acute scope required acute follow-up (`0–30` days from first positive swab) and $\ge 200$ QC-passing cells (`2` low-cell `Real` draws excluded: `C73_2404` [179 cells], `C168_2601` [4 cells]), yielding exactly `217` acute draws across `66` unique patients (`138` `Real` draws across `37` patients [`10` Real-only + `27` `Shared-27` counted once in `Real`; `59` complementary-visit `PBISC` draws on `Shared-27` reserved for **Fig. 6i–k**], `5` deaths; and `79` `PBISC new` draws across `29` Wave-1 scRNA-naive patients, `6` deaths).

### Paired five-donor LoRA reconstruction, `limma-voom` disease recovery and unpaired cohort DEG evaluation (Figs. 4–5)
For the paired within-donor benchmark (**Fig. 4** and full-manuscript `Supplementary Figs. 8–13`), the pretrained `6,000`-gene, `1×` PBISC model was adapted via LoRA on five paired support donors per reference selection across three deterministic fixed selections per cohort: `Sepsis D0/D1/D3` (`4` adult sepsis + `1` healthy support donor: `HC07`, `HC12` or `PHC05`, clinically excluding ICU supports `D2: PIC01` and `D4: IC23`; `77` held-out paired Sepsis donors per reference) and `COVID-19 D0/D1/D4` (`3` COVID-19-positive + `2` COVID-19-negative ESKD support donors: `C106/C237`, `C142/C164` or `C152/C137`; `52` held-out paired COVID-19 donors across `142–143` visits, excluding all longitudinal visits of support donors). For each held-out bulk sample, `4,000` single cells were generated (`×1` bulk inputs, support-gated ceiling counts) and compared with `scSemiProfiler`⁴ (using the exact same five support donors and held-out bulks under its recorded Poisson readout) and with observed `Real` cells of the same donors across donor-balanced 101-rank `log1p` gene profiles (`5,975` Sepsis and `5,927` COVID-19 shared genes), donor-balanced UMI depth and six-type compositions, and matched source-mixing `iLISI` in the unintegrated 50-dimensional three-source shared `PRE-PCA` space (two equal disjoint observed halves of $\le 75$ cells per side per visit, averaging three query-seed medians per reference with no post-Harmony integration). Paired disease differential expression across `T`, `B`, `Monocyte` and `NK` compartments was tested using identical cell-type-specific visit masks and observed-defined gene families via `TMM` normalization and `limma-voom` moderated tests accounting for repeated donor–condition observations (`BH`-adjusted $P < 0.05$, no fold-change cutoff; sepsis versus healthy controls excluding ICU; COVID-19-positive versus negative ESKD excluding recovery), evaluating paired `logFC r`, `DEG recall`, `DEG precision`, `DEG F1`, and `12`-gene `Real`-scaled `ln(1 + CP10K)` donor–condition heatmaps. For unpaired bulk-only cohort expansion (**Fig. 5**; `NewLora` in sepsis and `20261104 New reference dataset` in COVID-19, generating `K = 4,000` ceiling-UMI cells across `122` sepsis and `164` COVID-19 bulk-only samples versus `51,000`/`34` and `60,000`/`102` `scSemiProfiler` samples), `Real` donors were split into `Real A` (reference) and `Real B` (replicate ceiling), normalized (`target_sum = 10,000`), $\log(1 + x)$-transformed, restricted to 2,000 `Real A` HVGs, projected onto 30 PCs and Harmony-integrated⁵ (`batch_key = source`, weighted 30-NN label transfer). Unpaired lineage-resolved pseudobulk DEGs (`T`, `B`, `NK`, `Monocyte`; $\ge 10$ cells/donor; $\log_2(\text{CPM} + 1)$) were tested across `5,664` (sepsis) and `5,610` (COVID-19) genes (`BH` $q < 0.05$, $|\log_2\text{FC}| > 1$), evaluating `Real`-DEG `AUROC` versus 50-split donor split-half ceilings, reliability-corrected slopes ($\hat{\beta}_{\text{corr}} = \hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}} / R_{\text{Real}}$) and double-disattenuated correlations $r / \sqrt{R_{\text{Real}} R_{\text{gen}}}$.

### Rare-population specificity, official R 4.3.2 deconvolution benchmark and single-cell `HSPC` 20-gene profiling (Fig. 6)
Across four focal rare states per cohort (`B_c07-MZB1`, `Macrophage`, `T_CD4_c07-MKI67`, `Mono_c06-CD14-MKI67` in sepsis; `B_ASC_IgG`, `CD14mono_dex`, `pDC`, `HSPC` in COVID-19), parent-lineage-subtracted specificity vectors ($\Delta \mu_{s,g} = \mu_{s,g} - \mu_{\text{parent}(s),g}$) across all 2,000 `Real A` HVGs were correlated between `Real`, `PBISC` and `scSemiProfiler`, and pairwise sample-level correlations (`Real–Real`, `Real–PBISC`, cross-state) were evaluated across the top signature HVG union (`119` sepsis / `131` COVID-19 genes). Patient-level abundances were compared via two-sided centered two-sample patient-block bootstrap (`49,999` resamples, BH-adjusted across 10 states/cohort). For the official R 4.3.2 deconvolution benchmark on `Shared-27` ($n = 27$ donors, two donor-disjoint folds, `43` fine cell types), native R packages (`BayesPrism`⁶ v2.2.3 initial/updated posteriors + expression tensors $Z$, `BisqueRNA`⁷ v1.0.5, `CIBERSORTx`⁸ `B-mode`/`S-mode` ComBat via `sva` v3.50.0, `CIBERSORT-R` $\nu$-SVR, `MuSiC`⁹ v1.0.0, `DWLS`¹⁰ v0.1.0, `nnls` v1.6 and calibrated `NNLS`) were evaluated via Spearman $\rho$ against paired `Real` scRNA-seq proportions and median `HSPC %` versus observed `Real` (`0.13%`). Across `3,124` single `HSPC`s (`149` draws $\ge 3$ `HSPC`s across `61` patients; `5,762` shared genes, $\log(1 + \text{CP10k})$), `16` lineage/stress modules (`BH16`) and `20` canonical genes (`BH20`) were evaluated under the primary same-day WHO GEE (`+wk`) and first `HSPC`-positive draw mortality framework.

### Primary longitudinal pairing, population number versus percentage auditing and deep T-cell ISG analysis (Fig. 7)
To remove systematic between-modality scale offsets (**Fig. 6i**) without touching clinical outcomes, every cellular abundance or single-cell transcriptional score was transformed separately within `Real` and within `PBISC new` via Van der Waerden rank-based inverse normal transformation ($z_i = \Phi^{-1}((\text{rank}(x_i) - 0.5)/n_s)$). Across all `43` fine-grained subsets ($\ge 30\%$ `Real` detection):
1. **Primary contemporaneous severity (`217` acute draws × same-day WHO)**: Exchangeable patient-clustered GEE (`statsmodels`, Huber–White and Mancl–DeRouen bias-reduced covariance) adjusted for weeks since first positive swab (`z ~ WHO_day + weeks`, `BH43` $q_{43} < 0.05$), Mundlak within–between decomposition (`z ~ (WHO_day − mean_WHO_i) + mean_WHO_i + weeks`) and `3,000`-resample patient-block cluster-bootstrap Spearman $\rho$.
2. **Primary baseline mortality (`66` donor first blood draws × `TRUE_PUB_11`)**: Exact two-sided Mann–Whitney `AUC` on each donor's earliest acute draw (`BH43` $q_{43} < 0.10$; `3,000` stratified patient bootstrap CIs), with same-day WHO residualization (`adjWHO`, fitted via categorical 4-level `C(WHO)` in primary `T2_ALL` and cross-checked against linear `WHO` in `A2`, yielding concordant $P \approx 0.03$ for both `T CD8 CTL` and `NK`), early first draws $\le 7\text{ d}$ (`n = 53`), `DIRECT_9` (`9` deaths), `WHO 2–3` (`n = 29`) and late acute draws (`>7 d`, `n = 50`). Power rescue (**Fig. 7d**) ran `300` mortality-stratified subsamples ($n = 20$ `Real` patients alone, `+29 PBISC new` or `+29` outcome-shuffled controls).
3. **Population number versus percentage and clinical CBC audit**: Every primary severity (`217`-draw `GEE +wk`) and first-draw mortality (`66`-pt `AUC`) model was re-executed identically across five scale definitions (**Fig. 7c** and **Supplementary Fig. 9a–d**): (i) within-source `% PBMC rank-INT` (primary), (ii) within-source `Raw Cell Count rank-INT`, (iii) `log1p(Raw Count)` GEE / naively pooled raw counts, (iv) within-source `% Parent Lineage rank-INT`, and (v) **true clinical absolute counts (`cells/µL`)** in `Real` computed by multiplying scRNA subset shares by same-day clinical hospital CBC absolute lymphocyte counts (for T, B and NK subsets; `n = 36` first draws / `89` total draws) or monocyte/WBC counts (for myeloid/HSPC subsets; `n = 19–20` first draws). Sequencing-depth confounding (`n_cells`) was tested directly in `Real` (`138` draws), and count-versus-percentage concordance was independently audited across `44` fine-grained adult bacterial sepsis subsets (`HC → ICU → Sepsis`).
4. **Deep 19-gene T-cell `ISG` analysis and leak-free `50 × 5` CV**: Across all `13` T-cell subsets (`≥5` cells/draw; `213–217` acute draws), the 19-gene type-I IFN module (`ISG15`, `IFI6`, `IFI44`, `IFI44L`, `IFIT1`, `IFIT2`, `IFIT3`, `MX1`, `MX2`, `OAS1`, `OAS2`, `OAS3`, `RSAD2`, `XAF1`, `STAT1`, `IRF7`, `EPSTI1`, `HERC5`, `SIGLEC1`) was audited across `Shared-27` (against official `BayesPrism`⁶ $Z$), patient-mean peak WHO, early $\le 7\text{ d}$ `GEE +wk`, all-draw `SD-WHO GEE +wk`, donor first-draw `AUC`, baseline `T CD8 CTL` abundance–`ISG` coupling strictly on exact draw 1 with $\ge 5$ CTL cells (`n = 63, 11` deaths, zero draw-2 substitution; with `N = 66` first ISG-evaluable draw sensitivity), longitudinal `Time × Fatal` exchangeable GEE on within-source `rank-INT` `isg_z` (`Fatal + Weeks + Fatal × Weeks`; source-adjusted sensitivity `Fatal + Weeks + Fatal × Weeks + Source` $\beta = -0.535, P = 7.7 \times 10^{-6}$), and cross-disease bacterial sepsis (`121` PBISC primary adults + `Real`/`PBISC`/`scSemiProfiler` 19-ISG T-cell DEG `log2FC`). Same-day hospital laboratory GEE (`BH36`) and four-cohort external meta-analysis¹¹,¹³⁻¹⁵ ($N = 220$, DerSimonian–Laird + modified Knapp–Hartung) followed pre-specified protocols. Leak-free `50 × 5-fold` stratified first-draw mortality CV (**Fig. 7j**) fitted within-source `rank-INT` and `StandardScaler` strictly inside each training fold before `L2`-penalized logistic regression (`C = 1.0`, `Age + Sex + SD-WHO` baseline versus exact canonical draw-1 `+T CD8 CTL` [`N = 66`], `+CTL + NK` [`N = 66`], `≤7 d +T CD8 CTL` [`n = 53`], `+T CD8 CTL ISG` [`n = 63`] and `+CTL + ISG` [`n = 63`]).

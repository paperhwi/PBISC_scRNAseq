## Results

### PBISC synthesizes realistic single-cell transcriptomes for clinical patients profiled only by bulk RNA-seq

In prospective hospital cohorts, whole-blood or peripheral blood mononuclear cell (PBMC) bulk RNA-seq is routinely collected across large patient waves, whereas paired single-cell RNA-seq (scRNA-seq) is restricted to smaller subsets owing to cost, cell viability and logistical constraints. To test whether PBISC can convert unpaired clinical bulk RNA-seq into analysis-ready scRNA-seq datasets, we evaluated two independent clinical cohorts containing both paired bulk–scRNA anchor donors and held-out clinical patients profiled exclusively by bulk RNA-seq (**Fig. 4a** and **Supplementary Figs. 1a–d and 6a**). In the bacterial sepsis cohort of Ye et al.¹ (`GSE279448` bulk and `GSE279452` scRNA-seq; 207 bulk samples, 3 excluded during quality control; strictly single cross-sectional blood draw per donor), 82 paired donors were used for five-fold out-of-fold low-rank adaptation (LoRA) of pretrained PBISC (held-out donor five-fold L1 composition error = 0.36 versus permutation null = 0.53), after which the cohort-adapted ensemble synthesized 4,000 discrete single-cell count profiles per sample across all 122 unpaired bulk-only patients (`121` primary adults [68 sepsis cases, 17 non-septic intensive care unit (ICU) controls and 36 healthy controls (HC)] plus `1` paediatric ICU control `PIC06`; 488,000 generated cells across a 6,000-gene panel, compared with 633,072 observed adult scRNA-seq cells across 108 adult donors [93 sepsis, 8 ICU and 7 HC] and 25 paediatric scRNA-seq donors [17 sepsis, 4 ICU and 4 HC], with zero donor overlap between the `Real` scRNA-seq and `PBISC` bulk-only cohorts). In the Imperial College end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) COVID-19 cohort²,³ (336 bulk samples, 15 excluded), five-fold LoRA adaptation across 57 paired donors (157 longitudinal visits; five-fold L1 = 0.36 versus null = 0.52) enabled de novo synthesis of 656,000 single-cell profiles (`K = 4,000` cells per sample) for 164 unpaired bulk-only samples (143 acute COVID-19-positive draws, 19 COVID-19-negative draws and 2 convalescent draws, compared with 578,835 observed scRNA-seq cells across 187 published samples). Across both cohorts, PBISC reproduced single-cell unique molecular identifier (UMI) sequencing depth distributions and empirical cumulative distribution functions without zero-count cell artifacts (sepsis median panel UMI per cell: PBISC 2,121 versus Real 2,042; COVID-19: PBISC 1,592 versus Real 1,338), whereas the semi-profiling baseline `scSemiProfiler`⁴ collapsed library sizes into an artificial unimodal Poisson spike around ~1,901 UMIs in sepsis (51,000 cells, 34 samples) and ~1,439 UMIs in COVID-19 (60,000 cells, 102 samples) (**Supplementary Fig. 1e–h**).

Across more than four orders of magnitude of expression dynamic range, PBISC-generated count matrices for bulk-only patients closely tracked observed scRNA-seq gene-mean UMI profiles, achieving $\log_{10}$ gene-mean Pearson $r = 0.98$ across 5,441 expressed genes in sepsis and $r = 0.97$ across 5,228 genes in COVID-19 (**Fig. 4b,c**). By contrast, `scSemiProfiler` exhibited severe dropout and systematic downward compression across medium- and low-abundance transcripts ($r = 0.76$ across 2,706 detected genes in sepsis and $r = 0.74$ across 2,452 genes in COVID-19). When projected into a shared transcriptional manifold alongside held-out observed donor split `Real A` using 2,000 highly variable genes (HVGs), PBISC cells from unpaired bulk-only patients co-embedded seamlessly across all six major PBMC lineages—T cells, natural killer (NK) cells, B cells, monocytes, dendritic cells (DCs) and other circulating cells (**Fig. 4d–g**). Quantitative local inverse Simpson's index (`iLISI`) evaluation confirmed strong manifold overlap even prior to batch integration (PCA pre-Harmony median `iLISI` = 1.48 in sepsis and 1.43 in COVID-19) and near-ceiling mixing after Harmony⁵ integration (`iLISI` = 1.57 versus the `Real A`/`Real B` biological replicate ceiling of 1.68 in sepsis; `iLISI` = 1.60 versus the `Real A`/`Real B` ceiling of 1.65 in COVID-19), whereas `scSemiProfiler` remained completely segregated from real single-cell manifolds (`iLISI` = 1.00 before and after Harmony in both cohorts; **Fig. 4h,i**).

At the cohort level, PBISC preserved six-lineage PBMC stoichiometry in both sepsis (54% T, 12% NK, 12% B and 15% monocyte versus 55%, 10%, 13% and 17% in `Real`) and COVID-19 (56% T, 9% NK, 17% B and 15% monocyte versus 53%, 16%, 10% and 17% in `Real`), avoiding the lineage distortions observed with `scSemiProfiler` (69% T cells in sepsis and 26% monocytes with 3% B cells in COVID-19; **Fig. 4j,k**). Because whole-blood transcriptional differences between cases and controls combine shifts in leukocyte proportions with cell-intrinsic gene regulation, we applied a Kitagawa decomposition across 3,188 expressed genes in sepsis and 3,175 genes in COVID-19 to partition total pseudobulk disease $\log_2$ fold changes into compositional ($\Delta$ share) and within-lineage ($\Delta$ expression) components, adjusting regression slopes for split-half measurement reliability in `Real` (**Fig. 4l,m**). In sepsis, PBISC accurately recovered total ($r = 0.81$, reliability-corrected slope = 0.91), compositional ($r = 0.84$, slope = 1.41) and within-lineage ($r = 0.76$, slope = 0.74) disease shifts, substantially outperforming `scSemiProfiler` ($r = 0.45$, 0.23 and 0.45; slopes = 0.73, 0.33 and 0.82). Concordant gains held in COVID-19 ($r = 0.61$, 0.53 and 0.64; slopes = 0.79, 0.97 and 0.70 for PBISC versus $r = 0.34$, 0.14 and 0.33 for `scSemiProfiler`). Consistently, PBISC recapitulated hallmark case–control lineage shifts—including T- and NK-cell lymphopenia alongside B-cell and monocyte expansion in sepsis, and T-cell depletion with B-cell expansion in COVID-19—whereas `scSemiProfiler` inverted the sign of T-cell and monocyte shifts in sepsis and B-cell and monocyte shifts in COVID-19 (**Fig. 4n,o**).

### Preservation of lineage-resolved disease differentially expressed genes across sepsis and COVID-19

We next tested whether PBISC-generated single-cell profiles from bulk-only patients faithfully recover both whole-PBMC and cell-type-resolved case-versus-control differentially expressed genes (DEGs; Benjamini–Hochberg `BH` $q < 0.05$, $|\log_2\text{FC}| > 1$) across 5,664 expressed panel genes in sepsis and 5,610 genes in COVID-19 (**Fig. 5** and **Supplementary Figs. 4 and 5**). Aggregated across whole PBMCs, PBISC pseudobulk fold changes matched independent cohort bulk RNA-seq benchmarks at the same fidelity as observed scRNA-seq: against `GSE279448` in bacterial sepsis (`4,157` shared genes), PBISC achieved reliability-corrected slope = 0.58 ($r = 0.70$) versus `Real` slope = 0.59 ($r = 0.70$) and `scSemiProfiler` slope = 0.16 ($r = 0.15$; **Fig. 5a** and **Supplementary Fig. 4a**); against `Zenodo 6497251` in ESKD COVID-19 (`4,067` shared genes), PBISC achieved slope = 0.79 ($r = 0.64$) versus `Real` slope = 0.59 ($r = 0.66$) and `scSemiProfiler` slope = 0.76 ($r = 0.37$; **Fig. 5b** and **Supplementary Fig. 5a**).

Within individual major lineages (`T`, `B`, `NK` and `Monocyte`), PBISC ranked true `Real` DEGs near the empirical `Real` split-half reliability ceiling across both acute inflammatory syndromes (**Fig. 5c–f**). In bacterial sepsis, PBISC achieved `Real`-DEG ranking `AUROC`s of 0.96 (`T`), 0.86 (`B`), 0.83 (`NK`) and 0.78 (`Monocyte`)—closely tracking the empirical `Real` split-half ceiling (0.94, 0.89, 0.86 and 0.91)—with 83–99% directional sign agreement on `Real` DEGs, double-disattenuated correlations $r = 0.81, 0.75, 0.66$ and $0.66$, and reliability-corrected effect-size slopes of 1.10, 1.11, 1.07 and 0.71 (**Fig. 5c,e** and **Supplementary Fig. 4b**). In ESKD COVID-19, PBISC achieved `AUROC`s of 0.99 (`T`), 0.87 (`B`), 0.85 (`NK`) and 0.88 (`Monocyte`) against split-half ceilings of 0.99, 0.89, 0.97 and 0.94 (sign agreement 93–100%; disattenuated $r = 0.77, 0.83, 0.58$ and $0.77$; corrected slopes = 0.81, 1.78, 0.76 and 0.79; **Fig. 5d,f** and **Supplementary Fig. 5b**). By contrast, `scSemiProfiler` suffered severe loss of both ranking and calibration in sepsis (`AUROC` = 0.56–0.65, slopes = 0.15–0.31, calling zero `NK` DEGs) and collapsed B-cell regulation in COVID-19 (`AUROC` = 0.68, disattenuated $r = 0.09$, slope = 0.09, 4 DEGs).

Inspecting lineage-specific volcano plots confirmed faithful preservation of canonical disease biology across all four lineages (**Fig. 5g,h** and **Supplementary Figs. 4c–f and 5c–f**). In sepsis monocytes (**Fig. 5g**), PBISC (`463` DEGs across 60 sepsis / 34 control pseudobulks) mirrored `Real` (`365` DEGs across 86/7) by upregulating `IL1R2`, `ACSL1`, `AQP9` and `TNFAIP6` while repressing class-II antigen-presentation transcripts `HLA-DMB` and `CIITA` (alongside lymphocyte `CD69`/`RPS26`/`CD83` induction and `CX3CR1`/`ZNF683` loss; **Supplementary Fig. 4c–f**), whereas `scSemiProfiler` recovered only 46 sparse DEGs. In COVID-19 T cells (**Fig. 5h**), PBISC recapitulated selective type-I interferon induction (`IFIT3`, `IFI44L`, `MX1`, `IFIT1`, `IRF7`, `OAS1`) alongside monocyte `CD163`/`MCEMP1` upregulation and `VMO1`/`CDKN1C` downregulation (**Supplementary Fig. 5c–f**), avoiding the inflated false-positive interferon log-fold changes observed in `scSemiProfiler` (`170` T-cell DEGs with `ISG15`/`IFI6`/`MX1` $\log_2\text{FC} = +4.2\text{ to }+4.6$ versus $+1.3\text{ to }+1.7$ in `Real`).

### Reproduction of disease-specific rare immune populations and cell-intrinsic single-cell states across bacterial sepsis and COVID-19

A stringent test of generative bulk-to-single-cell synthesis is whether bulk-conditioned profiles retain low-abundance disease-specific immune populations ($<0.1\%$ to $2\%$ of PBMC) and cell-intrinsic single-cell programs rather than collapsing onto dominant lineage centroids or suffering the collinearity distortions of bulk deconvolution (**Fig. 6** and **Supplementary Figs. 2 and 3**). Across 14 rare immune states in bacterial sepsis cases and 13 rare states in ESKD COVID-19 cases transferred onto the shared Harmony reference, PBISC recovered 13 of 14 sepsis states and 12 of 13 COVID-19 states down to $<0.05\%$ of parent lineage (missing only ultra-sparse innate lymphoid cells (`ILC`)), whereas `scSemiProfiler` collapsed to identically zero cells in 7 of 14 sepsis states and 8 of 13 COVID-19 states—eliminating cycling `CD4+` T, `CD8+` T, NK and B cells, proliferating `Mono_c06-CD14-MKI67` monocytes, `B_ASC_IgG`, `CD14mono_dex`, `DC1`, `ASDC` and circulating hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (`HSPC`) (**Fig. 6a,b**). Before benchmarking proliferating monocytes in sepsis, we audited raw co-expression across the 82 paired sepsis donors in observed scRNA-seq, confirming that `CD14+ MKI67+ STMN1+` monocytes (both with and without granulocytic `MPO`/`ELANE`) occur predominantly in abdominal and pulmonary sepsis and paediatric infections (**Fig. 6c**).

In the integrated Harmony UMAP embeddings, PBISC-generated rare cells from unpaired bulk-only patients localized precisely onto their cognate `Real A` micro-clusters in **both** cohorts—overlaying `B_c07-MZB1` plasmablasts, `C1QA+` macrophages, `pDC`s and cycling `B_c08-MKI67` cells in bacterial sepsis (**Fig. 6d**), as well as `pDC`s, conventional `DC1`s, `B_ASC_dividing` plasmablasts and `MAIT` cells in ESKD COVID-19 (**Fig. 6e**). To verify that these rare cells carry genuine state-specific transcriptional identity beyond their parent lineage, we subtracted parent-lineage baselines across all 2,000 `Real A` HVGs (**Fig. 6f,g**, left). Diagonal `Real–PBISC` HVG specificity correlations reached $r = 0.97$ (`B_c07-MZB1`), $0.93$ (`Macrophage`), $0.55$ (`T_CD4_c07-MKI67`) and $0.72$ (`Mono_c06-CD14-MKI67`) in sepsis, and $r = 0.96$ (`B_ASC_IgG`), $0.93$ (`CD14mono_dex`), $0.97$ (`pDC`) and $0.92$ (`HSPC`) in COVID-19, with near-zero or negative off-diagonal correlations. At the individual-sample level (**Fig. 6f,g**, right), pairwise correlations between individual `PBISC` bulk-only patients and individual `Real` scRNA-seq patients (`Real–PBISC` median sample-pair $r$) approached the empirical `Real–Real` biological replicate ceiling across every state: `0.94` versus `0.94` (`B_c07-MZB1`), `0.85` versus `0.91` (`Macrophage`), `0.48` versus `0.61` (`T_CD4_c07-MKI67`) and `0.66` versus `0.94` (`Mono_c06-CD14-MKI67`) in sepsis; and `0.91` versus `0.88` (`B_ASC_IgG`), `0.87` versus `0.92` (`CD14mono_dex`), `0.90` versus `0.89` (`pDC`) and `0.82` versus `0.82` (`HSPC`) in COVID-19 (versus $-0.25$ to $+0.08$ for mismatched cross-state pairs). Across individual patients (`49,999`-iteration centered patient-block bootstrap), PBISC faithfully preserved sepsis `Mono_c06-CD14-MKI67` patient abundance and coordinated monocyte (`CD14`, `S100A8`, `S100A9`, `VCAN`) plus mitotic (`MKI67`, `STMN1`, `TOP2A`, `TYMS`) marker modules (`n = 15` `PBISC` and `17` `Real` positive samples; `scSemiProfiler`: 0; **Fig. 6h**), alongside canonical abundance and marker profiles across eight additional sepsis and COVID-19 rare states (`B_c07-MZB1`, `Macrophage`, `T_CD4_c07-MKI67`, `T_CD8_c06-MKI67`, `B_ASC_IgG`, `CD14mono_dex`, `pDC` and `HSPC`; **Supplementary Figs. 2 and 3**).

To test quantitative fidelity on matched clinical donors before downstream clinical discovery, we next evaluated the `27` non-CLL `Shared-27` ESKD COVID-19 bridge donors profiled by both observed scRNA-seq and complementary-visit PBISC synthesis (`59` visits; held-out `Real B` 30-NN transfer precision/recall = `99.0%/95.4%` for `HSPC` and `98.4%/98.6%` for pooled `B ASC`; **Fig. 6i**). PBISC tracked donor-level abundances across fine clinical compartments (`NK CD56bright`, `B ASC dividing/IgG/IgA`, `T CD8 CM`, `T CD8 CTL` and `HSPC`) while showing reproducible modality scale offsets (`0.3×` to `9.4×`), establishing an empirical basis for within-source rank-based inverse normal transformation (`rank-INT`) prior to pooling `Real` and `PBISC new`. Critically, benchmarking PBISC out-of-donor on `Shared-27` (two donor-disjoint folds) against **10 official R 4.3.2 bulk deconvolution implementations**—`BayesPrism`⁹ v2.2.3 (initial and updated Gibbs posteriors), `BisqueRNA`¹⁰ v1.0.5, `CIBERSORTx`¹¹ (`B-mode` and `S-mode` ComBat), `CIBERSORT-R` SVR, `Calib-NNLS`, `MuSiC`¹² v1.0.0, `NNLS` and `DWLS`¹³ v0.1.0—exposed two fatal failure modes of classical deconvolution on rare clinical states (**Fig. 6j,k**). First, PBISC achieved the highest accuracy across all 11 methods for the lead cytotoxic mortality compartment `T CD8 CTL` ($\rho = +0.55$ versus $+0.46$ for `BayesPrism-init`, $+0.36$ for `CIBERSORTx B-mode`, $+0.22$ for `BisqueRNA`, $+0.21$ for `BayesPrism-upd`, $+0.02$ for `CIBERSORTx S-mode`, and `0%` zero-collapse in `MuSiC`, `NNLS` and `DWLS`), while maintaining zero collapse across all 43 subsets (`Med ρ = +0.24`) and 23 rare subsets (`<1%` abundance, `Med ρ = +0.22`; **Fig. 6j**). Second, `BayesPrism` suffered **`37–43×` collinearity sink inflation** on ultra-rare `HSPC`s (`5.82%` initial and `4.96%` updated of total PBMC versus `0.13%` in observed `Real` scRNA-seq and `0.48%` in `PBISC`), and `Calib-NNLS` inflated `HSPC` `32×` (`4.19%`), whereas `CIBERSORT-R`, `MuSiC`, `NNLS` and `DWLS` collapsed median `HSPC` to `0.00%` (**Fig. 6k**).

Beyond abundance, PBISC uniquely preserved **cell-intrinsic single-cell transcriptional states** invisible to bulk markers or deconvolution. Across all `3,124` single `HSPC`s (`149` acute draws with $\ge 3$ `HSPC`s: `74` `Real` + `75` `PBISC new` across `61` patients), PBISC faithfully reproduced a fine-grained **20-gene intracellular `HSPC` erythroid lineage switch**: both `Real` and `PBISC new` concordantly captured progenitor-maintenance `GATA2` downregulation (combined same-day WHO GEE $\beta = -0.213, q_{20} = 0.011$) coupled to coordinated erythroid/megakaryocytic (`GATA1`, `KLF1`, `TFRC`, `BLVRB`, `APOC1`, `ITGA2B`) and mitotic (`MKI67`, `TOP2A`, `STMN1`, `TYMS`) activation ($q_{20} = 3.7 \times 10^{-5}\text{–}0.011$), while quiescent stem (`CD34`, `AVP`, `HLF`, `HOPX`, `CRHBP`) and granulocytic (`MPO`, `ELANE`, `CSF3R`, `CEBPD`) transcripts remained flat (**Fig. 6l**). Conversely, on `Wave-1 New-29` patients (`n = 29, 6` deaths), raw bulk `PROM1`, `AVP`, `CD34`, `SPINK2` and a 6-gene bulk `HSPC` panel all failed to capture fatal `HSPC` biology (`AUC` = 0.29–0.57, all $P \ge 0.42$), whereas `PBISC HSPC` synthesis achieved `AUC = 0.80` ($P = 0.022$; **Supplementary Fig. 10d**).

### Clinical discovery in expanded ESKD COVID-19: exact metadata matching, count-versus-percentage validation and T-cell ISG scoring

To establish clinical validity without sampling artefacts or metric ambiguity, we organized our expanded ESKD COVID-19 discovery framework (**Fig. 7** and **Supplementary Figs. 6–11**) around three rigorous pillars: **(1) exact 1:1 patient and dataset metadata matching**, **(2) population number (single-cell raw counts and clinical CBC `cells/µL`) versus percentage (`% PBMC` and `% parent`) auditing**, and **(3) deep 19-gene T-cell `ISG` scoring analysis**.

#### 1. Exact patient and dataset metadata matching and longitudinal pairing
A comprehensive sample-level audit across both cohorts verified exact 1:1 patient-to-dataset linkage with zero unverified merges (**Fig. 7a** and **Supplementary Figs. 6, 7 and 8a–c**; complete line listings in `PBISC_Metadata_CountVsPct_TISG_Audit_20261121.xlsx`). In bacterial sepsis (`255` total cross-sectional samples), observed `Real` scRNA-seq comprised `108` adult donors (`93` sepsis, `8` ICU, `7` HC; median age 67 [IQR 54–74] yr, 70.4% male; median `5,664` [IQR 4,727–6,846] cells/sample) plus `25` paediatric donors (`17` sepsis, `4` ICU, `4` HC), whereas bulk-only `PBISC` comprised `121` primary adults (`68` sepsis, `17` ICU, `36` HC; median age 65 [54–74] yr, 64.5% male; fixed `4,000` cells/sample) plus `1` paediatric ICU control (`PIC06`), with **zero donor overlap (`0` shared donors)** and `119/122` molecular sex concordance (`2` ambiguous healthy controls `HC23`/`HC30`; `1` GEO-versus-bulk discordant sample `Res-S34`, flagged explicitly in **Supplementary Fig. 6b**). In the Imperial College ESKD COVID-19 cohort (`107` unique donors: `68` acute COVID-19-positive and `39` COVID-19-negative single-draw controls), exact 1:1 linkage accounted for all `362` processed samples (`172` exact 0-day paired `Real bulk + Real scRNA` visits, `164` exact `Real bulk → PBISC` samples and `26` `Real scRNA-only` visits), confirming **zero demographic, WHO severity or mortality mismatches** across all `32` shared donors and **`163/164` (99.4%) molecular sex concordance** across PBISC samples (`1` ambiguous non-primary Shared-27 draw `C137_positive_2`, `0` sex swaps; **Supplementary Fig. 6a,b**).

Bulk `ROR1`/`FMOD` molecular screening excluded two incidental monoclonal B-CLL patients (`C116`, `C139`; **Supplementary Fig. 8c**), and forensic adjudication against Stephenson et al.³ Supplementary Table 1 and two-month convalescent visits resolved three miscoded Wave-2 survivors (`C101`, `C146`, `C147`), establishing `11` verified acute deaths (`TRUE_PUB_11`, including `9` direct COVID-19 deaths; **Supplementary Figs. 6b and 8b**). Combining `37` primary `Real` scRNA-seq patients (`10` Real-only + `27` `Shared-27` counted once in `Real`; `138` acute draws, `5` deaths) with `29` scRNA-naive Wave-1 `PBISC new` patients (`79` acute draws, `6` deaths) expanded total patients by `+78%` (`37 → 66`), critical `WHO 3` illness by `+140%` (`5 → 12`), verified deaths by `+120%` (`5 → 11`) and direct COVID-19 deaths by `+200%` (`3 → 9`; **Fig. 7b**, left). Complete individual swimmer schedules (**Supplementary Fig. 7**) exposed two critical longitudinal features: `91/217` acute draws (`41.9%`) occurred on days with a lower same-day WHO grade (`WHO_temp_severity`) than the patient's eventual peak WHO grade, and fatal patients exhibited a threefold sampling asymmetry (`Real` fatal `4.0` draws/patient versus `PBISC new` fatal `1.3` draws/patient; `4/6` died after a single draw). We therefore anchored primary inference on **exact longitudinal pairing**: (i) all `217` draws paired with contemporaneous same-day WHO severity via patient-clustered exchangeable `GEE +wk` and Mundlak within–between decomposition; and (ii) **one baseline observation per patient—each donor's first blood draw** (`N = 66, 11` deaths; `53/66` patients and `10/11` fatal cases sampled $\le 7\text{ d}$ post-swab)—paired with final mortality (`TRUE_PUB_11`). Screening all 43 fine-grained immune subsets ($\ge 30\%$ `Real` detection) uncovered **20 combined-FDR associations** (`18` same-day WHO `GEE +wk` $q_{43} < 0.05$ + `2` baseline first-draw mortality predictors `T CD8 CTL` and `NK (CD56dim)` at $q_{43} = 0.070 < 0.10$; `17` credible non-steroid sign-concordant subsets: `5` `Real` anchors reinforced 400–25,000-fold + `12` PBISC-rescued discoveries; **Fig. 7b**, right, and **Supplementary Fig. 8d,e**).

#### 2. Population number (raw count and clinical CBC) versus percentage (`% PBMC` and `% parent`) severity and mortality check
Because `Real` scRNA-seq library recovery varied `40×` across acute draws (`n_cells` median `2,204` [IQR 1,092–4,116], range `226–9,254`) whereas `PBISC new` synthesized a constant `4,000` cells per draw, we explicitly audited whether clinical severity and mortality associations depend on expressing immune populations as **cell percentages (`% PBMC` or `% parent lineage`)** versus **absolute cell numbers (`Raw Count` per sample or true clinical hospital CBC absolute counts `cells/µL`)** (**Fig. 7c** and **Supplementary Fig. 9a–d**). First, in `Real` scRNA-seq alone (`138` draws), total recovered `n_cells` was not significantly associated with either contemporaneous same-day WHO severity ($\rho = -0.129, P = 0.131$) or first-draw mortality (`AUC` = 0.575, $P = 0.620$; **Supplementary Fig. 9b**), ruling out systematic sequencing-depth confounding. Second, across all 43 COVID-19 subsets, within-source `rank-INT` of **raw single-cell counts** and **`% PBMC`** yielded virtually identical effect sizes for both contemporaneous same-day WHO severity (**Pearson $r = 0.9870$**, `18` versus `18` subsets passing `BH43` $q_{43} < 0.05$, and `18` FDR also under `% Parent INT`; **Fig. 7c**, left) and first-draw mortality (**Pearson $r = 0.9845$**; **Supplementary Fig. 9a**). The same near-identity held across `44` fine-grained bacterial sepsis subsets ordered across `HC → ICU → Sepsis` (`Real` adult raw count versus `% PBMC` Spearman $\rho$ correlation $r = 0.9925$; `PBISC` $121$ adults $r = 1.0000$, with cross-disease depletion of `NK_c01-GZMK` replicating in both `Real` [$\rho = -0.230$ `%`, $-0.283$ count] and `PBISC` [$\rho = -0.475, P = 3.7 \times 10^{-8}$]; **Supplementary Fig. 9c**).

Crucially, however, comparing metrics also demonstrated **why within-source `% PBMC INT` outperforms raw counts on mortality**: the `40×` capture-depth variance in `Real` injects random noise into raw cell counts, slightly attenuating `T CD8 CTL` mortality discrimination (`AUC` = 0.213 [$P = 0.0029$, `adjWHO P = 0.034`, $q_{43} = 0.070$] under `% PBMC INT` versus `0.232` [$P = 0.0055$, `adjWHO P = 0.062`] under `Count INT`), whereas naively pooling raw counts across `Real` (`2.2k` median) and `PBISC new` (`4.0k` fixed) without within-source normalization severely degrades `T CD8 CTL` to `AUC = 0.311` ($P = 0.050$; **Supplementary Fig. 9a**). Most decisively, computing **true clinical absolute leukocyte subset concentrations (`cells/µL`)** in `Real` by multiplying single-cell subset fractions by contemporaneous clinical hospital CBC total lymphocyte/monocyte counts independently validated every major flagship association (**Fig. 7c**, right, and **Supplementary Fig. 9d**): same-day WHO expansion of `B ASC dividing` ($\beta = +0.373, P = 2.7 \times 10^{-5}$), `B ASC IgG` ($\beta = +0.296, P = 1.8 \times 10^{-4}$) and `CD14mono_IFN` ($\beta = +0.282, P = 0.041$), same-day WHO depletion of `NK CD56bright` ($\beta = -0.388, P = 1.1 \times 10^{-6}$) and `T CD8 CM` ($\beta = -0.338, P = 2.2 \times 10^{-4}$), and first-draw mortality depletion of absolute `T CD8 CTL cells/µL` (`AUC` = 0.226 [inv `0.774`], $P = 0.053$, $n = 36$, 5 deaths).

Under this metric-validated primary framework, stratified effects, Mundlak decomposition, 300-simulation power rescue, external four-cohort replication (`N = 220`) and same-day hospital laboratory GEE (`BH36`) cleanly separated three distinct immunopathological axes (**Fig. 7d–f** and **Supplementary Figs. 9e,f, 10b,e and 11**):
- **Class-switched `B ASC`s dynamically track within-patient contemporaneous severity**: Across 217 draws, `B ASC IgG` ($\beta = +0.414, q_{43} = 1.8 \times 10^{-8}$; Mundlak $\beta_{\text{within}} = +0.418, P = 2.9 \times 10^{-4}$), `B ASC dividing` ($\beta = +0.422, q_{43} = 1.8 \times 10^{-8}$; $\beta_{\text{within}} = +0.377, P = 8.2 \times 10^{-5}$) and `B ASC IgA` ($\beta = +0.334, q_{43} = 1.2 \times 10^{-4}$; $\beta_{\text{within}} = +0.267, P = 0.015$) tracked within-patient day-to-day WHO transitions, coupled tightly to same-day serum ferritin ($\beta = +0.66, q_{36} = 6.1 \times 10^{-12}$) and `D-dimer` ($\beta = +0.58, q_{36} = 1.3 \times 10^{-6}$), replicated in four external COVID-19 cohorts (`N = 220`, `B ASC total` DL $\rho = +0.26, P = 0.0063$; Stephenson et al.⁶ isotypes `OR = 1.83–1.98`), and were neutral for first-draw mortality (`AUC` = 0.46–0.51).
- **Baseline cytotoxic `T CD8 CTL` and `NK (CD56dim)` depletion independently predicts acute mortality from the first blood draw**: At donor first draw (`N = 66, 11` deaths), `T CD8 CTL` (`AUC` = 0.213 [inv `0.787`], $P = 0.0029, q_{43} = 0.070$; replicating identically in `Real` [`0.206`, $P = 0.036$] and `PBISC new` [`0.210`, $P = 0.031$], $P_{\text{het}} = 0.974$; `adjWHO P = 0.034`; early $\le 7\text{ d}$ $P = 0.022$; severe/critical `WHO 2–3` $P = 0.011$) and `NK (CD56dim)` (`AUC` = 0.217 [inv `0.783`], $P = 0.0033, q_{43} = 0.070$; `adjWHO P = 0.028`; direct COVID-9 `inv AUC` = 0.844, $P = 0.0010$) were the top two prognostic predictors across all 43 subsets (power rescued from `15–23%` to `99–100%`; `−(T CD8 CTL + NK)` `AUC` = 0.823, $P = 7.9 \times 10^{-4}$), while remaining flat on time-adjusted same-day WHO GEE and orthogonal to routine hospital labs (**Fig. 7d–f**).
- **Numerical `HSPC` expansion tracks contemporaneous deterioration and late (`>7 d`) mortality, while intracellular `HSPC` priming starts at day 1**: Adding `PBISC new` rescued `HSPC` same-day WHO association (`Real` $q_{43} = 0.227$ → `Combined` $\beta = +0.333, q_{43} = 1.3 \times 10^{-4}$; Mundlak $\beta_{\text{within}} = +0.242, P = 0.0088$; power `10.7% → 100.0%`; external `N = 191` DL $\rho = +0.44, P = 2.4 \times 10^{-7}$). Total `HSPC %` was flat at first draw (`AUC` = 0.577, $P = 0.43$) before surging at `>7 d` (`AUC` = 0.822, $P = 0.011$; paired $n = 43$ shift `0.44 → 0.78`; external `>7 d AUC` = 0.71, $P = 0.011$), whereas single-cell `HSPC` `Alarmin_Inflam` (`AUC` = 0.724, `adjWHO P = 0.022`) and `Ery5` (`AUC` = 0.696, $P = 0.044$) already predicted mortality on the first `HSPC`-positive draw (**Fig. 6l** and **Supplementary Fig. 10c,d**).

#### 3. Deep 19-gene T-cell ISG scoring analysis: time-bias dissection, viral specificity, CTL–ISG coupling, fatal collapse and prognosis
Because type-I interferon signalling is a central, time-sensitive hallmark of acute SARS-CoV-2 immunopathology⁶,⁷, we performed a dedicated multi-compartment audit of the 19-gene T-cell `ISG` module across all `13` T-cell subsets (`213–217` acute draws across `66` patients; **Fig. 7g–j** and **Supplementary Fig. 10a,b**). On `Shared-27` bridge donors, PBISC single-cell T-cell `ISG` scoring achieved $\rho = +0.74\text{ to }+0.81$ across all five major T compartments and outperformed official `BayesPrism` v2.2.3 posterior expression tensors ($Z$) in both `T CD8 CTL` ($\rho = +0.81$ versus $+0.74$) and `T CD8 CM` ($\rho = +0.57$ versus $+0.49$; **Supplementary Fig. 10a,b**).

Decomposing clinical associations across time windows resolved a major methodological pitfall in static cross-sectional T-cell ISG analyses (**Fig. 7g**, top, and **Supplementary Fig. 10a**). Under conventional patient-mean peak WHO correlation, all major T compartments appeared positively associated with peak severity ($\rho = +0.29\text{ to }+0.33, q_{13} = 0.037$). However, stratifying longitudinal draws revealed that this positive signal is confined strictly to the **early acute viral phase ($\le 7\text{ d}$ post-swab: `GEE +wk` $\beta = +0.22\text{ to }+0.25, P = 0.014\text{–}0.024$)**, whereas adjusting across all 213–217 draws for time since first positive swab (`SD-WHO GEE +wk`) drove severity slopes to exact zero across all 13 T-cell subsets ($\beta = -0.04\text{ to }+0.03, P = 0.59\text{–}0.96, q_{13} = 0.96$), demonstrating that static patient-mean ISG–severity correlations are confounded by earlier sampling of rapidly deteriorating patients. To confirm biological specificity to viral infection, we scored the exact same 19-gene T-cell ISG module (all `19/19` genes present) in our bacterial sepsis cohort (**Fig. 7g**, bottom, and **Supplementary Fig. 10b**, bottom): in stark contrast to COVID-19 induction (`log2FC = +0.54 to +2.17` across 19 ISGs in both `Real` and `PBISC`), PBISC bacterial sepsis T-cell ISG **declined monotonically** across `Adult HC` (median `0.479`) → `ICU` (`0.413`) → `Sepsis` (`0.422`; Spearman $\rho = -0.388, P = 1.10 \times 10^{-5}$; Sepsis versus HC $P = 1.43 \times 10^{-6}$), matching observed `Real` sepsis downregulation of `IFI44L`, `IFIT1`, `STAT1` and `OAS2` (whereas `scSemiProfiler` erroneously inverted sign to positive in sepsis).

Linking T-cell ISG state back to cellular abundance uncovered a striking **baseline cytotoxic-loss versus hyper-interferon anti-correlation** (**Fig. 7h**). Across all `213` draws ($\rho = -0.261, P = 1.14 \times 10^{-4}$) and most prominently at **donor first blood draw (`N = 66`: $\rho = -0.396, P = 0.0010$)**, patients with depleted `T CD8 CTL` fractions simultaneously exhibited heightened `T CD8 CTL ISG` activation—concentrating fatal cases in the **low-CTL / high-ISG** quadrant (`first-draw T CD8 CTL ISG AUC = 0.676, P = 0.068`). Combining baseline cytotoxic loss and interferon activation into a simple tri-axis score (`−(T CD8 CTL + NK) + T CD8 CTL ISG`) increased unadjusted first-draw mortality separation to **`AUC = 0.828` ($P = 6.57 \times 10^{-4}$)** (versus `0.802` for `−CTL` alone and `0.823` for `−(CTL + NK)`). Longitudinally across all 213–217 acute draws, fatal patients who started with elevated early T-cell ISG underwent a **steep `Time × Fatal` interferon collapse** across all five major T compartments (**Fig. 7i**): `T CD8 CTL ISG` fell at $\beta = -0.55\text{ s.d. week}^{-1}$ ($P = 3.7 \times 10^{-6}$, replicating identically in `Real` [$-0.53, P = 9.1 \times 10^{-5}$] and `PBISC new` [$-0.51, P = 0.016$]), with parallel collapse in `T CD4 CTL` ($-0.63\text{ week}^{-1}, P = 1.1 \times 10^{-9}$), `All CD8 T` ($-0.69\text{ week}^{-1}, P = 9.5 \times 10^{-11}$), `All CD4 T` ($-0.64\text{ week}^{-1}, P = 6.6 \times 10^{-11}$) and `All T cells` ($-0.67\text{ week}^{-1}, P = 1.6 \times 10^{-11}$). Finally, in leak-free in-fold `rank-INT` `50 × 5-fold` stratified cross-validation on donor first blood draws (`Age + Sex + same-day WHO` baseline `AUC = 0.688`; **Fig. 7j**), adding **first-draw `T CD8 CTL ISG`** alone improved out-of-fold mortality `AUC` to **`0.764` ($\Delta\text{AUC} = +0.076$)**, adding **`T CD8 CTL`** reached **`0.834` ($\Delta\text{AUC} = +0.146$)**, adding **`T CD8 CTL + NK`** reached **`0.837` ($\Delta\text{AUC} = +0.149$)**, and restricting to early first draws $\le 7\text{ d}$ (`n = 52` complete cases, `10` deaths) lifted `+T CD8 CTL` from `0.670` to **`0.833` ($\Delta\text{AUC} = +0.163$)**, whereas adding late-peaking `HSPC %` within `WHO 2–3` yielded only $\Delta\text{AUC} = +0.01$. All primary findings were fully corroborated by the 14-variant patient-mean sensitivity audit (**Supplementary Figs. 9f and 11**).

## Discussion (Figures 4–7 Section)

A central bottleneck in clinical single-cell genomics is the gap between cohort-scale bulk transcriptomics and small, logistically constrained scRNA-seq subcohorts. Across two distinct acute inflammatory syndromes—bacterial sepsis¹ and ESKD COVID-19²,³—our findings show that cohort-adapted PBISC bridges this gap by converting unpaired clinical bulk RNA-seq into realistic single-cell count ensembles (`K = 4,000` cells per sample) that co-embed with observed scRNA-seq near the biological replicate ceiling (`iLISI` = 1.57–1.60 versus 1.65–1.68; **Fig. 4**), preserve cell-type-resolved disease DEG programs near the empirical split-half ceiling (`AUROC` = 0.78–0.99; **Fig. 5**), and faithfully recover low-abundance rare immune populations and cell-intrinsic single-cell transcriptional states across both bacterial sepsis and COVID-19 (**Fig. 6**). Directly benchmarking PBISC against semi-profiling and 10 official R 4.3.2 bulk deconvolution implementations clarifies why generative single-cell synthesis is uniquely required for rare-cell biology: `scSemiProfiler`⁴ collapsed library sizes onto a narrow Poisson spike and eliminated 50–62% of rare states (`Mono_c06-CD14-MKI67`, `B_ASC_IgG`, `CD14mono_dex`, cycling T/NK cells and `HSPC`), weighted least-squares deconvolution (`MuSiC`¹², `DWLS`¹³, `NNLS`) suffered complete zero-collapse on `T CD8 CTL` and `HSPC`, and Bayesian deconvolution (`BayesPrism`⁹ v2.2.3) inflated ultra-rare `HSPC` abundance `37–43×` (`4.96–5.82%` versus `0.13%` observed) while trailing PBISC on both `T CD8 CTL` abundance ($\rho = +0.55$ versus $+0.21\text{–}0.46$) and `T CD8 CTL ISG` state scoring ($\rho = +0.81$ versus $+0.74$; **Fig. 6j,k** and **Supplementary Fig. 10b**). Moreover, PBISC uniquely reproduced the fine-grained `GATA2↓ → GATA1/KLF1/TYMS↑` erythroid switch across `3,124` single circulating `HSPC`s (**Fig. 6l**) where raw bulk `CD34`/`SPINK2`/`PROM1`/`AVP` transcripts failed completely.

Translating bulk-to-single-cell cohort expansion into rigorous clinical discovery (**Fig. 7**) hinges on three methodological principles audited directly here:
1. **Exact 1:1 patient-to-dataset metadata auditing and longitudinal pairing**: Forensic reconciliation across `255` bacterial sepsis samples (`0` overlap between `Real` and `PBISC`, `119/122` molecular sex concordant) and `107` ESKD COVID-19 donors (`362` 1:1 mapped samples, `0` shared demographic/WHO/death mismatches, `163/164` sex concordant, `−2 CLL`, `TRUE_PUB_11`; **Fig. 7a** and **Supplementary Figs. 6–8**) prevents silent label contamination, while pairing repeated draws to contemporaneous same-day WHO severity and anchoring mortality strictly to each donor's first blood draw removes both within-patient severity dilution (`41.9%` same-day ≠ Peak WHO) and threefold fatal sampling-frequency bias (`4.0` versus `1.3` draws/patient).
2. **Population number versus percentage invariance—and the need for within-source normalization**: Auditing cell percentages (`% PBMC` and `% parent`) against both raw single-cell counts and clinical hospital CBC absolute counts (`cells/µL`) across `43` COVID-19 and `44` sepsis subsets proves that clinical associations are biologically invariant to number versus percentage representation ($r = 0.985\text{–}0.993$ across endpoints and diseases, independently confirmed in `Real` clinical `CBC cells/µL`; **Fig. 7c** and **Supplementary Fig. 9a–d**). Simultaneously, it highlights a practical pitfall: because real droplet scRNA-seq cell recovery fluctuates `40×` (`226–9,254` cells) whereas synthetic cohorts generate fixed cell counts (`4,000`), naively pooling raw cell counts degrades prognostic discrimination (`T CD8 CTL AUC 0.213 → 0.311`), establishing within-source `% PBMC rank-INT` as the mathematically principled primary scale.
3. **Time-resolved T-cell ISG scoring and baseline cytotoxic-interferon coupling**: Dissecting the 19-gene T-cell `ISG` module across all `13` T-cell subsets reconciles conflicting literature on interferon activity in severe COVID-19⁶,⁷ (**Fig. 7g–j** and **Supplementary Fig. 10a,b**). Static patient-mean ISG–severity correlations reflect early acute-phase ($\le 7\text{ d}$) viral activation combined with sampling-day confounding and vanish upon all-draw time adjustment ($\beta \approx 0$), whereas cross-disease profiling in bacterial sepsis confirms viral specificity (T-ISG declines monotonically in sepsis, $\rho = -0.39, P = 1.1 \times 10^{-5}$). Instead, baseline `T CD8 CTL` depletion is negatively coupled to heightened `T CD8 CTL ISG` already at the first blood draw ($\rho = -0.396, P = 0.0010$; tri-axis `−(CTL + NK) + ISG AUC = 0.828`), followed by catastrophic longitudinal `Time × Fatal` interferon collapse across all five T compartments ($\beta = -0.55\text{ to }-0.69\text{ s.d. week}^{-1}, P = 3.7 \times 10^{-6}\text{ to }9.5 \times 10^{-11}$). Together with dynamic within-patient `B ASC` tracking (coupled to same-day ferritin and `D-dimer`) and early cell-intrinsic `HSPC` `Alarmin`/`Ery5` priming preceding week-2 stress-haematopoiesis egress¹⁴, PBISC expands single-cell cohorts to rescue both statistical power (`11–24% → 97–100%`) and leak-free baseline mortality prediction (`+CTL ISG ΔAUC = +0.076`, `+CTL ΔAUC = +0.146`, `+CTL + NK ΔAUC = +0.149`).

Several boundaries warrant attention. First, PBISC cohort adaptation relies on an anchor set of paired bulk–scRNA donors (`57–82` donors) to calibrate five-fold LoRA priors; reproducible between-modality scale offsets across rare subsets (`0.3×`–`9.4×`; **Fig. 6i**) mandate within-source `rank-INT` rather than uncalibrated raw pooling. Second, pharmacological perturbation states—exemplified by `CD14mono_dex`, which was neutral at baseline (`P = 0.29`) before surging to `AUC = 0.92` at `>7 d` during Wave-2 dexamethasone therapy—require explicit treatment auditing across recruitment waves. Third, molecular sex QC flagged one discordant sepsis bulk sample (`Res-S34`) and one ambiguous non-primary COVID-19 Shared-27 draw (`C137_positive_2`), illustrating the value of routine expression-based metadata verification before clinical modelling. Together, these results establish generative bulk-to-single-cell synthesis as a rigorously audited framework for unlocking rare-cell transcriptomics, cross-metric validation and longitudinal clinical discovery from archival and prospective bulk cohorts.

## Methods (Figures 4–7 Section)

### Clinical cohorts, 1:1 metadata matching and molecular sex-QC auditing
**Bacterial sepsis cohort (`Ye et al., 2026`).** Paired whole-blood bulk RNA-seq (`GSE279448`) and PBMC scRNA-seq (`GSE279452`) from Ye et al.¹ (`255` total cross-sectional single-draw samples) were audited 1:1 against GEO sample metadata (`geo_characteristics.csv` and published sample tables; **Supplementary Fig. 6a** and `A1_sepsis_255_samples_matched_metadata.csv`). Observed `Real` scRNA-seq comprised `133` total donors: `108` adult donors (`93` sepsis, `8` non-septic ICU controls, `7` healthy controls; median age 67 [IQR 54–74] yr, 76/108 [70.4%] male; median `5,664` [4,727–6,846] cells/sample; `633,072` adult cells across `37,474` features, of which `5,967` mapped uniquely to the 6,000-gene PBISC panel) plus `25` paediatric donors (`17` sepsis, `4` ICU, `4` HC). After excluding 3 QC-failed bulk libraries from `207` bulk RNA-seq samples, `82` paired anchor donors were used for five-fold donor-blocked LoRA adaptation and all `122` unpaired bulk-only donors (`121` primary adults: `68` sepsis, `17` ICU, `36` HC; median age 65 [54–74] yr, 78/121 [64.5%] male; plus `1` paediatric ICU control `PIC06`) were synthesized at `K = 4,000` cells/sample (`0` donor overlap with `Real` scRNA-seq). Molecular sex verification comparing GEO sex labels against bulk Y/X-chromosome expression (`sex_flag`) confirmed `119/122` matches (`2` ambiguous healthy controls `HC23`/`HC30`; `1` discordant sample `Res-S34`, documented in **Supplementary Fig. 6b**).

**Imperial College ESKD COVID-19 cohort (`Gisby et al., 2022`; `Stephenson et al., 2025`).** Longitudinal blood bulk RNA-seq (`Zenodo 6497251` / `E-MTAB-11536`, `336` samples, `15` excluded) and PBMC scRNA-seq (`187` published samples, `578,835` cells across `33,559` features; `5,791` mapped to the 6,000-gene PBISC panel) were integrated across Wave 1 and Wave 2²,³. Exact 1:1 sample linkage across all `107` ESKD donors (`68` acute COVID-19-positive, `39` COVID-19-negative single-draw controls) mapped all `362` processed rows (`172` exact 0-day paired `Real bulk + Real scRNA` samples, `164` exact `Real bulk → PBISC` synthesized samples and `26` `Real scRNA-only` visits), verifying **zero demographic, WHO or fatal-outcome mismatches** across all `32` shared donors and **`163/164` (99.4%) molecular sex concordance** (`1` ambiguous non-primary Shared-27 draw `C137_positive_2`, `0` swaps). Five-fold donor-blocked LoRA adaptation on `57` paired donors (`157` visits) generated `656,000` cells across all `164` bulk-only samples. Two pre-specified clinical audits excluded two incidental monoclonal B-CLL patients (`C116`, `C139`; bulk `ROR1 > 2.5`, `FMOD > 1.0`) and resolved three miscoded Wave-2 survivors (`C101`, `C146`, `C147`) against Stephenson et al.³ Table S1 and two-month convalescent visits, yielding `11` verified acute deaths (`TRUE_PUB_11`, including `9` direct deaths `DIRECT_9`). Primary acute scope required acute follow-up (`0–30` days from first positive swab) and $\ge 200$ QC-passing cells (`2` low-cell `Real` draws excluded: `C73_2404` [179 cells], `C168_2601` [4 cells]), yielding exactly `217` acute draws across `66` unique patients (`138` `Real` draws across `37` patients [`10` Real-only + `27` `Shared-27` counted once in `Real`; `59` complementary-visit `PBISC` draws on `Shared-27` reserved for **Fig. 6i–k**], `5` deaths; and `79` `PBISC new` draws across `29` Wave-1 scRNA-naive patients, `6` deaths).

### Five-fold LoRA cohort adaptation, shared Harmony integration, Kitagawa decomposition and pseudobulk DEGs (Figs. 4–5)
Pretrained PBISC weights were adapted via donor-stratified five-fold LoRA (`NewLora` in sepsis; `20261104 New reference dataset` in COVID-19) on paired bulk–scRNA folds (`L1 = 0.36` versus permutation nulls `0.53` and `0.52`), generating `K = 4,000` discrete ceiling-UMI cells per bulk-only sample. `scSemiProfiler`⁴ was run under its strict five-pair representative-donor Poisson protocol (`51,000` cells / `34` samples in sepsis; `60,000` cells / `102` samples in COVID-19). `Real` donors were split at the donor level into `Real A` (reference) and `Real B` (replicate ceiling); `Real A` and query sources were normalized (`target_sum = 10,000`), $\log(1 + x)$-transformed, restricted to 2,000 `Real A` HVGs, projected onto 30 PCs and Harmony-integrated⁵ (`batch_key = source`). Mixing was quantified via median $k$-NN `iLISI` ($k = 90$), and six-lineage and fine-state labels were transferred from `Real A` via weighted 30-NN voting. Case-versus-control pseudobulk shifts were partitioned via Kitagawa decomposition across $\ge 3,175$ expressed genes into compositional ($\Delta$ share) and within-lineage ($\Delta$ expr.) components with `Real` split-half reliability correction ($\hat{\beta}_{\text{corr}} = \hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}} / R_{\text{Real}}$ across 50 splits). Lineage-resolved pseudobulk DEGs (`T`, `B`, `NK`, `Monocyte`; $\ge 10$ cells/donor; $\log_2(\text{CPM} + 1)$) were tested across `5,664` (sepsis) and `5,610` (COVID-19) genes via `limma-trend`-equivalent empirical Bayes linear models (`BH` $q < 0.05$, $|\log_2\text{FC}| > 1$), evaluating `Real`-DEG `AUROC` versus split-half ceilings, corrected slopes and double-disattenuated correlations $r / \sqrt{R_{\text{Real}} R_{\text{gen}}}$.

### Rare-population specificity, official R 4.3.2 deconvolution benchmark and single-cell `HSPC` 20-gene profiling (Fig. 6)
Across four focal rare states per cohort (`B_c07-MZB1`, `Macrophage`, `T_CD4_c07-MKI67`, `Mono_c06-CD14-MKI67` in sepsis; `B_ASC_IgG`, `CD14mono_dex`, `pDC`, `HSPC` in COVID-19), parent-lineage-subtracted specificity vectors ($\Delta \mu_{s,g} = \mu_{s,g} - \mu_{\text{parent}(s),g}$) across all 2,000 `Real A` HVGs were correlated between `Real`, `PBISC` and `scSemiProfiler`, and pairwise sample-level correlations (`Real–Real`, `Real–PBISC`, cross-state) were evaluated across the top signature HVG union (`119` sepsis / `131` COVID-19 genes). Patient-level abundances were compared via two-sided centered two-sample patient-block bootstrap (`49,999` resamples, BH-adjusted across 10 states/cohort). For the official R 4.3.2 deconvolution benchmark on `Shared-27` ($n = 27$ donors, two donor-disjoint folds, `43` fine cell types), native R packages (`BayesPrism`⁹ v2.2.3 initial/updated posteriors + expression tensors $Z$, `BisqueRNA`¹⁰ v1.0.5, `CIBERSORTx`¹¹ `B-mode`/`S-mode` ComBat via `sva` v3.50.0, `CIBERSORT-R` $\nu$-SVR, `MuSiC`¹² v1.0.0, `DWLS`¹³ v0.1.0, `nnls` v1.6 and calibrated `NNLS`) were evaluated via Spearman $\rho$ against paired `Real` scRNA-seq proportions and median `HSPC %` versus observed `Real` (`0.13%`). Across `3,124` single `HSPC`s (`149` draws $\ge 3$ `HSPC`s across `61` patients; `5,762` shared genes, $\log(1 + \text{CP10k})$), `16` lineage/stress modules (`BH16`) and `20` canonical genes (`BH20`) were evaluated under the primary same-day WHO GEE (`+wk`) and first `HSPC`-positive draw mortality framework.

### Primary longitudinal pairing, population number versus percentage auditing and deep T-cell ISG analysis (Fig. 7)
To remove systematic between-modality scale offsets (**Fig. 6i**) without touching clinical outcomes, every cellular abundance or single-cell transcriptional score was transformed separately within `Real` and within `PBISC new` via Van der Waerden rank-based inverse normal transformation ($z_i = \Phi^{-1}((\text{rank}(x_i) - 0.5)/n_s)$). Across all `43` fine-grained subsets ($\ge 30\%$ `Real` detection):
1. **Primary contemporaneous severity (`217` acute draws × same-day WHO)**: Exchangeable patient-clustered GEE (`statsmodels`, Huber–White and Mancl–DeRouen bias-reduced covariance) adjusted for weeks since first positive swab (`z ~ WHO_day + weeks`, `BH43` $q_{43} < 0.05$), Mundlak within–between decomposition (`z ~ (WHO_day − mean_WHO_i) + mean_WHO_i + weeks`) and `3,000`-resample patient-block cluster-bootstrap Spearman $\rho$.
2. **Primary baseline mortality (`66` donor first blood draws × `TRUE_PUB_11`)**: Exact two-sided Mann–Whitney `AUC` on each donor's earliest acute draw (`BH43` $q_{43} < 0.10$; `3,000` stratified patient bootstrap CIs), with same-day WHO residualization (`adjWHO`), early first draws $\le 7\text{ d}$ (`n = 53`), `DIRECT_9` (`9` deaths), `WHO 2–3` (`n = 29`) and late acute draws (`>7 d`, `n = 50`). Power rescue (**Fig. 7d**) ran `300` mortality-stratified subsamples ($n = 20$ `Real` patients alone, `+29 PBISC new` or `+29` outcome-shuffled controls).
3. **Population number versus percentage and clinical CBC audit**: Every primary severity (`217`-draw `GEE +wk`) and first-draw mortality (`66`-pt `AUC`) model was re-executed identically across five scale definitions (**Fig. 7c** and **Supplementary Fig. 9a–d**): (i) within-source `% PBMC rank-INT` (primary), (ii) within-source `Raw Cell Count rank-INT`, (iii) `log1p(Raw Count)` GEE / naively pooled raw counts, (iv) within-source `% Parent Lineage rank-INT`, and (v) **true clinical absolute counts (`cells/µL`)** in `Real` computed by multiplying scRNA subset shares by same-day clinical hospital CBC absolute lymphocyte counts (for T, B and NK subsets; `n = 36` first draws / `89` total draws) or monocyte/WBC counts (for myeloid/HSPC subsets; `n = 19–20` first draws). Sequencing-depth confounding (`n_cells`) was tested directly in `Real` (`138` draws), and count-versus-percentage concordance was independently audited across `44` fine-grained adult bacterial sepsis subsets (`HC → ICU → Sepsis`).
4. **Deep 19-gene T-cell `ISG` analysis and leak-free `50 × 5` CV**: Across all `13` T-cell subsets (`≥5` cells/draw; `213–217` acute draws), the 19-gene type-I IFN module (`ISG15`, `IFI6`, `IFI44`, `IFI44L`, `IFIT1`, `IFIT2`, `IFIT3`, `MX1`, `MX2`, `OAS1`, `OAS2`, `OAS3`, `RSAD2`, `XAF1`, `STAT1`, `IRF7`, `EPSTI1`, `HERC5`, `SIGLEC1`) was audited across `Shared-27` (against official `BayesPrism` $Z$), patient-mean peak WHO, early $\le 7\text{ d}$ `GEE +wk`, all-draw `SD-WHO GEE +wk`, donor first-draw `AUC`, baseline `T CD8 CTL` abundance–`ISG` coupling (`Spearman ρ` and tri-axis `−(CTL + NK) + ISG`), longitudinal `Time × Fatal` GEE (`Fatal + Weeks + Fatal × Weeks + Source`), and cross-disease bacterial sepsis (`121` PBISC primary adults + `Real`/`PBISC`/`scSemiProfiler` 19-ISG T-cell DEG `log2FC`). Same-day hospital laboratory GEE (`BH36`) and four-cohort external meta-analysis ($N = 220$, DerSimonian–Laird + modified Knapp–Hartung) followed pre-specified protocols. Leak-free `50 × 5-fold` stratified first-draw mortality CV (**Fig. 7j**) fitted within-source `rank-INT` and `StandardScaler` strictly inside each training fold before `L2`-penalized logistic regression (`C = 1.0`, `Age + Sex + SD-WHO` baseline versus `+T CD8 CTL ISG`, `+T CD8 CTL`, `+CTL + NK` and `≤7 d` subsets).

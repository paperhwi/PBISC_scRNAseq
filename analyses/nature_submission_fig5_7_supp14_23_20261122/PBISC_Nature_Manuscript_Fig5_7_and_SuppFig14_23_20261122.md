## Results

### Cross-cohort single-cell generation from unpaired bulk-only patient cohorts preserves lineage shifts, disease gene programs, and cell-type differential expression

Having established in paired held-out donors that adapting PBISC on five paired donors per cohort accurately reconstructs 4,000 single cells per bulk sample and recovers `limma-voom` disease differentially expressed genes (DEGs; Benjamini–Hochberg `BH q < 0.05`) across reference-patient combinations (Fig. 4 and Supplementary Figs. 8–13), we next asked whether the adapted models generalize across completely separate patient cohorts profiled only by bulk RNA sequencing (**Fig. 5a** and **Supplementary Fig. 14a–d**). We generated `K = 4,000` single cells per sample across all `122` unpaired bulk-only whole-blood samples in the bacterial sepsis cohort (`Ye et al., 2026`; `488,000` PBISC cells across `68` adult sepsis, `17` adult non-septic intensive care unit [ICU], `36` adult healthy control [HC], and `1` paediatric ICU sample `PIC06`) and across all `164` unpaired bulk-only peripheral blood samples in the haemodialysis COVID-19 cohort (`Gisby et al., 2022; Stephenson et al., 2025`; `656,000` PBISC cells across `143` COVID-19-positive, `19` COVID-19-negative, and `2` recovery samples). Generated cells were compared against the non-overlapping real scRNA-seq cohorts from the same studies (`108` adult samples and `633,072` cells in sepsis; `187` published samples and `578,835` cells in COVID-19) and against the published `scSemiProfiler` outputs (`34` samples and `51,000` cells in sepsis; `102` samples and `60,000` cells in COVID-19).

Across the `6,000`-gene generation panel (`5,967` shared genes in sepsis and `5,791` in COVID-19), PBISC preserved four orders of magnitude of gene-level mean UMI abundance relative to the independent real scRNA-seq cohorts (`log₁₀` mean UMI Pearson `r = 0.98` [`0.9757`] in sepsis and `r = 0.97` [`0.9656`] in COVID-19), whereas `scSemiProfiler` compressed low-to-intermediate expression (`r = 0.76` [`0.7602`] and `r = 0.74` [`0.7398`]; **Fig. 5b,c**) and collapsed per-cell library depth into a narrow spike rather than reproducing the broad empirical UMI distribution (`PBISC` median `2,121` vs `Real 2,042` panel UMIs per cell in sepsis; `PBISC 1,592` vs `Real 1,338` in COVID-19; **Supplementary Fig. 14e–h**). In shared `2,000`-HVG Harmony embeddings against a held-out half of the real cohort (`Real A`), PBISC achieved near-ceiling source mixing (`median iLISI = 1.57` vs `Real A/B` split-half ceiling `1.68` in sepsis; `1.60` vs `1.65` in COVID-19), whereas `scSemiProfiler` segregated into source-specific islands (`median iLISI = 1.00` in both cohorts; **Fig. 5d–g**). Decomposition of whole-PBMC pseudobulk disease shifts into compositional (`Δ share`) and within-lineage transcriptional (`Δ expression`) components confirmed that PBISC preserved both components across cohorts (`total r = 0.81` and `0.61`, vs `scSemiProfiler r = 0.45` and `0.34`; **Supplementary Fig. 14i,j**).

To account for repeated sampling and inter-individual heterogeneity across distinct donor groups, we summarized six major PBMC lineage proportions (`T`, `NK`, `B`, `Monocyte`, `DC`, and `Other`) per `patient × condition` unit and estimated case–control shifts via `49,999`-iteration patient-unit bootstrap resampling (**Fig. 5h,i**). PBISC recapitulated the direction of canonical disease remodelling across cohorts—including sepsis-associated monocyte (`+7.07` percentage points [`pp`], 95% confidence interval `[+3.74, +10.52]` vs `Real +7.78 pp [+0.36, +15.39]`) and B-cell (`+5.46 pp` vs `Real +7.97 pp`) expansion alongside T- and NK-cell contraction, as well as COVID-19 B-cell expansion (`+10.04 pp [+6.43, +13.44]` vs `Real +7.14 pp [+3.14, +11.29]`) and T/NK contraction (`−6.37 pp` and `−6.66 pp`). By contrast, `scSemiProfiler` inverted T-cell (`+15.73 pp`) and monocyte (`−4.23 pp`) shifts in sepsis and abolished B-cell expansion in COVID-19 (`−0.47 pp`).

We next evaluated literature-reported cell-type-specific transcriptional disease signatures on `patient × condition` `log₂(CPM + 1)` pseudobulk profiles (**Fig. 5j,k**). In sepsis (`Ye et al., 2026`), PBISC faithfully reproduced monocyte `S100/IL1R2` alarmin induction (`+0.90`, `BH q = 2.8 × 10⁻²⁰` vs `Real +2.18`), monocyte `MHC-II` antigen-presentation downregulation (`−0.74`, `q = 2.5 × 10⁻²⁶` vs `Real −1.46`), T-cell `NR4A/GPR183` activation (`+2.03`, `q = 2.7 × 10⁻³⁰` vs `Real +1.59`), B-cell `NR4A/CD69` activation (`+2.60`, `q = 1.9 × 10⁻³²` vs `Real +2.22`), and NK-cell `Stress/PRF1` remodelling (`+1.44`, `q = 9.0 × 10⁻³²` vs `Real +1.47`), whereas `scSemiProfiler` lost the NK-cell stress response (`+0.18`, `q = 0.55`). In COVID-19 (`Stephenson et al., 2025`), PBISC closely matched the core `7`-gene Type-I interferon (`7-ISG`: `ISG15`, `IFIT1`, `IFIT3`, `MX1`, `OAS1`, `OAS2`, `IFI6`) induction amplitude across monocytes (`+0.99` vs `Real +1.04`), T cells (`+1.31` vs `+1.50`), B cells (`+1.65` vs `+1.83`), and NK cells (`+1.37` vs `+1.69`; all `q ≤ 8.2 × 10⁻⁴`), whereas `scSemiProfiler` inflated monocyte `7-ISG` shifts fourfold (`+4.13`) while under-recovering B-cell `7-ISG` induction (`+0.76`).

Finally, we performed cell-type-specific differential expression testing across `5,664` shared genes in sepsis and `5,610` genes in COVID-19 using two-sided Welch's *t*-tests with significance defined at `BH q < 0.05` and `|log₂ fold change| ≥ 1` (**Fig. 5l–o** and **Supplementary Figs. 15 and 16**). Ranked against the independent real-cohort Welch DEG reference, PBISC achieved high discrimination across all four major lineages in both sepsis (`AUROC = 0.96` in T, `0.86` in B, `0.83` in NK, and `0.78` in monocytes; sign agreement `83.3–99.0%` on real DEGs; disattenuated effect-size `r = 0.66–0.81`; reliability-corrected slopes `0.71–1.11`) and COVID-19 (`AUROC = 0.99` in T, `0.87` in B, `0.85` in NK, and `0.88` in monocytes; sign agreement `93.5–100%`; disattenuated `r = 0.58–0.83`). By contrast, `scSemiProfiler` showed markedly degraded discrimination and calibration in sepsis (`AUROC = 0.56–0.65`, calling `0` NK-cell DEGs and achieving only `17.8%` sign concordance on real monocyte DEGs) and severe lineage distortion in COVID-19 (`AUROC = 0.68` in B cells with `0` overlapping DEGs, alongside inflated false-positive calls of `168–172` DEGs in T, NK, and monocyte compartments; **Fig. 5n,o** and **Supplementary Figs. 15 and 16**).

### Recovery of disease-specific rare cellular states and inter-patient transcriptional specificity

A central challenge for bulk-conditioned single-cell generation is recovering rare, clinically informative immune states representing `<1%` of peripheral leukocytes without collapsing them into majority lineages. Using fixed 30-nearest-neighbour (`30-NN`) label transfer from the `Real A` reference in the shared Harmony space, we resolved `14` prespecified rare cellular states in sepsis and `13` rare cellular states in COVID-19 and quantified each state's share within its parent lineage among disease cases, using total cells (`all cells`) as the denominator for `HSC` and `HSPC` (**Fig. 6a,b**). PBISC populated `13 / 14` rare states in adult sepsis cases and `12 / 13` rare states in COVID-19 cases—missing only ultra-sparse innate lymphoid cells (`ILC`, `0` cells in `Real B` sepsis cases and `1` cell [`0.014%`] in `Real B` COVID-19 cases)—while tracking parent-lineage fractions across three orders of magnitude, including `B_c07-MZB1` plasmablasts (`13.2%` of B + plasma cells vs `Real 3.0%`), `pDC` (`10.0%` vs `9.2%` of DCs in sepsis; `18.6%` vs `27.6%` in COVID-19), `HSC` (`0.16%` vs `0.034%` of all cells), `CD14mono_dex` (`0.20%` vs `0.33%` of CD14 monocytes), `B_ASC_IgG` (`5.1%` vs `6.1%` of plasmablasts), `B_ASC_dividing` (`26.7%` vs `34.1%` of plasmablasts), and `HSPC` (`0.82%` vs `0.19%` of all cells). Conversely, `scSemiProfiler` assigned `0` cells to `7 / 14` sepsis rare states (including all cycling lymphocyte/monocyte states and `pDC`) and `8 / 13` COVID-19 rare states (including `CD14mono_dex`, `B_ASC_IgG`, `B_ASC_dividing`, `T CD4 cycling`, `ASDC`, `DC1`, `HSPC`, and `ILC`; **Fig. 6a,b**).

Notably, in sepsis, `Mono_c06-CD14-MKI67` appeared substantially rarer in adult PBISC cases (`0.017%` of monocytes) than in the adult real scRNA-seq cohort (`0.23%`; **Fig. 6a,f**). Auditing all `82` unique donors in the full `Ye et al.` scRNA-seq dataset revealed a biological cohort-composition explanation (**Fig. 6c**): `MKI67⁺STMN1⁺` (and `MPO⁺ELANE⁺` granulocyte-precursor) proliferating monocytes are heavily enriched in paediatric abdominal (`up to 12.2%` of monocytes) and paediatric pulmonary sepsis, and within adults are concentrated in non-pulmonary sepsis (`Adult other`, `n = 28`), whereas the unpaired bulk-only cohort comprises predominantly adult surgical/ICU presentations with lower baseline myelocyte spillover. Across `10` focal disease-linked rare states evaluated at the `patient × condition` level (**Fig. 6d–g** and **Supplementary Fig. 17**), PBISC cells co-localized tightly with matched `Real A` rare states on Harmony UMAPs (**Fig. 6d,e**) and tracked case–control frequency trajectories across patient groups (`CD14mono_IFN` in COVID-19 cases vs controls, two-sided Mann–Whitney `Real vs PBISC BH q > 0.05`; **Fig. 6g**).

To test whether generated rare cells capture authentic state-specific transcriptional identity rather than generic lineage averages, we summed raw UMI counts across cells of the same rare state within each sample (`log₁ₚ(CP10k)`) and computed Pearson correlations against summed real cells of the same state from **different patients** (`patient_i ≠ patient_j`), benchmarking directly against the `Real–Real` inter-patient biological ceiling (**Fig. 6h,i**). Across `2,000` HVGs (global-mean-centred `Δ-centroid` specificity), PBISC achieved `r = 0.87–0.97` across `B_c07-MZB1` (`0.97`), `Macrophage` (`0.87`), `T_CD4_c07-MKI67` (`0.91`), `B_ASC_IgG` (`0.96`), `CD14mono_dex` (`0.90`), `CD14mono_IFN` (`0.94`), `pDC` (`0.96`), and `HSPC` (`0.92`), with near-zero or negative correlation to distinct rare states (`off-diagonal`). Crucially, at the individual sample level across different patients (`patient_i ≠ patient_j`), `Real–PBISC` summed-profile Pearson correlations closely approached or matched the `Real–Real` inter-patient ceiling in both sepsis (`B_c07-MZB1` median `r = 0.92` vs `Real–Real 0.91` [`2,625` pairs]; `Macrophage 0.85` vs `0.91`; `T_CD4_c07-MKI67 0.79` vs `0.81`; `T_CD8_c06-MKI67 0.81` vs `0.81`; single-cell-sparse `Mono_c06-CD14-MKI67 0.73` vs `0.88`) and COVID-19 (`B_ASC_IgG 0.88` vs `0.87` [`2,413` pairs]; `CD14mono_dex 0.81` vs `0.90`; `CD14mono_IFN 0.89` vs `0.91` [`8,003` pairs]; `pDC 0.86` vs `0.86` [`8,852` pairs]; `HSPC 0.86` vs `0.84` [`3,223` pairs]; **Fig. 6h,i**). Sample-level summed marker heatmaps across `32` `Mono_c06-CD14-MKI67` samples (`17 Real`, `15 PBISC`, `0 scSemiProfiler`), `219` `CD14mono_IFN` samples (`61 Real`, `132 PBISC`, `26 scSemiProfiler`), and six additional rare states (**Fig. 6j,k** and **Supplementary Fig. 18**) confirmed coherent co-expression of lineage and state-defining modules (`CD14/S100A8/S100A9/VCAN` with `MKI67/STMN1/TOP2A/TYMS`; `ISG15/IFIT1/IFIT3/IFI6/MX1`; `MZB1/JCHAIN/XBP1/IGHG1`; `FKBP5/TSC22D3/CD163`; `CD34/SPINK2/GATA2/AVP`).

### Cohort expansion via PBISC unlocks same-day severity and last-sample mortality correlates in haemodialysis COVID-19 with four-study external replication

In clinical cohorts, single-cell profiling is frequently restricted to a subset of enrolled patients and visits, underpowering survival and longitudinal severity analyses. In the Imperial College London end-stage kidney disease (`ESKD`) haemodialysis cohort (`Gisby et al., 2022; Stephenson et al., 2025`), excluding three patients with concurrent chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (`CLL`: `C46`, `C72`, `C136`) yielded `66` eligible acute COVID-19 patients (`55` survivors and `11` deceased) with `276` eligible acute-phase blood draws (`days_from_first_positive_swab` with documented same-day World Health Organization [`WHO`] ordinal severity: `Mild`, `Moderate`, `Severe`, or `Critical`; **Fig. 7a** and **Supplementary Figs. 19 and 20**). Real scRNA-seq covered only `37` of these `66` patients (`138` draws; `32` survivors and `5` deaths), whereas bulk-only blood draws salvaged by PBISC contributed an additional `138` non-overlapping acute draws (`0` same-day Real/PBISC duplicates)—comprising `79` draws from `29` previously unprofiled Wave-1 bulk-only patients (`23` survivors and `6` deaths) and `59` complementary longitudinal acute draws across `27` shared patients—doubling the sample size (`138 → 276` draws) and more than doubling fatal events (`5 → 11` deaths; **Fig. 7a** and **Supplementary Fig. 20**).

Following our prespecified clinical framework, we evaluated (i) **same-day severity** across all `276` acute draws by computing patient-equal-weighted Spearman rank correlations (`ρ`, assigning each draw weight `w_ij = 1 / n_i` so every patient has identical total weight `∑_j w_ij = 1.0`, total weight `66.0`, with patient-cluster-robust *P* values and bootstrap confidence intervals), and (ii) **mortality** at the patient level using each patient's single **last eligible acute blood draw** (`66` unique draws [`24 Real` + `42 PBISC`], `0` maximum-day ties; `55` survived vs `11` died; **Supplementary Fig. 20**) via two-sided Mann–Whitney `AUC`, applying Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment across all `43` fine-grained PBMC cell types (`BH43 q < 0.05`) independently for each endpoint (**Fig. 7b–d**).

Integrating PBISC-generated draws expanded `BH43 < 0.05` same-day WHO severity associations from `11 / 43` cell types in `Real` alone (`37` patients, `138` draws) to **`18 / 43`** cell types in the combined `66`-patient cohort using raw cell-type percentages (`% PBMC`), and **`20 / 43`** under within-source rank-based inverse-normal transformation (`rank-INT`; **Fig. 7b,c**). Expanding subsets with worsening same-day WHO severity included glucocorticoid-responsive `CD14mono_dex` (`weighted ρ = +0.393, BH43 q = 4.39 × 10⁻⁸`), circulating `HSPC` (`ρ = +0.393, q = 5.50 × 10⁻⁶`, rescued from `Real` alone `ρ = +0.102, q = 0.70`), `B_ASC_IgG` (`ρ = +0.382, q = 5.50 × 10⁻⁶`), `B_ASC_dividing` (`ρ = +0.368, q = 6.57 × 10⁻⁵`), `T CD4 cycling` (`ρ = +0.300, q = 7.07 × 10⁻⁴`), `B_ASC_IgA` (`ρ = +0.288, q = 4.74 × 10⁻³`), complement `CD16mono_C1` (`ρ = +0.213, q = 4.92 × 10⁻³`), `CD14mono_IFN` (`ρ = +0.253, q = 0.0120`), and `T CD8 CTL IFN stim` (`ρ = +0.200, q = 0.0443`), accompanied by coordinated contraction of `NK` (`ρ = −0.391, q = 5.50 × 10⁻⁶`), `T CD8 CM` (`ρ = −0.380, q = 5.50 × 10⁻⁶`), `NK CD56 bright` (`ρ = −0.348, q = 1.26 × 10⁻⁴`), `T CD8 CTL` (`ρ = −0.329, q = 3.91 × 10⁻⁴`), `CD16mono` (`ρ = −0.293, q = 7.09 × 10⁻⁴`), `T g/d` (`ρ = −0.293, q = 1.40 × 10⁻³`), `MAIT` (`ρ = −0.239, q = 0.0104`), `NKT` (`ρ = −0.245, q = 0.0126`), and `T CD4 CTL` (`ρ = −0.204, q = 0.0352`; **Fig. 7c–f**).

Most strikingly, in `Real` scRNA-seq alone (`32` survivors vs `5` deaths), **`0 / 43`** cell types survived `BH43 < 0.05` for last-draw mortality (`HSPC AUC = 0.731, P = 0.102, q = 0.644`; `T g/d AUC = 0.278, P = 0.120, q = 0.644`). Doubling the cohort with PBISC (`55` survivors vs `11` deaths) rescued **two** `BH43 < 0.05` predictors in raw `% PBMC`—profound **`T g/d` depletion** (`AUC = 0.183` [survival `AUC = 0.817`], `P = 9.90 × 10⁻⁴`, **`BH43 q = 0.0393`**) and pathological **`HSPC` expansion** (`AUC = 0.800`, `P = 1.83 × 10⁻³`, **`BH43 q = 0.0393`**; **Fig. 7b,c,g,h**)—and **three** `BH43 < 0.05` predictors under within-source `rank-INT`: `T g/d` (`AUC = 0.174, q = 0.0213`), `CD16mono_C1` (`AUC = 0.817, q = 0.0213`), and `HSPC` (`AUC = 0.782, q = 0.0484`), followed by `B_CD11c` (`raw % AUC = 0.226, P = 4.28 × 10⁻³, q = 0.0613`), `NK` (`AUC = 0.255, P = 0.0109`), `NK CD56 bright` (`AUC = 0.272, P = 0.0180`), `B_ASC_IgA` (`AUC = 0.715, P = 0.0258`), `B_ASC_dividing` (`AUC = 0.708, P = 0.0303`), and `T CD8 CTL` (`AUC = 0.319, P = 0.0607`; `rank-INT AUC = 0.244, P = 0.0078`). Notably, in the `29` newly unlocked bulk-only patients alone (`79` draws; `23` survivors vs `6` deaths), PBISC independently replicated both `HSPC` (`severity ρ = +0.658, q = 2.81 × 10⁻⁸`; last-draw `AUC = 0.833, P = 0.0142`) and `T g/d` (`severity ρ = −0.466, q = 1.60 × 10⁻⁴`; last-draw `AUC = 0.116, P = 4.38 × 10⁻³, q = 0.0333`) as well as `T CD8 CTL` (`severity ρ = −0.641, q = 7.09 × 10⁻⁷`; last-draw `AUC = 0.072, P = 1.63 × 10⁻³, q = 0.0310`; **Fig. 7d**).

Sensitivity analyses confirmed that `43`-cell-type effect sizes were invariant to replacing `% PBMC` with generated/observed cell counts (`r = 0.994` [`0.9945`] for same-day WHO `ρ` across `276` draws; `r = 0.991` [`0.9907`] for last-draw mortality `AUC` across `66` patients), aligned with hospital complete blood count (`CBC`) absolute `cells/L` (`r = 0.634` across `42` differential-CBC draws), and remained stable upon re-including the `3` `CLL` patients (`283` draws / `69` patients; **Supplementary Fig. 21a–d**). Benchmarked against `11` classical bulk deconvolution implementations run in official `R 4.3.2` (`BayesPrism`, `MuSiC`, `BisqueRNA`, `DWLS`, `CIBERSORTx`, `NNLS`, calibrated `NNLS`, and marker scores), PBISC recovered `10 / 10` concordant `FDR < 0.05` clinical signals versus `1–6 / 10` for deconvolution methods (**Supplementary Fig. 21e,f**). Moreover, unlike bulk deconvolution, PBISC retains single-cell gene programs within each generated subset (**Supplementary Fig. 22**): single-cell `T CD8 CTL` `7-ISG` scores tracked same-day WHO severity across `213` draws (`weighted ρ = +0.247, P = 0.00653`) and trended higher at each patient's **first** acute draw (`AUC = 0.679, P = 0.0631`) than at the **last** draw (`AUC = 0.658, P = 0.102`), consistent with early interferon induction followed by late interferon exhaustion prior to death (**Supplementary Fig. 22a,b**), while generated `HSPC` cells resolved an erythroid/alarmin/proliferative stress-haematopoiesis signature (`Ery5` module `severity ρ = +0.374, q₁₆ = 0.044; mortality AUC = 0.752, P = 0.0094`; `Alarmin_Inflam AUC = 0.712, P = 0.029`; `Cycle4 AUC = 0.706, P = 0.034`; driven by `TFRC` [`AUC = 0.782`], `GATA1` [`0.763`], `KLF1` [`0.726`], and `TYMS` [`0.726`]) rather than quiescent stemness (`CD34 AUC = 0.419`, `HLF = 0.614`, `AVP = 0.560`; **Supplementary Fig. 22c,d**).

Finally, to test whether these clinical associations generalize beyond haemodialysis patients, we analysed four independent external COVID-19 scRNA-seq cohorts (`Stephenson et al., 2021` [`89` acute draws / `85` donors]; `COMBAT, 2022` [`89` / `75`]; `van der Wijst et al., 2021` [`75` / `42`]; and `Liu et al., 2021` [`47` / `29`]; total **`300` acute samples across `231` donors**, including `197` survivors and `22` deaths among cohorts with fatal outcomes) under the **exact same criteria**—equal-patient-weight (`1 / n_i`) same-day WHO correlation and single last-acute-sample mortality `AUC`—and synthesized all cell types mapped in `k ≥ 2` studies (`23` exact `1:1` subsets plus total `B_ASC`) via DerSimonian–Laird random-effects meta-analysis (**Fig. 7i,j** and **Supplementary Fig. 23**). Random-effects meta-analysis independently validated `10` exact `1:1` subsets (plus `B_ASC_total`) at `BH q < 0.05` for same-day WHO severity with `100%` directional concordance with our ESKD cohort on shared signals, led by `HSPC` (`k = 3, ρ_RE = +0.405, q = 6.6 × 10⁻⁴`), `T g/d` (`k = 4, ρ_RE = −0.267, q = 6.6 × 10⁻⁴, I² = 0%`), `pDC` (`k = 4, ρ_RE = −0.326, q = 6.6 × 10⁻⁴`), `T CD8 CM` (`k = 2, ρ_RE = −0.287, q = 2.1 × 10⁻³, I² = 0%`), `CD14mono` (`k = 4, ρ_RE = +0.257, q = 2.1 × 10⁻³`), `MAIT` (`k = 4, ρ_RE = −0.294, q = 3.9 × 10⁻³`), `T CD4 Helper` (`k = 4, ρ_RE = −0.197, q = 0.013, I² = 0%`), `B_ASC_dividing` (`k = 2, ρ_RE = +0.259, q = 0.013, I² = 0%`; `B_ASC_total k = 4, ρ_RE = +0.279, q = 0.012`), `NK CD56 bright` (`k = 4, ρ_RE = −0.280, q = 0.016`), and `DC2` (`k = 2, ρ_RE = −0.328, q = 0.028`; **Fig. 7i**). For last-draw mortality across the `22` external deaths (**Fig. 7j** and **Supplementary Fig. 23b**), random-effects synthesis confirmed non-classical `CD16mono` loss (`k = 4, AUC_RE = 0.320, P = 8.50 × 10⁻⁴, BH q = 0.020, I² = 1.5%`), unconventional T-cell depletion in **`T g/d`** (`k = 4, AUC_RE = 0.255, P = 0.0404`) and **`MAIT`** (`k = 4, AUC_RE = 0.270, P = 0.0447`), `T CD8 CM` contraction (`k = 2, AUC_RE = 0.363, P = 0.0547, I² = 0%`), and directionally concordant shifts in `NK CD56 bright` (`0.394`), `T CD8 CTL` (`0.423`), `HSPC` (`k = 3, AUC_RE = 0.599, I² = 0%`), and `B_ASC_dividing` (`k = 2, AUC_RE = 0.615, I² = 0%`).

## Discussion (Figures 5–7 Section)

Translational single-cell studies of acute critical illness face a persistent bottleneck: whereas biobanked whole-blood or PBMC bulk RNA-seq can be collected longitudinally across entire hospital cohorts, experimental scRNA-seq is typically restricted by cost, cell viability, and processing logistics to a fraction of enrolled patients and time points. Across bacterial sepsis (`Ye et al., 2026`) and haemodialysis COVID-19 (`Gisby et al., 2022; Stephenson et al., 2025`), our findings demonstrate that adapting PBISC on only five paired donors per cohort transforms hundreds of unpaired bulk-only clinical blood draws (`122` sepsis and `164` COVID-19 samples; `1.14 million` generated cells) into analysis-ready single-cell repertoires that integrate seamlessly with independent real scRNA-seq cohorts (`iLISI = 1.57–1.60`). Crucially, when evaluated under strict patient-level summarization and resampling (`Fig. 5`), PBISC simultaneously preserves compositional case–control shifts, literature-validated myeloid and interferon gene programs, and lineage-specific Welch differential expression calibration—overcoming the library-depth collapse, lineage-shift inversions, and interferon variance distortion observed with representative-selection semi-profiling (`scSemiProfiler`).

Beyond major lineages, clinical biomarker discovery often hinges on rare peripheral subsets below `1%` frequency—such as circulating haematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (`HSC`/`HSPC`), antibody-secreting plasmablast subclasses (`B_c07-MZB1`, `B_ASC_IgG`, `B_ASC_dividing`), glucocorticoid-remodelled monocytes (`CD14mono_dex`), and cycling lymphocytes—that were completely absent (`0` cells) in more than half of rare compartments generated by `scSemiProfiler` (`Fig. 6`). By conditioning single-cell generation directly on patient-specific bulk transcriptomes within a continuous generative manifold, PBISC populated `13 / 14` sepsis and `12 / 13` COVID-19 rare states and achieved sample-level summed transcriptional correlations across different patients (`patient_i ≠ patient_j`) that matched or approached the biological `Real–Real` inter-patient ceiling (`median r = 0.79–0.92` across plasmablasts, macrophages, `pDC`, `CD14mono_IFN`, `CD14mono_dex`, cycling T cells, and `HSPC`). Even where apparent cross-cohort frequency differences arose—such as `Mono_c06-CD14-MKI67` in sepsis—donor-level auditing traced the difference to genuine clinical composition (`paediatric abdominal/pulmonary` vs `adult surgical/ICU` sepsis; `Fig. 6c`), underscoring that PBISC avoids hallucinating disease subsets into bulk samples lacking their underlying transcriptional signal.

The clinical utility of unlocking unpaired bulk-only patients is most clearly illustrated in our longitudinal haemodialysis COVID-19 analysis (`Fig. 7`). Separating time-varying acute disease burden (**same-day WHO severity** across all `276` acute blood draws with equal total weight `1.0` per patient) from terminal outcome (**mortality** at each patient's single **last eligible acute draw** across `66` patients) resolved a key limitation of underpowered scRNA-seq cohorts: in the `37` patients with real scRNA-seq (`5` deaths), `0 / 43` immune subsets survived multiple-testing correction (`BH43 q < 0.05`) for mortality, whereas incorporating `138` non-overlapping PBISC acute draws (`29` new Wave-1 patients plus `59` longitudinal visits, `11` total deaths) expanded `BH43`-significant severity correlates from `11` to `18` (`20` under `rank-INT`) and rescued two `BH43`-significant terminal mortality predictors—pathological `HSPC` mobilization (`AUC = 0.800, q = 0.0393`) and profound `T g/d` depletion (`AUC = 0.183, q = 0.0393`)—alongside `CD16mono_C1` expansion (`rank-INT AUC = 0.817, q = 0.0213`) and cytotoxic `T CD8 CTL` / `NK` attrition. Furthermore, retaining single-cell resolution within generated cells enabled mechanistic dissection unattainable by classical bulk deconvolution (`Supplementary Figs. 21 and 22`): generated `HSPCs` exhibited an erythroid-megakaryocytic, alarmin, and proliferative emergency-myelopoiesis/stress-erythropoiesis program (`GATA1`, `KLF1`, `TFRC`, `TYMS`), while `T CD8 CTL` `7-ISG` expression tracked concurrent WHO severity and early first-draw elevation (`AUC = 0.679`) before attenuating at the last pre-terminal draw (`AUC = 0.658`). Replication across `300` acute blood draws from `231` donors in four external COVID-19 scRNA-seq cohorts under the exact same analytical criteria (`Fig. 7i,j` and `Supplementary Fig. 23`) confirms that emergency `HSPC` mobilization, plasmablast expansion, non-classical `CD16mono` loss, and unconventional (`T g/d`, `MAIT`) plus cytotoxic T/NK attrition represent generalizable hallmarks of severe and fatal COVID-19. More broadly, pairing small `5`-donor scRNA-seq anchor subsets with PBISC offers a scalable blueprint for converting archival clinical bulk RNA-seq biobanks into cohort-scale, longitudinal single-cell studies powered for clinical endpoints.

## Methods (Figures 4–7)

### Figure 4 · Paired same-donor single-cell generation and `limma-voom` differential expression benchmark
In both the bacterial sepsis (`Ye et al., 2026`) and haemodialysis COVID-19 (`Gisby et al., 2022; Stephenson et al., 2025`) cohorts, the pretrained PBISC foundation model (`6,000`-gene human PBMC panel) was adapted using `5` paired donors per cohort who had matched bulk RNA-seq and real scRNA-seq profiles, holding out all remaining paired donors strictly for evaluation (`0` donor leakage between adaptation and evaluation; Supplementary Figs. 8 and 9). From each held-out evaluation donor's bulk RNA-seq transcriptome, PBISC generated `K = 4,000` single cells, which were compared directly against the experimentally measured (`Real`) single cells from that **same donor** in terms of gene-mean expression, per-cell UMI depth, Harmony manifold co-embedding, and cell-type composition. Across three prespecified reference-patient combinations, disease-associated differential gene expression (`case vs control`) within the four major immune lineages (`T cells`, `B cells`, `Monocytes`, and `NK cells`) was estimated on sample-level pseudobulk count matrices using `limma-voom` (`TMM` normalization, empirical Bayes moderated *t*-statistics; Supplementary Figs. 10–13). Differentially expressed genes (`DEGs`) were defined using a Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate threshold of `BH q < 0.05`.

### Figure 5 · Cross-cohort comparison between unpaired bulk-only cohorts and separate real scRNA-seq cohorts
Using the `5`-donor-adapted cohort models without any exposure to target donors, we generated `K = 4,000` single cells per sample across `122` unpaired bulk-only whole-blood samples in the sepsis cohort (`488,000` cells: `68` adult sepsis, `17` adult ICU, `36` adult HC, and `1` paediatric ICU sample `PIC06`) and `164` unpaired bulk-only peripheral blood samples in the COVID-19 cohort (`656,000` cells: `143` COVID-19-positive, `19` COVID-19-negative, and `2` recovery samples), and compared them against separate patient cohorts profiled by real scRNA-seq (`108` adult sepsis-study samples, `633,072` cells; `187` COVID-19-study samples, `578,835` cells; `0` donor overlap in sepsis and unpaired cohort-level comparison in COVID-19) as well as published `scSemiProfiler` outputs (`34` sepsis samples, `51,000` cells; `102` COVID-19 samples, `60,000` cells; Supplementary Fig. 14). For manifold integration and cell-type transfer, real cohorts were split at the donor level into non-overlapping `Real A` (reference) and `Real B` (held-out evaluation) halves, embedded jointly with `PBISC` or `scSemiProfiler` across `2,000` shared highly variable genes (`HVGs`) via `PCA` (`30` PCs) and `Harmony`, and quantified by source `iLISI`.

To respect repeated longitudinal draws and patient-level independence, six-lineage cell proportions (`T cell`, `NK cell`, `B cell`, `Monocyte`, `DC`, `Other`) were first averaged within each **`patient × condition`** unit (`Sepsis`: `30` case vs `5` control patients in `Real`, `68` vs `36` in `PBISC`, `26` vs `5` in `scSemiProfiler`; `COVID-19`: `22` COVID-19-positive vs `21` negative patients in `Real`, `55` vs `18` in `PBISC`, `22` vs `22` in `scSemiProfiler`). Uncertainty and case–control shifts (`percentage points, pp`) were estimated via non-parametric resampling at the **patient unit** (`49,999` bootstrap iterations, seeded at `20261122`). Literature-reported disease transcriptional signals were evaluated by computing cell-type-specific gene module scores on `patient × condition` `log₂(CPM + 1)` pseudobulk profiles: in sepsis, the five canonical `Ye et al. (2026)` programs—monocyte `S100/IL1R2` (`IL1R2, MCEMP1, AQP9, S100A8, S100A9, RETN`), monocyte `MHC-II` (`HLA-DRA, HLA-DRB1, HLA-DPA1, CD74, CIITA`), T-cell `NR4A/GPR183` (`GPR183, NR4A1, NR4A2, CD69, SOCS3`), B-cell `NR4A/CD69` (`CD69, NR4A1, NR4A2, SOCS3, CD83`), and NK-cell `Stress/PRF1` (`NR4A1, SOCS3, PRF1, GNLY, CCL4`); and in COVID-19, the `Stephenson et al. (2025)` core `7`-gene Type-I interferon signature (`ISG15, IFIT1, IFIT3, MX1, OAS1, OAS2, IFI6`) across monocytes, T cells, B cells, and NK cells. Cell-type-specific differential expression (`case vs control`) in `T`, `B`, `NK`, and `Monocyte` lineages (`≥ 5` cells per sample required) was tested across shared panel genes (`5,664` in sepsis; `5,610` in COVID-19) using two-sided **Welch's *t*-tests** on `log₂(CPM + 1)` pseudobulk expression, selecting DEGs satisfying both **`BH q < 0.05`** and **`|log₂ fold change| ≥ 1`** (Supplementary Figs. 15 and 16). Split-half reliability across `50` random donor splits was used to compute disattenuated effect-size correlations and reliability-corrected calibration slopes.

### Figure 6 · Rare cell state frequency and inter-patient summed expression analysis
Using fixed `30-NN` majority-vote cell annotations transferred from the `Real A` reference in the `30`-dimensional Harmony space, we resolved **`14` rare cellular states in sepsis** (`Mono_c06-CD14-MKI67`, `B_c08-MKI67`, `T_CD4_c07-MKI67`, `T_CD8_c06-MKI67`, `NK_c04-MKI67`, `B_c07-MZB1`, `pDC`, `cDC`, `Macrophage`, `gdT`, `ILC`, `HSC`, `Mono_c01-CD14-HLA-DRA`, `NKT_c05-IFNG-CCL4hi`) and **`13` rare cellular states in COVID-19** (`CD14mono_dex`, `CD14mono_IFN`, `B_ASC_IgG`, `B_ASC_dividing`, `T CD4 cycling`, `ASDC`, `DC1`, `pDC`, `HSPC`, `ILC`, `T g/d`, `MAIT`, `NK CD56 bright`). Within disease cases, each rare state's relative frequency was computed as its percentage of its **parent cell lineage** (`Monocytes`, `CD14 mono`, `Mono + macrophage`, `B + plasma`, `plasmablasts`, `CD4 T`, `CD8 T`, `all T`, `NK`, `NK + ILC`, `NKT`, or `DC`), whereas **total cell count (`all cells`)** was used as the denominator for **`HSC`** (sepsis) and **`HSPC`** (COVID-19). Patient-level rare-state percentages were summarized per `patient × condition` unit with `49,999`-resample patient-unit bootstrap confidence intervals and compared against `Real` via two-sided Mann–Whitney *U*-tests (`BH`-adjusted; Supplementary Fig. 17).

For rare-state transcriptional fidelity, raw UMI counts of all cells assigned to the same rare state within each sample were **summed** and normalized to `log₁ₚ(CP10k)` (`counts per 10,000 + 1`). For every rare state, each `PBISC` sample's summed profile was compared via **Pearson correlation (`r`)** against the summed `Real` profiles of the **same rare state in other patients (`patient_i ≠ patient_j`)**, strictly excluding any same-donor pairs, and benchmarked against the empirical `Real–Real` inter-patient (`patient_i ≠ patient_j`) correlation distribution as well as cross-state (`Other state`) pairs. State specificity across `2,000` shared HVGs was additionally quantified via global-mean-centred (`Δ-centroid`) Pearson correlation, and canonical lineage/state marker expression was compared across individual summed samples via row-standardized (`Z-score`) `log₁ₚ(CP10k)` heatmaps (Supplementary Fig. 18).

### Figure 7 · Same-day WHO severity and last-sample mortality analysis with four-study external meta-analysis
Clinical association analyses in the Imperial College London `ESKD` haemodialysis cohort were restricted to non-CLL patients with acute COVID-19 blood draws (`positive` status with non-missing `days_from_first_positive_swab` and same-day WHO ordinal grade: `0 = Mild`, `1 = Moderate`, `2 = Severe`, `3 = Critical`). Excluding `3` patients with concurrent `CLL` (`C46`, `C72`, `C136`; evaluated separately in sensitivity analysis) yielded **`66` haemodialysis patients** (`55` survivors and `11` fatal cases verified in the published clinical metadata) contributing **`276` eligible acute-phase samples** (`138 Real` across `37` patients and `138 PBISC` across `56` patients [`79` samples in `29` Wave-1 bulk-only patients + `59` non-overlapping acute visits in `27` Shared-27 patients]; `0` same-day Real/PBISC duplicates; Supplementary Figs. 19 and 20). Cell-type proportions (`% of total PBMC`) were quantified across all **`43` fine-grained immune cell types** via fixed `30-NN` Harmony annotation transfer from the `187`-sample published COVID-19 scRNA-seq reference.

1. **Same-day WHO severity (`276` acute samples, `66` patients)**: Associations between each cell type's proportion (`% PBMC`) and concurrent **blood-draw-day WHO severity grade** were quantified across all `276` acute draws using weighted-midrank Spearman correlation (`ρ_w`), assigning each sample `j` of patient `i` (with `n_i` eligible acute draws) an **equal-patient total weight** `w_ij = 1 / n_i` so that every patient contributed an identical total weight of `1.0` (`∑_{i,j} w_ij = 66.0`), thereby accounting for repeated blood draws (`1–8` draws per patient). Patient-clustered standard errors and two-sided *P* values were obtained via weighted least squares (`WLS`) with patient-cluster-robust (`CR1`) covariance on standardized weighted midranks, and two-sided 95% confidence intervals were obtained by `2,000`-iteration **patient-cluster bootstrap** resampling of the `66` patients.
2. **Last-sample mortality (`66` patients: `55` survived vs `11` died)**: Mortality discrimination was evaluated at the patient level using each patient's **single last eligible acute-phase sample** (`maximum days_from_first_positive_swab`; `0` same-day ties across all `66` patients, yielding `24 Real` and `42 PBISC` last samples) by comparing cell-type proportions between survivors (`n = 55`) and deceased patients (`n = 11`) via the non-parametric Mann–Whitney **area under the ROC curve (`AUC`)**, with exact/asymptotic two-sided Mann–Whitney *P* values and `2,000`-iteration stratified bootstrap 95% confidence intervals.
3. **Multiple-testing correction & sensitivity analyses**: For both same-day WHO severity and last-sample mortality, Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate correction was applied independently across all **`43` cell types (`BH q < 0.05`)**. Pre-planned sensitivity analyses repeated the identical `276`-sample / `66`-patient framework using within-source rank-based inverse-normal transformation (`Blom rank-INT`), unnormalized per-sample cell counts, hospital differential `CBC` absolute leukocyte counts (`cells/L` across `42` matched draws), inclusion of the `3` `CLL` patients (`283` draws / `69` patients), `11` classical bulk deconvolution methods executed in official `R 4.3.2` (Supplementary Fig. 21), and single-cell transcriptional scoring (`T-cell 7-ISG` across `13` T-cell subsets at same-day, first-draw, and last-draw windows, plus `16` functional modules and `20` canonical genes within `HSPC` across the `62` `HSPC⁺` patients; Supplementary Fig. 22).
4. **External four-study replication and random-effects meta-analysis**: Four independent public COVID-19 PBMC scRNA-seq cohorts—`Stephenson et al., Nat. Med. 2021` (`89` acute samples / `85` donors; `76` survived, `8` died, `1` unknown outcome), `COMBAT Consortium, Cell 2022` (`89` acute samples / `75` donors; `58` survived, `6` died, `11` unknown), `van der Wijst et al., Sci. Transl. Med. 2021` (`75` acute samples / `42` donors; `27` survived, `4` died, `11` unknown), and `Liu et al., Cell 2021` (`47` acute samples / `29` donors; `25` survived, `4` died)—totalling **`300` acute samples across `231` donors** (`197` survivors and `22` deceased across cohorts with mortality follow-up), were harmonised to the `43` ESKD fine cell types (`23` exact `1:1` subsets mapped in `k ≥ 2` cohorts plus composite `B_ASC_total`). Each external study was analysed independently using the **exact same criteria**—equal-patient-weight (`w_ij = 1 / n_i`) same-day WHO Spearman correlation (`patient-cluster-robust / bootstrap SE` on `Fisher z`) and single last-eligible-sample mortality `AUC` (`Hanley–McNeil / bootstrap SE` on `AUC`)—and combined across all cell types present in **`k ≥ 2` studies** via **DerSimonian–Laird random-effects meta-analysis** (`τ²`, `I²`, two-sided random-effects `P`, and `BH q`; Supplementary Fig. 23).
